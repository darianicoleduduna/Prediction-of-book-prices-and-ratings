i abused my eyes, brain, heart, and soul.

this book has gusted my flabbers, but not in a good way. for the last few days, i’ve been continuously thinking about what to write, but trust me, my mind is completely blank (not blissfully, either). this mind-numbingly bad book has caused some serious tissue damage inside my brain. at this point, i’m continuing for two reasons. first, it’s written by meatbun. i’m forever grateful to her for erha and yuwu, so i’ll read everything written by her. second, i can’t leave things unfinished. if i read volume one, i’ll read volume ten. it’s definitely a psychological problem. for that, i can’t blame anyone but myself. so, with a heavy heart, i’m sharing my thoughts.

before i start, i’d better clarify a few things. this review is entirely subjective. it’s a trash book for me, and i don’t see any reason to sugarcoat it. my purpose is not to misinfluence anyone. we’re all different people with different perspectives, so read it and form your own opinions. also, i don’t think i need to use a spoiler warning, the cover is enough for that.

if i had to express all my feelings in a single sentence, i’d say this book is very convenient, just for the sake of convenience. everything happened, but at the same time, nothing of substance actually happened.

lately, whenever i open instagram, those ai-generated dramas pop up, filled with one obvious misunderstanding after another, where the maniac villains are always laughing and screaming for no apparent reason. this snoozefest in the form of a book somewhat felt like that.

again, our star, the best character of this entire series, dear pegasus, enters the chat, and this time all three people, xie qingcheng, he yu, and chen man are riding it. please have mercy on the poor beast. (i’m still bitter that it’s not an original pegasus.) they’re on their way to destroy the laser. it’s interesting to see how three people momentarily fooled the most futuristic organisation.

now, for zoya, the girl with the tragic backstory. do i really need to feel bad for her? let’s just say that, in the end, she became the monster she had always hated. the frost laser destruction fight scene is totally unbelievable. it’s a serious situation, but zoya faked being under the influence of he yu’s blood toxin just to have her “ha ha ha” scene. during a life-and-death situation, who has time to do that? never beating the cartoon villain allegations, i see. and instead of firing bullets or tranquilizer darts, those modified humans were fighting with daggers?? and our superhuman team of three was countering those things? i’m not reading mortal instruments. pack it up.

bingo, it’s chapter 235, and we suddenly have a new character. li yun’s introduction was just to give some shallow depth to duan wen’s character. i see no other reason. duan wen, the inhuman villain who was groomed since childhood, turned into a calculating, unfeeling monster who solves everything with logic and if i remember correctly, once said he had no feelings for men, he suddenly turned gay? idk why, but i feel like even the author initially had no idea the story would take such turns. and the final moments between these two? the regret over what it could have been and how it turned out felt very superficial to me. personally, i felt nothing. (just to add, i somewhat like the scenes where their academy days were described.)

anthony...oh, bitch. a very disgusting character. he doesn’t know what he wants. either he wants to be xqc or wants to be with xqc. (kidding. yikes, that was gross.) well, in the end, he knew that he wanted to be in xqc’s place and steal everything from him, but his motivation for that and his backstory feel very flimsy. like, bro, the truth was right in front of you, but you were just blind. anyways, that’s just another cartoon villain.

now, the main villain: the little boy on red heels, who is basically a fake god chasing immortality. (little bro should just transmigrate and cultivate; it’ll save a lot of hassle.) she actually turns out to be duan wen’s great-grandmother, the mother of the entire mandela island, with a chip of utmost importance inside her head. with her death, the entire power supply will be cut off and the island will sink. dude, i was having a hard time reading it. old hag is a human, not a nuclear power reactor. the plot is seriously laughable. now to her backstory. she turned evil and did all of that just because she was obsessed with a man and later got used by him. girl, get off the floor and have some self-respect. obsessing over a man, killing his grandson, and becoming doctor moreau isn’t worth it.

oh, and the age gap between duan wen and evil great-grandma just doesn’t add up. again, if i remember correctly, duan wen is in his forties, and mandela started like 70 years ago? i may be wrong, idk. correct me.

another thing that really pissed me off is the incessant mention of the tattered teddy bear in front of a ferris wheel and the little charmander. i get that those things have some symbolism in the story, but for the sake of god, just stop milking it. i feel like i’ve been reading the same description for the last five volumes. (even my goat charizard will get pissed, or maybe it’ll fuel his never-ending ego. idk.)

another thing that doesn’t add up is xqc getting pregnant with the power of blood toxin. here, i’m pasting the paragraph. read it yourself.

blood toxin…
that was right… after their first sexual encounter on mandela island, when he was trapped in he yu’s room, he had experienced fever, vomiting, and a number of other obvious physical reactions. these symptoms didn’t resemble the side effects of case no. 2’s blood serum, nor were they typical of a normal inflammatory response.
he hadn’t understood what was wrong with him back then, but now that he was thinking about it, the blood serum formulated by meiyu private hospital contained enzymes to facilitate his absorption of other psychological ebola abilities.
in this state, he’d gone and had intense, unprotected sex with psychological ebola case no. 4, he yu…
and thus his body had been forced to accept case no. 4’s bodily fluids.
of course, the enzymes in the blood serum wouldn’t and couldn’t distinguish between cases no. 2 and no. 4. any special abilities would have been assimilated and adapted. furthermore, he yu’s blood toxin was the most aggressive and powerful of all the known comorbid abilities of psychological ebola. when it entered xie qingcheng’s body, it had triggered an intense discomfort from the first emperor as the enzymes worked to break it down. like a violent male beast attempting to subdue the most powerful female beast, forcing a twisted union through an aggressive and destructive process.
the fever and vomiting had likely been symptoms of he yu’s blood toxin interacting with the first emperor’s constitution. but in a surprising turn, this blood toxin’s overbearing effects did not fade as easily as the gentle ones of case no. 2.
he yu’s cells had penetrated the first emperor’s cells and buried themselves deeply within, knotting and proliferating; forcing the first emperor’s cells to hold them, to remember them, to accept them.
now engraved, they refused to fade.
sitting in his cell in total shock, xie qingcheng suddenly understood what he was now capable of.


again, utterly hilarious and convenient for the sake of the plot, with no real logic behind it. my bro literally became alien x from ben 10 with omnipotence. i’m seriously so, so, so done.

finally, xqc becoming the sacrificial lamb and the final battle scene. that is seriously the worst part of the book. the most bogus fight ever. the sudden appearance of the militia, the power of blood toxin's command being able to breach the protection of the purification ring, and dora the explorer consciousness being able to travel from one body to another is just ridiculous.

from the moment he yu attacked and everything that happened afterwards, both xqc and grandpa cop had ages to do or think of something else, but no, xqc had to die. maybe i’m an unfeeling monster, but it didn’t affect me that much, knowing he’ll get resurrected like frankenstein’s monster in the next volume. and the final brief encounter with vivian...at this point, i’m just grateful that the author didn’t magically resurrect her too.

and the final nail in the coffin is that fuckass letter that hy received after xqc’s death. even though it was emotional to a certain degree, stop romanticising that shit, seriously. why did xie qingcheng keep apologising and saying how good he yu was to him? are we collectively forgetting what happened throughout the entire story? i get it, they both went through trials and tribulations, and their dynamic changed significantly, but still, all this “i owe you” or “you owe me” type of thing sounds like cacophony to my ears. after everything that happened, the best possible ending could’ve been with both of them dead. not every book deserves a happy ending.

for some time, i actually enjoyed certain aspects of this story, but after volume five, things deteriorated way too fast. i can appreciate certain prose, but the angst is so forced and the plot is garbage. instead of becoming overwhelming, it turned underwhelming. ngl, this story went in an entirely different direction than i expected, which ultimately didn’t work for me.

(some kids get offended at everything and take everything personally, so i’m mentioning it again: everything written here is my personal opinion. i don’t want anyone’s validation or hatred. i know i’m apparently in the minority here, everyone seems to love this book, so i am the problem. and i take the full blame.)

as a living embodiment of psyduck with a chronic migraine, i really can’t think of anything else right now. this is not the articulated review i wanted to write, and i say this after every single review because that’s just how it is. i have the nagging feeling that i’m missing something, so if i feel like it, i’ll add more paragraphs later.

“The truth is, people are limited by their own perspectives. You fight for what you believe to be justice, yet what seems right to you may be wrong in the eyes of another.”



As excited as I was to start this volume, I was also kind of reluctant because just looking at the cover, I already knew I was going to be heartbroken in one way or another. And yep, I was right. I was only a few chapters in and already bawling my eyes out. Then I got to chapter 236 and started thinking I was onto something with Duan Wen. I kept thinking, “Wait, I feel like this is someone we already know.” And omg! I was right. I hate that I was right. There was a point where I was just completely devastated, but I kept holding on to that tiny bit of hope because everyone keeps saying this series has a happy ending. But where is it!? It hurts! And then it said “the end” when it wasn’t actually the end. Ugh!! I immediately started looking for an FTL because there was no way I was going to sleep like that. I needed to know what happened next.

My biggest problem with this volume is honestly just how many twists and revelations were thrown at me in such a short amount of time. Two or three plot twists? Fine, I can handle that. But there were too many. Duan Wen’s other persona, Li Yun, the history behind his persona, Anthony, the whole situation with the other dead people, Vivian… there was a point where I was just like, okay, what else?! Some of them worked really well for me, and I actually liked how certain things connected back to mysteries that had been sitting there for so long. But some of the revelations felt way too convenient, especially when several of them were happening one right after another. It started making some of the emotional moments feel less effective because instead of sitting with one revelation, I was already being hit with another one. And there is too much suffering in this volume. Xie Qingcheng goes through one horrible thing after another, and while some of those scenes absolutely destroyed me, there were also moments where I was starting to feel a little numb from it. I cried, but I also facepalmed. Sometimes I was emotionally wrecked, and sometimes I was just staring at the page thinking, “Girl, are you okay?”

I have to be really honest, though. I love Meatbun. I love her so much. She really knows how to weave a story together, and when all those little pieces finally connect, it can be so satisfying. I just feel like I’m starting to notice a pattern with the series. First there was the non-con and misunderstanding, then the one step forward, two steps back situation, and now there’s the “someone dies and leaves a letter behind to explain everything” thing. Once is fine because it can have a huge emotional impact and actually add something to the story. But when it happens more than once, it starts to feel like a repeated device instead of something that hits naturally. I still think the way Meatbun has woven this whole story together is stunning, and there were plenty of moments in this volume where I was completely invested. I just wish some of the twists had been given more room to breathe instead of being stacked on top of each other. But honestly, after that ending, I keep on thinking about what twist is gonna happen again. I needed the FTL. I needed sleep. I needed the happy ending. I needed something because I was definitely not okay.

This was quite a disappointing read. After the action-packed previous volume, this one felt like a bit of a letdown, even though it included the final battle and the main villain's defeat. The final battle itself was rather anticlimactic. Not that it was boring, but it almost felt like reading a step-by-step depiction of it, which was not suspenseful in the least. The main villain acting like a parody of a villain didn't help either.


I liked the parts about Duan Wen and Li Yun. Their story is awfully tragic, especially knowing that if not for the grandmother's toxic upbringing and values, Duan Wen could've grown up to be a decent human being, and he could've had his happy ending with Li Yun. Despite being one of the bad guys, Duan Wen was still somewhat better than the rest; he was fair in some regards, and even he despised the likes of Anthony. But sadly, he only realized his love for Li Yun too late. I found his and Li Yun's end far more tragic and sad than what happened to Xie Qingcheng. For me, Xie Qingcheng's end and the chapters showing He Yu coping with it felt more melodramatic than sad, and far too depressing to enjoy reading. In fact, the last few chapters were so depressing that I just wanted to read it as quickly as I could so I could finally end it. He Yu's reaction to Xie Qingcheng's death was on point and totally believable, though. What I didn't like was all the melodrama that preceded it because it made Xie Qingcheng's death more melodramatic than tragic, which is a tragedy in itself.


I liked that in his letter, Xie Qingcheng finally admitted a lot of things both to He Yu and to himself as well that he previously outright denied, even to himself. I liked that he faced many things he previously didn't want to face or didn't even bother to think about, and he reconsidered his previous viewpoint regarding certain matters and realized and admitted that he was wrong for thinking those things or thinking that way. It's very much like Xie Qingcheng, but I still hate how much he says over and over again that he owes He Yu, and that He Yu is right to be angry at him and resent him, while completely ignoring all the things He Yu had actually done to him as if they didn't happen. Honestly, He Yu owes him just as much as he owes He Yu. But at least both of them matured a lot by the end of this volume. Xie Qingcheng grew to understand himself better and became better at expressing himself and his feelings, while He Yu became more selfless, wanting nothing more than for Xie Qingcheng to survive, to be alive even if he won't love him. But I liked how Xie Qingcheng finally told Anthony his opinion of him and made him face his actions and realize his true feelings. Still, it came far too late. It could have prevented many bad things from happening if they had cleared up the misunderstandings earlier.


Overall, the story was interesting, but sadly not as good as the previous volume. Bonus points for being the only volume so far without a single detailed, multiple-page-long sex scene. There was a short implied one between side characters, but that's it. And I honestly don't mind it.
