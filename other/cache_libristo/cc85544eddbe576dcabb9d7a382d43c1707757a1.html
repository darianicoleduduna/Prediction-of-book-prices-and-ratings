Wendy Heiss

A witch.

A vampire.

And a five-hundred-year-old pact between them.

Every summer solstice, a witch must marry a vampire. And in return, a vampire must die as a sacrifice to keep an ancient curse from killing the witches. A pact that was meant to keep peace between Meridian and Mortdale.

When word is sent that the witches of Meridian seem to keep dying soon after they are wed to a vampire, Willow leaves her position as an occult pathologist to marry Lord Logan Duncan and investigate the deaths of her coven sisters.

Working right under the King of Mortdale as his second-in-command, Logan is forced to keep a close eye on his new wife and figure out what mission she was assigned in Mortdale while also struggling to face a plague of madness that is forcing his species near extinction.

About the author

Wendy Heiss debuted with a political dark fantasy romance, Winter Gods & Serpents, the first book in The Auran Chronicles series. Since then, she has written in various subgenres, from romantic tragedies to gothic horror-comedy. She has graduated with a Forensics Science degree, but literature has been one of her passions since she could manage to read and write. And despite being severely tempted to ride the Agatha Christie route to crime novels, she chose to follow the Tolkien path to fantasy. She’ll choose fairy dust over fingerprint powder any day. When she’s not writing, reading, or dissociating, she’s stress-baking, being dragged around on walks by her half-feral cat who thinks she’s a dog, and probably having her next existential crisis.

ㅤ ‍‍‍‍‍‍ ‍‍‍‍‍‍ ⋅ ☾ I am such a sucker for anything set in a gothic world, I genuinely cannot help it, and this is exactly the kind of read that feels perfect for the season. I absolutely loved what the author did with the setting of this book. It was vivid, dark, slightly eerie, and somehow even a little cozy, while still being peculiar in the best way possible. The atmosphere was honestly one of my favorite parts of the entire book.

ㅤ ‍‍‍‍‍‍ ‍‍‍‍‍‍ ⋅ ☾ I feel like this is exactly the kind of book you pick up when you want something easy to read, nothing too dark or serious, and something you can enjoy purely for the vibes. I had SO much fun with it, and I genuinely had trouble putting it down. I could not help it. These characters and their sheer absurdity were like a drug to me, and apparently I had absolutely no intention of getting clean.

A small trigger warning for torture, bullying, including a particularly twisted version of it, and suicide, because these themes are not exactly handled with kid gloves. And honestly, that discomfort works in the book’s favour, because they are presented incredibly well.

ㅤ ‍‍‍‍‍‍ ‍‍‍‍‍‍ ⋅ ☾ The banter was absolutely EVERYTHING. I am not even joking when I say I was genuinely laughing out loud at these characters’ interactions. The FMC is an absolute menace, a rather nervous one, yes, but a menace nonetheless. I loved her and her dark sense of humour, and honestly, watching her interact with everyone was half the fun of this book. She was chaotic in exactly the way I wanted her to be.

ㅤ ‍‍‍‍‍‍ ‍‍‍‍‍‍ ⋅ ☾ I loved all the medical talk in this book. I am in absolutely no position to judge or verify the correctness of everything being thrown at me, but honestly, I did not even care. Apparently, all I needed was medical jargon and I was sold.

They did not really bother me enough to ruin neither my reading experience, nor my rating, but you know...just a heads-up, because you WILL notice them.

I do feel like this is the type of book that puts a lot more emphasis on the romance, but honestly, it was such a refreshing way to watch a love story unfold. You go from jokes, to protection, to obsession, to some... very freaky and carnal scenes, so you pretty much get the full package. The dynamic and chemistry between the FMC and MMC are there from the very beginning, and that made it nearly impossible to get bored whenever they were on the page together.

ㅤ ‍‍‍‍‍‍ ‍‍‍‍‍‍ ⋅ ☾ I do admit I would have loved to see a bit more development in the ending. It somehow felt a little rushed, with so many things happening and so many revelations being thrown at me one after another that they did not have enough time to feel as impactful as I would have liked. It was still the perfect ending for this kind of book, but honestly, I think it just needed another at least 30 pages to let everything breathe a little.

I started this book planning to read a few chapters before bed and ended up finishing it in one sitting, turning the last page in the early hours of the morning, wrecked and thoroughly happy. I could not put it down.

What hooked me first was the humour. The characters trade barbs and teasing so naturally that I kept bursting into laughter, and each of them speaks in a voice so distinct that it stayed in my head long after I’d finished. The conversations never feel forced. They are sharp, warm, and full of small moments that make everyone feel like a real person rather than a character on a page.

Willow Rose Briar is the heart of it all. She is an occult pathologist witch who speaks to the dead, and her coven has cast her out. She is a walking contradiction: brilliant yet slightly unhinged, deadly serious about her work yet hopelessly accident-prone, and so gloriously odd that everything she says is gold. I was in stitches every time she spoke. Seeing a woman embrace her strangeness so completely, after all the cruelty she has survived, was a breath of fresh air. For all her sharp wit and competence, she melts into a flustered mess the second her commanding husband appears. She is a morbid little marshmallow, and I would protect her with my life.

Then there’s Logan. He is obsessive, protective, and possessive, and anyone who calls him toxic can take it up with me, because he was perfection. He has endured so much, yet he would still walk through fire for the people in his care. The way he calls Willow “honey”? Unreal. Even his smoking was hot, and he is the rare blonde I will happily accept.

And their niece/daughter Piper she was seriously stinky sweet. I absolutely loved her and the nicknames she gave everyone around her. An absolute joy to be around

Be warned, though: the humour sits alongside some very dark material, and a few passages are genuinely uncomfortable to read. Trigger warning for torture and bullying. That discomfort is exactly why it works.

There is also a tenderness here that caught me off guard. Watching someone who has never quite belonged begin to find her people, the ones who see the quirks and sharp edges and stay anyway, is a special kind of comforting. I will never tire of stories that remind us family is sometimes not who you are born to, but who makes you feel a little less alone.

Dark, bloody, funny, romantic, and unexpectedly comforting. I picked it up expecting a fun witch and vampire romance and came out ridiculously attached to the entire found family. Overall it was even better than i ever expected.
