It’s time to pay the Piper.

Our world cracked open like an egg. Power burst out, and with it, nine monstrous Banes ready to destroy everything. The powerful Core fae finally slayed them all, but each Bane left a Plague to punish us.

But that’s not the only thing they left behind.

I’m half Bane, half fae, and wholly hated. Survival means enduring every vicious glare and cursed outbreak. Until one day, a Plague descends upon my city that means certain death.

Then he comes. The Pied Piper.

He rids my city of the Plague, but it comes at a cost. One hundred and thirty people are taken as payment. Including me.

I don’t know what he’s going to do to us, but I know I have to get home. My family’s lives depend on it. But the Piper won’t let me go, and something wicked stirs between us. The sweet scent in my veins beckons him, while the seductive lure of his magic sinks under my skin.

I have to escape before it’s too late. Because if I don’t, it’s not just my heart at risk…

But the entire world.

4.25 ⭐🎧

I fear the morally questionable Pied Piper has dragged me out of my reading slump.

The Sweetest Fiend had me SAT. Forced proximity, aggressive caretaking, a filthy mouth and Varrik going from captor to “Death can’t have you.” Modern medicine could never.

Did the plot occasionally require a welfare check? Yes.

The romance? Alive. Thriving. Paying taxes.

What worked for me
(aka: the Piper provided)

Old fairytale. New trauma

This is exactly what I want from a fairytale reimagining. I could see the Pied Piper bones immediately - Hamelyn, the rats, the pipe, the multicoloured cloak, the desperate city and the terrible price - but Raven Kennedy built an entirely different fantasy world around them.

The Plagues were especially effective. Flies stripping people to bone, poisoned water, rats flooding the streets, a permanently wounded sky - it gave Brekon a genuinely bleak atmosphere without burying me beneath lore. I learned the world mostly by watching Esti survive inside it.

And then came my favourite inversion: fiends are treated as monsters and blamed for the Plagues, only for their blood to potentially be the thing that can stop them.

Nothing like generations of prejudice discovering they've been bullying the emergency medical supplies.

Forced proximity, thank you for your service

The romance was easily my favourite part because the progression actually felt like progression.

Varrik and Esti start with genuine hostility and an enormous power imbalance. He steals her. She fights him. He calls her “blight.” She develops a running list of his offences and eventually takes a needle to his belongings. Romance.

But the forced proximity keeps changing form - one horse, one tent, one bedroll, wound care, his room, the Latch - and every stage pushes their relationship somewhere new. Attraction grows through observation, banter, jealousy, caretaking and vulnerability rather than the magical bond doing all the work.

I loved knowing Varrik wanted her before the Latch chose her. He was already terrorising Conrad over her recovery.

Sir had an Esti problem before magic made it official.

For a man this possessive, Varrik becomes surprisingly serious about choice. He asks. He waits. Esti chooses. By their second kiss, his permission feels like knocking on a door - and she’s the one who opens it.

That distinction mattered when their entire relationship began with him literally taking away her control.

Varrik’s type is Esti....and more Esti

This man noticing everything about her absolutely fed me.

Esti has spent her life hiding the features that mark her as a fiend. Her horns. Her sparkling freckles. Her hair. Even her emotion-changing nails get dismissed as paper dye.

Unfortunately for Esti, Varrik has eyes and misses nothing.

He remembers the colour of her nails. Learns what the colours mean. Notices when her breathing changes. Touches the horns nobody touches. Removes the powder covering her freckles because “I like seeing you.” He has a veillet made that doesn't hide her horns but frames them with silver rings like crowns.

And when she sees those silver-crowned horns in the mirror:
“I’ve never looked so beautiful.”
“I’ve never looked so utterly fiend.”
The world taught Esti to tone herself down. Varrik’s preference is full volume.

He isn't attracted to her despite everything the world has taught her to hide. He's fascinated by it. His attraction feels like ongoing research with increasingly inappropriate fieldwork.

And “Blight” somehow going from hostile nickname to something that felt like a caress? Unfortunately, I am the target audience.

Caretaking with a side of menace

Varrik’s caretaker era starts long before he’s willing to admit he has one.

His denial apparently involves extremely hands-on wound care. One minute Esti is just his irritating little blight; the next he’s furious about her untreated pain, personally tending her injuries and ready to make thirteen lashes everyone’s problem.

Then he growls:
“Death can’t have you.”
Sir. Denial is a river in Egypt. This is devotion.

I loved that Varrik’s protectiveness never comes at the expense of Esti’s competence. She saves herself. She saves him. Eventually, they help save everyone else.

Nobody gets demoted to damsel. We love to see it.

Romantic suffering: Now a Group Activity

Conrad, Frid and the rest of Varrik’s orbit were such a fun addition because they knew this man was down catastrophic before he’d even received the memo.

The teasing, jealousy and general refusal to take his Esti-related suffering seriously were hilarious but I also loved what they revealed about him. Varrik feels different around people who already know and love him - less Piper, more man.

Turns out found family is also excellent for bullying a Govern through his first crush.

Where it lost me
(aka: meanwhile, the plot)

Varrik gave me obsession before he gave me Varrik

I got Varrik’s obsession in HD while the man behind the Piper stayed frustratingly out of focus.

The prologue gave me a broken man. The romance gave me a devoted one. I kept waiting for the bridge between them.

We get glimpses, especially when his Defender side cracks through, but thirty missing years is a LOT of character development to leave off-page. I finished knowing exactly how Varrik loves but not enough about how he became Varrik.

Too much pining. Not enough piping.

The slow opening didn’t bother me. Esti needed a life worth missing before Varrik could rudely Pied Piper her out of it.

My pacing issue arrived later, when these two had reached mutual yearning: painfully obvious edition, and proceeded to marinate in it. Meanwhile, the plot had several unanswered questions and was presumably tapping its watch.

Eventually the romance stopped edging the reader and delivered. Spectacularly. Hydration recommended.

Then I checked the remaining page count.

Oh.

There were still mysteries in this house.

All that growth just to choose violence

The final stretch circles back to everything I had been waiting on: Hamelyn, Esti's mother, the origin of her strange magic and the possibility that the history we've been told about Banes isn't remotely complete.

Then Marryn sprouts antlers.

At 97%.

Ma'am.

And somehow THAT isn't even the cruelest part.

The entire romance is a slow evolution from taking to asking, force to choice, possession to reciprocity.

Then the ending takes every inch of that growth and weaponises it against Esti.

The man who learned to ask takes again. The woman who finally chose him loses her choice again.

That’s the betrayal.

Then Raven resurrects the three words we've been carrying since Esti first read his stars:

Defender. Thief. LIAR.

We’d spent the whole book sanding the warning edges off those words.

Defender became obvious. Thief became almost affectionate - he stole her heart. Liar became something I assumed we'd eventually understand.

Then:
“And stars, unlike him, never lie.”
RUDE.

It’s an effective cliffhanger because I desperately need to know what both Varrik and Esti have been withholding. Unfortunately, several of my answers appear to have been kidnapped along with Esti.

Book Two, please report to the principal’s office.

Final Thoughts

For all my complaints about the plot occasionally standing in the corner while Varrik and Esti stared at each other, I had such a good time with this.

Esti was easy to root for: kind without being spineless, funny without becoming comic relief and stubbornly capable even when the world treated her like something monstrous. Varrik was exactly my preferred flavour of morally grey - possessive, ruthless and deeply questionable but increasingly forced to confront the difference between protecting someone and controlling them.

Most importantly, the romance earned my investment. The magical bond didn't replace relationship development; it complicated it. Attraction wasn't trust. Wanting wasn't permission. And when Esti eventually chose Varrik, I believed her.

Then he shattered that trust and left me staring into the middle distance.

So Book Two is less a choice and more a summons.

And the audio? Sean Masters and Isabelle Turner in duet narration were dangerous. Isabelle gave Esti exactly the right amount of bite, while Sean's Varrik could go from threatening Piper to vulnerable caretaker to saying things that had me lowering the volume like the people around me could somehow hear my thoughts.

“Death can’t have you” was already good.

Sean Masters growling it was a separate rating category.

Final Verdict

4.25 ⭐️ — A genuinely addictive Pied Piper reimagining with excellent forced proximity, earned chemistry and a morally grey romance that occasionally left the plot buffering.

My reading slump died so my trust issues could live.

4.25⭐️ This book will ruin your sleep schedule. Seriously, I could not put it down!

The beginning is VERY action-packed, and I loved the post-apocalyptic setting (you rarely see that in Romantasy!). The middle portion has less plot movement and focuses more on the romance, but the chemistry and banter between Esti and the Piper definitely made up for it🙌

Esti is such a refreshing FMC. I often get tired of reading about the same copy and paste heroines: skilled fighters, lost princesses, etc. Esti is simply a girl trying to survive and protect the people she loves. Despite her softer demeanor, I loved seeing the Piper bring out her snarkier, more stubborn side. Poor girl could NOT catch a break, but through it all, she remained the “sweetest” fiend!😉

To say I’m obsessed with the Piper is a huge understatement. He’s everything I love in an MMC: mysterious, powerful, possessive, and a little unhinged at times. There’s an immediate attraction between him and Esti, but thankfully, there wasn’t as much insta-lust as I expected. The spice felt like a satisfying payoff and didn’t occur too soon in the story.

My main complaint is that I REALLY think this book should have been told from both Esti’s and the Piper’s perspectives. The Piper is emotionally guarded and hides behind a prickly exterior, so it was sometimes difficult to get a clear sense of his feelings for Esti. There were several scenes and interactions I would have loved to read from his perspective. Additionally, the whole “latching” concept (the book’s version of mating bonds) felt very forced, so I didn’t care for that aspect.

As for the ending, I completely agree with fellow reviewers who said it came across as hypocritical. I’m not sure yet whether I think it was a smart move; that will depend on how the second book handles it and where the story goes from here.

Overall, this was such an addictive read that you could easily finish in one sitting!

(Friends, I am sorry 😢)

I loved The Plated Prisoner series because it was raw, layered and emotionally complex. This, on the other hand, felt one dimensional.

The plot was spelled out, nothing was left to simmer, and I found myself getting bored.

The clichés and tropes sucked every last bit of originality out of the story. At times, it felt like someone had taken the highest polling BookTok phrases, thrown them into a blender and called it a novel.

The premise:

A reimagining of the Pied Piper, where Esti, a young woman devoted to caring for her sick mother, is taken by the mysterious Pied Piper.

Nine Banes emerged into the Fae world and each unleashed a different Plague before being defeated. Their descendants are half Bane, half Fae and are called Fiends.

Fiends are looked down upon and treated terribly by the lesser Fae.

There are a few Core Fae left in the world, powerful enough to fight the Plagues, but their magic comes at a cost. They are looking for a way to rid the world of the Plagues permanently.

When the Pied Piper, who is a Core Fae, comes to Esti’s town, he clears the Rat and Poison Plagues. In payment, he demands 130 citizens. Esti is one of the claimed Fiends, and she isn’t happy about it.

This is where I needed more complexity. I needed to feel her concern for her life. I needed the Piper to be darker and more morally grey.
Instead, I got “who did this to you”.

I wanted Esti to think she was going to die, and not immediately find herself under his protection.
We should be allowed to dislike the MMC for a little bit and still love him at the end. He really doesn’t have to BookTok swoon at all times.

They instantly start bickering with each other, because Esti isn’t worried about her life at all, which means they are, of course, mates… spoon feeding me again.

In reality, this was, at best, a very loose and rather disappointing reimagining of the Pied Piper. The original story’s themes and underlying message were barely represented. Raven Kennedy’s Midas reimagining was a much more successful story.

The chemistry between the two main characters was completely nonexistent from the get go. They behaved like an old married couple from the minute they met, except without the history, intimacy or emotional foundation to justify it.

I found Esti petulant. The word was used over and over again, but in the end, it just annoyed me. Because it just underlined, that she was indeed Petulant.

The Piper felt like a character assembled from a collection of BookTok approved traits. I didn’t find their dynamic dark, compelling or particularly suspenseful. It felt childish.

Esti was whipped and then handed over to The Piper by the ruling Core Fae of her city, and she wants to go back without thinking twice about it?What does she think she can achieve by going home without a plan?
She doesn’t even know where she is? It’s lazy storytelling. At least make her find a library and do some research into her situation or something, what do I know?

Her lies and omissions weren’t harmless mistakes; they had consequences for an entire world. Yet somehow, the narrative never seemed particularly interested in making her confront the full weight of that.

The emotional conflict is supposed to be that he’s trying to save the world at great cost to himself, whereas she only thinks about protecting her mother? How does that even measure up?

Sure, the Piper was controlling and took her against her will, but it didn’t feel as though the alternative would be any better for Esti. So, again, I felt nothing. I never got the dark, dangerous or suspenseful atmosphere I was expecting.

We weren’t allowed to dislike the Piper for a second. And ultimately that is kind of a boring setup.

Let me hate him, like I did with Kelly Cove’s The Hidden of Vrohkaria or Malyr in Feathers So Vicious. Those were courageous choices by the two authors. I hated both MMCs in the good way for the entirety of the first book. Deliciousness!

But more importantly, let’s talk about what a slow burn really should be. In my not so humble opinion. 🙄

A kiss at 60% does not make a romance a slow burn.
Slow burn is about yearning, longing, anticipation and tension. It’s about making the reader feel the pull between two characters long before anything happens.

Here, they were simply in the same room for a very long time. I felt no real escalation, no frustration, and no emotional build up.

Spoilers: The ending.

The double standard surrounding Esti’s mother was frustrating, to say the least.

Her mother was one of the nine Banes, the very beings responsible for unleashing the Plagues upon the world. And Esti remained loyal to her, choosing to conceal the truth because she’s her mom!

The world suffered, but apparently that was an acceptable price to pay so she could hold on to her mother?
He captures her mother and takes Esti with him, and THAT is the conflict?
His life’s purpose was to eradicate the Plagues that her mother had help unleash. What exactly did Esti expect? 🤦‍♀️ I was so confused that this was the big betrayal, there really was no moral dilemma here.
My daughter should totally hand me over if I did something like that.
So I needed more moral complexity, Esti was wrong. Please let me know what you think.

Should you read it?
It’s a no from me.
