Something wicked is coming...

In the sequel to City of Gods and Monsters, Loren Calla and Darien Cassel have settled back into their old routines, grateful that the whirlwind of dangers they’d faced on Kalendae and the weeks leading up to it are now behind them―or so they think.

But Loren soon finds herself caught in a new storm of trouble when the Terran Imperator blackmails her into working for him. There’s only one problem: he expects her to use her magic, and she doesn’t seem to have any of it left. To keep Darien and the other Devils safe, she agrees to everything the imperator asks of her. But the secrets she is forced to keep from Darien, and her involvement with the imperator’s son, threaten to drive her away from Darien in ways she might never recover from…

As Randal Slade’s successor, Darien wants nothing to do with the throne his father once sat in, but the city of Angelthene has other plans. The Magical Protections Unit asks for his help in taking down the illegal arms trade his father once dabbled in, and to do that Darien must claim his place at the top of the tiers of Darkslayers―a crown the other circles are willing to kill to possess. As he spirals deeper into the secrets his father kept, he makes enemies of friends and friends of enemies―and finds himself buried so deep in Angelthene’s underbelly that he is uncertain he’ll ever get out of it. And if that wasn’t enough, Darien fears he is going crazy when he starts to see creatures that only he can see…

Return to Angelthene in City of Souls and Sinners, a gripping, action-packed tale that tackles themes of love and loss, light and darkness, and the battle between good and evil―and what happens when you can’t tell which is which.

Kayla Edwards is the USA Today and International bestselling author of the new adult House of Devils series, including City of Gods and Monsters, City of Souls and Sinners, City of Lies and Legends, and City of Smoke and Brimstone. DNF @ 60%

Let me preface this by saying: if you’re publishing a book that’s over 900 pages and there’s not even a hint of character growth, it might be time to reevaluate some choices. Personally, I don’t want to commit to something this long without seeing some kind of development.

Goodreads shows this series as seven books in total, and yet... why was the romance rushed so badly?

By Book 2, the MMC is already talking about marriage and kids with the FMC — but there’s zero emotional connection between them. Honestly, half the time I forgot they were even together. There’s no depth, just physical attraction. And over half of this book, they’re doing their own thing, hiding things from each other, and lying constantly.

Kayla had a real opportunity to build this relationship — to let us see that connection grow — but instead it feels like she just skipped the groundwork entirely. As a reader, I want to watch the characters evolve and go through that emotional journey with them.

And beyond the romance? There’s no personal growth either. Darien keeps spiraling into the same self-destructive habits, and Loren only ever acts if it benefits him. Why couldn’t she explore her magic for herself?

We’ve all read tortured heroes before — and I usually love them — but I cannot stand Darien. He’s aware of his toxicity and just… doesn’t care? He keeps waxing poetic about Loren being his “light,” but never actually changes. There’s not even the slightest hint of growth.

Honestly, this story might’ve worked better as pure fantasy without any romance at all.

And the writing… whew. Some of it is just plain bad. Like this gem:

"He was able to snap his magic back into position, shielding him from yet another blow that would’ve been fatal if he’d acted half a second later.

Detective Finn Solace also gets in touch with Darien shortly after, asking him to assist him and a new head detective Nolan in taking down Gaven and his syndicate. In the meantime, Loren is approached by Klay - a new student who also happens to be the Imperators son. They coerce her into assisting them to find the real Well which is still out there. The catch? They have put some spell on her which prevents her from speaking about it to anyone…even Darien. Oh and they will kill everyone in her family if she even tries.

Lies and betrayal threaten to tear the two lovers apart around every corner. Can they overcome the many obstacles stacked against them?

We get to learn a lot more about Loren and Darien’s relationship. It’s wonderfully written. The dialogue was so good, you could feel the emotion between these two and the build up of their relationship.
It was a 3,5/5🌶️ on the spice scale. Nothing that really pushed any limits but again, beautifully written.
Darien is just head over heels for her, and vice versa. You can feel their love for each other and their pain and hurt.

We get to learn more about Dallas and Max and their relationship too. On a lesser scale than the MC’s but it was nice to get their perspectives as well.
We get a little more insight into Dallas’ relationship with her parents (Rourk and Taega Bright).

Some new characters are introduced, Blue, Klay etc…and they’re great for the story.

There are obviously the same old ones we loved from the first - all the Devils (Tanner, Max, Ivy, Lacey, Jack, Darien and Travis), The Angels, The Wargs, Vampires etc….

Singer and Loren’s bond is really cool, we see more of that in the story.

Mortifer the Hob also features a bit more and I loved his character in the story.

All in all, I loved every second of this book, and I cannot wait for the next one. The story is so compelling and well paced and it was a pleasure to read.

This book was ridiculous. Was it good? No. Well-written? No. Did I have a good time reading it? Yes, but only when I could look past all the gaping plot holes and inconsistencies.

I was questioning every decision the author made. There were so many sporadic events that didn’t amount to or lead to anything.
• So much build-up to the dance, but they were there for half a chapter and nothing came of it?
• I thought there was going to be more to the medicinal tea that Loren’s dad mentioned?
• They talked about how addictive Venom was, but Loren and Darien both took a lot of it and never felt addicted? There was a missed opportunity for a storyline there.

There were more crazy conveniences that made me feel like the author was making up the plot as she went along.
• Dom just happens to know an extremely rare, unknown, and ancient language and just happens to be at their house to translate when a stranger shows up speaking it?
• How convenient that there’s a “weak spot” wherever Loren needs one.
• The guy they pulled for questioning didn’t die until after he gave up all the information because the drug he used had a delay on it?

The inconsistencies were never-ending.
• The Butcher, a drug dealer, wants to help Darien put scumbags behind bars?
• Is this supposed to take place in a futuristic world? Or is this modern day? There was a mix of both. Also, why don’t the cars have Bluetooth?!
• Sometimes they would say “gods” (like “gods-awful) and other times they would say god (like “oh my god”).
• Darien set off a grenade in the library of the biggest & best university in the city, setting everything on fire. But everything was repaired and back to normal a few chapters later? Wouldn’t all the books have burned?
• The leader of a vampire clan, who was hundreds of years old, is suddenly murdered and everyone’s just like, “huh that’s weird”.
• Why was a carnival for families and children being held at the black market?
• The suit was able to withstand the explosion of the Well and save Loren from a building literally collapsing on top of her; but now they get torn up by demon claws?
• The Life Clock was on Loren’s neck but she could somehow see how much time was left?

This was not a literary work of art. There was too much description of pointless, mundane actions like walking down a store aisle, driving to the next location, pressing buttons in the elevator, etc. The dialogue was corny and reminded me of a low-budget Lifetime movie. That being said, this book was also fast-paced and entertaining. I would have enjoyed it more if I wasn’t constantly thinking of all the stuff that didn’t make sense or add up. I will not be continuing with the series.
