A young woman bargains with a demon to save her brothers, only to lose her heart in this slow-burn romantasy about dark magic, unlikely love, and the imperfect beauty of mortality.

This stunning hardcover edition of Reap & Sow will feature deluxe embellishments, including a dazzling metallic jacket, full color printed edges, patterned end papers, and a specially designed case.

The penniless in Blackbridge have only two choices: Sell the body or sell the soul. And thanks to Eda Shaw, business is booming.

Indentured to a capricious demon known as Mr. Night, Eda and her brothers arrange illicit Pacts on his behalf, luring the city's most desperate into trading years of their lives for their deepest desires. When a Pact goes awry and her brothers are arrested, Eda realizes she’ll need an infernal ally of her own: Kit'rath, an infuriating demon with a curious penchant for humanity and the only being in Blackbridge that Mr. Night seems to fear. As they race to rescue her family and save the city from ruin, an undeniable connection sparks between mortal and demon.

But Kit's help doesn’t come cheap. Now, Eda risks losing her heart to the one who claims her years—and saving herself may prove the steepest cost of all.

Rich with gothic atmosphere, sharp wit, and simmering romantic tension, Reap & Sow is a fantasy debut perfect for fans of Rachel Gillig, Hannah Whitten, and Heather Fawcett.

I see your down bad human men and I raise you.. down bad DEMON MEN.

A note on expectations. What genre is this book? Is it YA? Adult? No one knows. I went into this expecting YA, and I think that's the best expectation to have. It has some spice and gore and other more adult themes, but it still feels like YA in a lot of ways.

I've had a busy and stressful couple of weeks, which might have contributed to how uncharacteristically willing I was to just turn my brain off and enjoy this book without picking everything apart. I don't know if I would have enjoyed it less under different circumstances. I don't think I would have, but I definitely don't have as many critical thoughts as I usually do, for better or worse. Ironically, I wish I could read like this all the time. Maybe I need to start stressing more??

Blackbridge is a gloomy city of canals, towering spires, wet stone and oil smoke. It's a city where faith and corruption coexist, sometimes so closely that it's difficult to tell them apart. Somewhere in the middle of it all, inside Shaw's Antiques and Curiosities, a desperate mortal is making a bargain with a demon. Not for his soul. These demons are interested in something much more useful: years of human life.

Eda Shaw and her two brothers have inherited a rather unfortunate debt from their father, who made a spectacularly bad bargain with a powerful demon known as Mr Night. They spend their days arranging Pacts on his behalf, watching desperate people sign away years of their lives in exchange for whatever they need. When a Pact goes wrong and her brothers are arrested, Eda finds herself in need of a bargain of her own. Enter Kit'rath, a mysterious demon who might just be powerful enough to help her.

The dynamic between Eda and Kit feels predictable in the way most romantasy enemies-to-lovers do, but I don't think anyone would go into this expecting any different. They bicker. There's fun banter. Kit has no shortage of innuendos. Do I need to tell my therapist about my love for male characters who use sexual innuendos as insults? Anyway.

Despite their bickering, their dynamic felt balanced. They put their differences aside and work together rather well, and then Kit pushes her into the river. Balance! I like that the demons felt rather ruthless in this book. It's a very fine line to balance. Make them too nice and they won't feel like demons, but make them too cruel and the romance will just be weird. Kit is perfectly in-between. His threats didn't feel empty when he made them, but he wasn't still threatening to drag her by the throat by the time the romance developed, which I really appreciate. It will take me right out of a supposed romance when they're supposed to be in love and one (or both) is still acting like they're enemies. Instead of doing both at the same time, he goes from threatening to YEARNINGG.

Do you love Osric? Howl? Cardan? Astarion? Here's another drama queen to add to your list. Kit is mysterious, condescending, ruthless, powerful and enough of a vain drama queen to complain about not being able to sleep on silk pillows. Life is so hard. But poor Kit falls first. And he's so down bad. Eda keeps pulling away while Kit keeps falling, and underneath all that arrogance, there is nothing but devotion and worship. For a book with so much religion, it's kinda ironic that the demon is the one who really worships, if ya catch my drift. 🌚

I quite liked Eda, and I think that is largely thanks to the fact that I was expecting YA. She is 20, and she acts the way a 20-year-old might. She can probably come across as pretty naive and impulsive if you're expecting her to act like a grown adult, but I felt like she acted appropriately for her age and the genre. She has been sheltered her whole life and has only really existed inside the bubble of the antiques shop and finding dealies for the Pacts to get out of the debt they inherited. The bubble bursts completely unexpectedly, and Eda has no experience to rely on, only desperation.

Eda is brave and so selfless, willing to give up quite literally everything in order to protect the people she cares about. But she's also bitter and judgmental. She has spent years watching people make Pacts, and she has very little sympathy for them. In her eyes, they're foolish, greedy or weak. Then she finds herself desperate enough to make an absolutely insane bargain herself, and it slowly becomes clear that she never really considered what drives people to make those choices in the first place. And ironically, it's Kit who starts making her question that. It takes a demon to teach Eda to have a little faith in humanity.

The side characters also stand out to me, and I usually forget about side characters as soon as they're off the page. There's Robin, a stern, witchy lady who will absolutely tell you to get your shit together in no uncertain terms. She's brusque and doesn't seem particularly interested in being anyone's friend, but underneath that exterior is someone just as selfless and willing to help as Eda. And then there's Tripp, Kit's familiar, a rude little imp who I absolutely adored. I have a weakness for these tiny, troublesome creatures and their lack of manners.

There are some interesting religious themes, particularly the way power and corruption are intertwined with faith. The Faithhood claims to protect the people of Blackbridge from demonic influence, but the people preaching salvation aren't necessarily any less dangerous than the demons themselves. There's an interesting question running through the story about what separates divine intervention from a demonic bargain. After all, if someone is desperate enough to beg a higher power for help, does it really matter to them whether the thing answering their prayers is a god or a demon? Is there even a difference? And what happens when the people offering salvation have just as much to gain from that desperation as the demons do?

The pacing was pretty much perfect. The romance and the larger plot take turns moving the story forward. There's always something happening. I never felt like I was waiting for the romance to resume so we could get to the good stuff, or waiting for the characters to stop flirting so the actual story could continue. Both parts were interesting enough to keep me reading, and neither felt like it was taking time away from the other.

I really think this is shaping up to be a great series. Everything in this book worked well for me. All the characters, the plot, and that LAST SENTENCE???

I’m elated to announce that this book, one of my most anticipated reads of the year so far, did not disappoint in the slightest. Not only did it not disappoint, it surpassed every single expectation I had of it.

Eda was the kind of character you can’t help but root for. She didn’t always make the wisest decisions, specifically in the beginning, but it was never difficult to see where she was coming from. This girl had me screaming “stopppp” at my phone screen with that one thing she did at the start, not just because she was being a complete idiot, but also because I just didn’t want her to suffer the consequences that were so obviously coming.

Kit’rath is a terrible name. Lucky for him, I love him enough to overlook that. He’s a demon, so that’s already +1000 points, but he’s also hilarious. The moment where he called Tripp and Eda children and sarcastically chastised them for bickering had me laughing out loud. And deep down, surprisingly sensitive for a demon. How fresh that he be the one to see the world with hope and awe while Eda is jaded and bitter.

Don’t get it twisted, he’s also a sassy asshole. So basically he is everything I love in a man—except the hope and awe thing, of course—especially the dark hair, because I can only tolerate so many blonds.

The romance was to die for. Plain and simple. I’m doing my best to avoid spoilers, but I HAVE to mention this one thing:

When he took her to the play, and she mentioned not liking sad endings, and he asked her if she would wish she hadn’t come if the ending would sadden her, but he was obviously talking about their ending? UGH. Literally a PERFECT scene.

I wish I could quote specific moments from the writing, because I loved it and there were so many that would make me squeal if I was a person who squealed. It was deceptively simple—it never tried too hard, wasn’t littered with fancy language, but still managed to be vivid, layered, and PULL ON MY HEARTSTRINGS THE WHOLE TIME.

The world was immersive; it felt lived in rather than a mere backdrop for the events of the story. From the cities to the people to the religion to Kit’s home realm, everything felt fully realized and authentic. I especially enjoyed the details and lore of the faith system. It was quite intriguing, but more importantly, it was incredibly fleshed out.

I loved the nuance in how Eda and her brothers viewed their father. Eda could only see him as a selfish, foolish man who sacrificed his children, while her brothers remembered a time before that, when he was a good dad, and still held so much love for him despite what he did. They mourned a man Eda never knew, and wanted her to do so as well, but she just couldn’t, especially with the way she was essentially groomed by Mr. Night. Their differing perspectives, along with her sentimental attachment to the demon they were indebted to, added so many layers to the grief they each shared and made it feel that much more real.

The ending pt. 1 was absolutely perfect—dare I say I’m obsessed?

ENDING PT. 2 WTF WAS THAT LAST PARAGRAPH? How am I meant to wait for a second book? I shall remain in excruciating. agony until it comes out.

Some stories flirt with darkness. This one signs a contract in blood and asks you to read the fine print.

From the very first chapter, we’re dropped into the gothic gloom with Eda—desperate, determined, and just reckless enough to consider that making a pact with a demon might be a reasonable life choice. She needs to save her two brothers. The stakes? High. The atmosphere? Deliciously shadowed. The moral compass? Slightly… negotiable.

It’s an opening that hooks you by the collar and whispers, “Trust me, darling…” before vanishing into the mist. And yes—you’ll absolutely want more after that first chapter.

Eda’s journey is laced with witches, demons, long-buried truths, and the kind of found family that blooms in the most unexpected (and probably cursed) places. There’s something deeply satisfying about watching her forge new friendships while unravelling secrets that may cost her more than she bargained for. Because really—can you have it all? Salvation, love, power… and no consequences?

Let’s talk about the MMC. Because oh, we must.

He arrives wrapped in shadow and sarcasm, delivering dry, razor-sharp humour that had me laughing out loud more than once. The banter? Immaculate. Effortless. The kind that crackles on the page. And his persistent use of “Darling”? Absolutely swoon-worthy. It’s the verbal equivalent of a gloved hand tipped under your chin—charming, dangerous, and just a little bit mocking.

But make no mistake: in a story about demons, the devil is always in the details.

The magic system hints at rules, loopholes, and the fine print of infernal bargains. You can’t help but read carefully, searching for the catch. Because there is one. There’s always one. The tension doesn’t just come from looming threats—it comes from the creeping suspicion that someone, somewhere, hasn’t read the contract closely enough.

This book revels in its gothic setting: shadowed corridors, whispered truths, morally grey choices, and the intoxicating pull of power at a price. It balances humour with darkness beautifully, allowing moments of levity to slice through the gloom without ever undercutting the stakes.

So the question lingers long after you turn the page:
If you were offered everything you ever wanted… would you check the details?

A wickedly fun, banter-filled, demon-infused tale that proves once again—trusting a devil is easy.

Understanding him? That’s the tricky part.

Some people like their mmcs akin to golden retrievers, or German shepards or even pitbulls. But how about the cat-coded mmc? You know the type: Cardan, Howl, Astarion, Antal from Voidwalker, Mal from How To Find A Nameless Fae... This one is another. Bonus points if he actually has a tail he emotes with, or with his ears. I have a type, and I'm not ashamed to admit it. Kit is very much that: charming, flirty, funny, fashionable, somewhat cowardly, will dance around subjects rather than jump into them head on, and in romantic dynamic he's more of an enabler than pressurer. And I always appreciate this. He let Eda withdraw when she wanted to, and didn't jump at her until she asked him to. I love when a romance plot hands the agency back to fmc instead of having the love interest coerce her into a relationship (why is that so popular still, I don't know).

But anyway, first things first. This isn't my first encounter with Charlotte B. Plumb's writing. There was a time when I beta read for strangers on the internet and I found out that Jessica Faust, head of the BookEnds Literary Agency, was right in her assessment that most books get rejected from publication because they're boring. Charlotte's book stood out in the sea of boring, saggy, soggy, meandering plots. It was propulsive, it had engaging characters, it had an interesting and logically tied together plot. Unfortunately, it wasn't this book, and I wish that book also saw the light of day, because it was so good. But this one? This one is better.

I loved that the plot starts from Eda, the protagonist and sole pov of this novel, making a careless mistake. Too many books I pick up are so preoccupied with making the protagonist "likeable" and morally right that they never allow them to make a grave mistake. These characters are always forced into the plot against their protests, accused unfairly, or burdened with useless family they have to provide for. Eda's brothers are the opposite of useless, they're more reasonable than her and they try to protect her even if it means sacrificing themselves for her. So her guilt after making a mistake her brothers take the blow for is much more relatable than when the family is useless and causing problems and yet the mc for some odd reason martyrs themselves for those deadweights. The sibling bonds here were very well done and nobody felt like a personalityless plot prop.

Eda is desperate, and out of desperation people often make rash or stupid decisions, but it never felt like pointless stubbornness or catching idiot ball story. She was also very reasonable towards her developing attraction towards Kit, she kept reminding herself about her goals, the divide between humans and demons and the dire consequences of her pact, it wasn't one of those "I hate him for no reason but I can't resist him" romances there are dime a dozen in romantasy.

The plot progressed at a steady pace and the romance filled the downtime and never felt like happening at the expense of the main plot of saving Eda's brothers and fixing all the problems the trio was in.

I also liked Eda wasn't a blushing virgin. She keeps reminiscing of her past lover, Wymond, who was okay - he wasn't an evil ex or an awful lover, as it commonly happens in romances. And she pushed him away due to her secrets rather than because of some great trauma. I liked how it was all normal instead of usual story of no past experience or horrible ones.

I appreciate that Kit's greatest challenge was facing his fears. I feel commonly that character arc is reserved for female characters while male characters are not allowed cowardice, because it's perceived as too unmanly. But I enjoy when the character's flaws aren't convenient, it makes them feel so much more human, and facing those flaws so much more compelling.

I loved the side characters too. Besides Eda's brothers, we have the "witch" Robin, the imp Tripp who played the role of "sassy animal companion" even though he's not exactly a baby dragon, but still adorable, and Tenelle who's a honorable Black woman leader of the holy knights, she really reminded me of Seelah from the game Pathfinder: Wrath of the Righteous, and I loved that game and I loved that character.

The humour in this book was impeccable. So many "humorous" books don't work for me because they feel more like a string of gags than an actual story. Here the humour was a good addition to the plot, but didn't replace it. Esp. chapters 15, 16, 30, 34 & 35. I felt it was more British humour with its sarcasm and deadpan tone than the more common "Marvel banter". Yes, the author is British, so we have all the trousers and arses in the story.

There are 2 spicy chapters back to back at around 77%, but they aren't very explicit, they're firmly open door but more on the euphemistic side. I saw this book was initially classified as YA in the UK, but since then has been moved into the new New Adult imprint, Dusk. I'm truly fascinated with the development of all those "new adult" "crossover" "romantasy" imprints because they all hint at the old truth: we need to free women-authored romantic fantasy from the confinements of YA. I enjoy a lot of YA novels, but I also like adventure fantasy that isn't that dark and gritty and we should have some of that in adult, adult doesn't mean it can't be fun.

The ending was... partially predictable, partially too neatly wrapped in a bow, but I don't care, because it was satisfying, and I didn't expect anything less from a romantasy.

I had a great time with Reap & Sow and I can only ask: Red Skies when?

Kit has no real prejudices to overcome with her, nothing to learn himself. He's not tasked with or challenged with the goal of understanding more about humans, because of course, hes lived in the human world and already reformed- and hes just grumpy because of family politics in the demon world that have made him mistrusting.

The gothic energy is also sucked out of the room the moment Kit appears. There were other characters I liked as well. Eda's brothers had potential, and I really liked the honorable lady knight and would have loved for this book to be from her perspective instead. Imagine the head guard of the religious order or whatever having to team up with a demon to take down a corrupt religious figure?

I think most of my issues come from Eda. I know that the main character has to start somewhere that leaves room for them to grow, but something about her grated on me and it never quite went away.
