What the hell did I just spend my weekend reading?!?

Here I was, in my naïveté, thinking that we would at least get a HEA for Galantia, Sebian and Malyr...

What I got was:
❌ Sebian's death
❌ FMC gang-raped
❌ FMC raped by Malyr
❌ Lorn
❌ Messy world-building
❌ Terrible pacing
❌ No epilogue

My thoughts on this book and indeed this duet are going to be messy. I have never felt so duped by an author in years.

❥ Sebian's death
I was not expecting what happened toward the end.

I had to go back and look at the book description because it clearly states that: "The two-part story features two morally gray raven shifters, a young woman at the mercy of their shadows, and a HEA".

WHERE. WAS. THE. HAPPILY. EVER. AFTER!?

It made no sense to kill Sebian after establishing him as a main character, building his background and developing a solid, deep and meaningful relationship with the FMC, for it all to be thrown out the window at the end of the book. I honestly thought he was going to turn out to be another mate for Galantia until the bomb dropped that he already had one.

❥ Kidnapping
I cannot understand why Galantia had to be kidnapped and gang-raped near the end of the book. This is where the pacing comes into play - if the situation with Lorn had not have been left to the last minute to deal with, this would have played out better near the beginning of the book.

Everything about it was rushed, from her being taken, to being on the boat, to the rescue. You didn't even get a chance to process what was happening to Galantia and her reaction was, yeah I'll just endure this and forget about it. Wtf?

❥ Malyr rapes Galantia (again)
I am convinced this author has a rape fetish. There was, again, no earthly reason why Malyr needed to rape Galantia. Every time they took 1 step forward they took 5 steps back. It was exhausting and frustrating to read as their relationship never really developed enough for the reader to be convinced that they would be successful in their mate-bond. The lack of an epilogue didn't help matters either.

The excuse/justification for the rape in this book is Malyr's shadows and that he had no agency over them to stop the rape from happening as only 1/3 of the shadows are his. This was such a flimsy excuse for rape. It was worse in this book than the last one. It was stomach churning.

He also tries to justify the rape through admitting to Galantia that because she was Brisden's daughter, it didn't matter, but now that he knew she was a Raven and wasn't related to his tormentor, he felt shame and regret.

Malyr had a shit ton of trauma to deal with and instead of opening up to Galantia he pushed her away. It was never resolved adequately enough for the reader to gain any pay-off after enduring two books of this torture porn.

❥ Lorn
Malyr's leniency toward her when Galantia's life was on the line in both books, was bizarre and again, really makes the mate trope redundant in this series.

I could not understand how she could have a fated-mate and not give a damn about him. How she didn't feel the supposed "pull" or the "ache" that they are supposed to feel.

Aros' "revenge" felt like an after-thought; he knew that Lorn could've been killed at any point being away from him. He cited Malyr as being the obstacle that stood between him and his mate, yet he waited until she had died to do something about it.

Unfortunately, her demise was left on the long finger and as a result it screwed with the pacing and what led to Galantia's kidnapping was just not believable that close to the end of the last book.

❥ World-building
The mates issue was tackled to a degree, but there was so much info-dumping around it that I was still left scratching my head. If you have a fated-mate and you know who they are, you can fuck around. If you are mate-bonded, you can still fuck around. It took away the importance of the mate-bond and made it utterly disposable here.

I would have liked to get some more history on the ravens and where they came from. If there were any other shape-shifting paranormals in this world and a clearer background on human/raven relations. Malyr hates the humans even though he himself is part human. The ravens were just as bad as the humans in book one - they committed atrocities and raped too, but that seemed to get glossed over here.

❥ Pacing
I have mentioned it already, but overall, the execution of the pacing messed this story up. Too much happened in the last 1/3 of the book to make any of it believable. The reader was dragged along on the guarantee of a HEA and instead got absolute mayhem with no satisfying conclusion to end the duet.

❥ Epilogue
Not having an epilogue did further damage to Malyr and Galantia's relationship due to the abrupt ending. Their relationship development became very rushed thanks to the terrible pacing; an epilogue based in the future would have saved it, I think. It would have given the reader somewhat of a pay-off after killing a major character, who was central to Malyr and Galantia's relationship.

Verdict: A messy end to a chaotic book that had Zander's fixation on torture porn overshadow any meaningful plot progression or relationship growth between Galantia and Malyr. The author gaslit the reader by promising a HEA with two raven shifters only to snatch it away by killing off a main character. The pacing was a disaster and only added to the tumultuous nature of the book and left me feeling like the story was unfinished.

Dark romance is a fine line of indulging in the darkest fantasies while maintaining the agency of the main character. It is also the line between dark experiences that result in growth, and gratuitous misery for the sake of shock value.

In book one, the author walked this line better. In book two, she trips back and forth all over the line, and the messages it’s sending don’t seem positive or empowering.

Are we really comfortable with the main character being forced into gratuitously violent situations with seemingly the sole intent of her being able to say “I’m not letting it control me, so neither should you?” Glossing over a horrific experience with a “can do” attitude and then acting like she has the grounds to determine how her partner should react to his own?

Do we really not see the difference between allowing someone to use the dark desires to cope with and process their trauma, and FORCING someone to just because you think it’s best? It’s not empowerment just because it’s the FMC that did it. Especially when it all occurs AFTER the character development, as if this is the ideal place to land….

If the genre of dark romance is going to slide into this space of “anything goes if we label it reclaiming” then I really think we’ve lost the collective plot and they need to be tagged horror and stay out of romance, because it’s lending credence to the criticisms that it’s just glorifying abuse.
