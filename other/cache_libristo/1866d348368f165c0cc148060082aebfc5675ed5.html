Shallow Hill is where souls suffer slow and miserable deaths.

When River McAllister escaped from Shallow Hill, she left her innocence behind--and some of her sanity, too. Now she's rebuilding her life with her dream man and life couldn't be better.

Ryan Fitzgerald is everything you'd want in a man. Attractive, rich, an aspiring lawyer, and would do anything to keep River. But that's not the only thing that attracts her. It's his attentiveness, his possessiveness... maybe not so much his temper.

When her relationship takes a dark turn, Ryan's estranged brother, Mako, is the only one who sees it. He knows exactly what Ryan is capable of, and it's not the first time he had to be a knight in shining armor.

River came from Shallow Hill, though. She's never needed saving before. What Mako doesn't see, is that River is perfectly capable of taking care of herself.

The only one who should be scared is Ryan.


Trigger Warning: Shallow River is a dark romance that contains domestic violence, rape, violence, and sexual situations.

⭐️⭐️⭐️⭐️¼ — 4,25/5 📚🚨🩵

This book frustrated me, hurt me, surprised me, and somehow still managed to leave me with a smile by the end. 🤧

Going into Shallow River, I wasn’t expecting this to become one of my favorite H.D. Carlton books. I’ve read four of her books before this one, and only one had managed to reach 3 stars for me, while the others simply didn’t work for me. So I started this with cautious curiosity rather than high expectations.

I’m very happy to admit that this was the book that changed my mind.

Not because it was perfect.

Because it made me feel. ✨

✦ what lies beneath the surface ✦

This isn’t the kind of story that relies on shocking moments alone. It’s uncomfortable in a much deeper way.

This story doesn’t ask, “Why doesn’t she leave?”

It quietly shows you why leaving can feel impossible. 💔

The abuse isn’t presented as a series of isolated moments. It builds slowly, piece by piece, until you can see how someone’s entire world can become smaller without them even realizing it. Every apology, every excuse, and every promise that things will be different becomes another thread keeping River tied to a life she never deserved.

It was exhausting.

It was heartbreaking.

And it felt frighteningly believable. 😞

There were moments where I had to stop reading—not because the book was too graphic, but because the emotional weight of River’s situation felt painfully real.

The psychological side of this story was, for me, its strongest element.

⸻

River tested my patience.

A lot. 😩

There were moments when I wanted to scream at her choices and beg her to stop giving chances to someone who had already shown her exactly who he was.

But frustration isn’t the same as dislike.

I never hated her.

Because every decision she made reflected the damage she had been carrying, not a flaw in who she was. It’s easy to judge someone from the outside when you aren’t the one whose confidence and sense of reality have been slowly worn down.

My frustration came from caring about her. 🥺

I wanted better for her long before she believed she deserved better herself.

And then there’s Mako Fitzgerald.

What stood out about him was that he never felt like a character written only to fix what came before. He felt like his own person, with a quiet strength that made his presence meaningful.

Sometimes the characters who don’t need to prove themselves are the ones you remember the most. ✨

⸻

✦ what made it different ✦

The most powerful part of their relationship was the contrast it created.

After seeing River’s life shaped by fear and uncertainty, watching her experience something completely different gave every small moment between them more meaning.

The romance didn’t erase the past. 🥹

It didn’t pretend that everything could be solved overnight.

Instead, it became a reminder that not every connection has to leave you feeling smaller.

That was what made their relationship stand out to me. 🫶🏻

✦ what remained ✦

This wasn’t an easy read.

It made me angry.
It made me uncomfortable.
It made me want to scream at certain characters more times than I can count. 😤

But it also reminded me that stories about survival don’t need to be beautiful to be powerful.

They just need to be honest.

Shallow River isn’t flawless, and there were definitely choices that kept it from reaching a higher rating for me. Yet despite all the frustration it caused me, I finished it genuinely caring about these characters and feeling invested in where they ended up.

Considering my previous experiences with H.D. Carlton, this ended up being the surprise I wasn’t expecting.

It didn’t make me love every choice the story made.

But it absolutely made me care—and sometimes, that’s the hardest thing a book can accomplish.

My biggest issue? The characters and the plot had absolutely zero chemistry. Honestly, separate them and let each find a better match. I think we’d all be happier. 😵‍💫

The plot had so much potential. The heroine was stuck in a volatile relationship and through the chaos, formed a connection with the hero. Sprinkle in her complicated past, the trauma she carried, and the people she just couldn't seem to escape, and it could have been the perfect plot recipe…But I had issues…

Problem numero uno: the Man, the hero, the disappointment. Mako had been introduced as the heroine’s rescuer, but who really was this man? Honestly, don’t ask me because I had no f*cking idea.

At first, he was giving off major Hawke vibes: broody, dark-haired, mysterious bad-boy energy. WE LOVE. Then suddenly he flipped the script and became this goody, saviour detective, and I struggled to figure out who he actually was. I knew he was empathetic and genuinely cared about the heroine and everything she was going through (more on that later), but his personality was hard to pin down.

I probably would have been happy with the story staying in the FMC’s POV and letting Mako remain…silent. Because honestly, he just wasn’t giving me much of anything.

River, on the other hand, couldn’t give any more if she tried!!! I knew this book was going to be trigger central, but I still was not prepared for just how intense it became.

I had expected the physical and sexual violence the heroine experienced to be the hardest part to read, but the psychological abuse ended up gutting me. Watching the villain slowly manipulate River into believing she was the problem. The way he withheld affection, slipped in those little comments designed to tear her down, and slowly chipped away at her confidence made me want to punch him so hard in 🍆

The worst part was that River couldn’t see what was happening to her. Watching her question herself, lose pieces of who she was, and slowly unravel had made the whole thing even more painful. The book handled triggers really well.

Okay, back to the negatives 🫣

Problem numero dos: the plot felt like a never-ending time loop 🔄 I understand the heroine being so heavily gaslit and manipulated that she genuinely couldn’t see the situation clearly, and I get why walking away was not as simple as it seemed.

What confused me was the way the decision-making had been structured throughout the chapters. One chapter could end with her realising she had made a mistake, promising herself she was done, and preparing to become a revenge-seeking badass. Then the next chapter would start with her talking about them having sex and almost forgetting everything that had happened before.

The emotional whiplash had been hard to keep up with. The constant back and forth was clearly meant to show her confusion, and I understood what the author was trying to do. But the changes happened so quickly that instead of feeling like a realistic internal battle, it started to feel like I was watching a completely different version of her every chapter.

I am fine with not always knowing where a character’s emotions are going, but I need to at least understand what is happening with the plot while I’m reading. Nothing was actually moving forward aside from the occasional mentions of the ghost killer storyline.

This became a DNF for me before I even reached the halfway point. I really did like River in this book, and I think her storyline had a lot of potential, but I just was not getting much from Mako beyond the fact that he was “nice”. Every time he appeared in a chapter, my reaction was basically, “F*ck me, why are you back again” Not the best sign.

(FYI: I’ve been deep in the trenches of a book slump for the last three weeks, and I cannot seem to shake it. So if anyone has any favourite books (any tropes) that might save me, please send them my way 🙏)
