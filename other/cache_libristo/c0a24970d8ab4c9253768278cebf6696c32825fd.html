An antimeme is an idea with self-censoring properties; an idea which, by its intrinsic nature, discourages or prevents people from spreading it. Antimemes are real. Think of any piece of information which you wouldn't share with anybody, like passwords, taboos and dirty secrets. Or any piece of information which would be difficult to share even if you complex equations, very boring passages of text, large blocks of random numbers, and dreams... But anomalous antimemes are another matter entirely. How do you contain something you can't record or remember? How do you fight a war against an enemy with effortless, perfect camouflage, when you can never even know that you're at war? Welcome to the Antimemetics Division. No, this is not your first day.

What did I just read? Why was it so terrifying but also so terrifyingly good? You will be asking these questions after diving into There Is No Antimemetics Division, along with other good questions like, What is reality? Can I trust my own mind? and How the heck did this author pack so many wild ideas into one book? It's an absolutely fascinating and riveting work of speculative fiction.

The basic premise, if anything can be called 'basic' about the novel: A secret Organization has been set up to study and contain anomalies in reality -- call them memes, monsters, aliens, artifacts, intellectual viruses -- thousands of which are all around us but imperceptible to most humans. These memes can be gigantic dinosaurs, skyscraper-sized monoliths, hallucinatory vampires that eat your existence, or dangerous ideas that overwrite human reason. Memes are carefully shielded by a sort of anti-perception camouflage. If you look at one, you think "What is that??" and then look away and immediately forget what you saw. If you try to take notes, the notes vanish. Try to record the phenomenon, and the footage is instantly erased. Some memes are murderous, some apocalyptic, some merely curious, like an ancient entity trapped in a stone who claims to be an aboriginal god of forgetting to learn how to ride a bicycle. (That chapter is worth the price of admission all by itself).

As we get into the novel and meet the intrepid agents of the Antimemetics Division, we quickly realize how dangerous their work is. One mistake, and you could be erased from reality. No one will even remember you existed. Take too many mnestic drugs to let you see the dangerous memes, and best case scenario, you'll get pancreatic cancer. Worst case scenario, your brain will turn to sludge. It's a tough job on any given day. Unfortunately, the division is beginning to realize that they've been fighting a war with a mega-dangerous meme that threatens to turn all of human civilization into a living nightmare, and this war has been going on for decades, perhaps centuries. The meme is so dangerous that it will kill and erase anyone who becomes aware of it. This means the division has to work in specially shielded labs to even discuss the problem, erasing their memories every time they leave, so they are not even aware of the problem or the war most of the time. And as they work, the meme is slowly hollowing out the organization and the world. Like I said, terrifying.

The novel was written in installments for periodic publication, which is why each chapter is so tightly crafted and elegantly handled -- each a self-contained mini-mystery / dark adventure that adds to the overall story. I flew through the whole book in a few sittings and still can't stop thinking about all the implications. If you like big mind-blowing ideas told in a compelling, page-turning story that will make you rethink the nature of sanity, a la The Twilight Zone, this will delight you.

Partly thanks to its origins on the web via the SCP Foundation project, the story is told in a series of vignettes, requiring the reader to deduce and piece together the whole story in their mind (until they can dimly perceive the vast outlines of...)

The text is philosophically interesting and narratively engaging. The horror will hit you in the guts, but also eat away slowly at your mind as you puzzle away at the implications (Thomas Ligotti retire binch!)

To say this is just typical of a qntm production is only to point out that he's consistently excellent.

It's only 200-odd pages long and a couple of quid on Google Play. In terms of various ratios (enjoyment/page, enjoyment/price, mindblows/page) this is probably the best book I've read this year. Go buy it!

I understood everything and nothing about this book at the same time. It was, without a doubt, the greatest love story where the end is a bittersweet reminder that humanity will forget everything that they need to forget, even sacrifices.

Honestly, I'm at a loss for what to tell you about this book because the best way to experience it is to go in as blind as possible and immerse yourself in the gradual descent into madness. The timeline in this story is all over the place, with no specific dates provided for each scene. You will find yourself constantly questioning when each event took place, whether it's before or after, whether the character involved is still alive or has already died, and whether we are reading about memories of the deceased or something else entirely. These questions will surely plague your mind as you read.

The way the horror element intensifies as you learn more about the so-called 'entities' that exist within this universe is something I will never forget. As the characters uncover more about the danger, your reaction will inevitably be one of dread (please, don't remember, don't discover it again) yet, paradoxically, the tragedy repeats itself over and over, darker each time. You'll definitely feel like you're slowly losing your grip on your own mind, with memories becoming so tangled and distorted that you'll struggle to understand what's really happening.

This is a sci-fi horror story that explores the terrifying question: what happens when the monster you must fight is invisible, and you're required to forget it entirely? The story offers a stark and clear answer to this unsettling dilemma.

In conclusion, I love Marion and I love Adam, and that's all I need to say. I’m giving it six stars because it was that good.
