A THRILLING MAGICAL NIGHTMARE.

Two sexy, scheming sisters. Disapproving wealthy, in-laws. And a mysterious family jewel that everyone’s dying to get their hands on.Happily married Wendy and Kesh are shopping for an Indian family festival when an old flame runs over and straddles him in the aisle. She’s gorgeous, charming, and one of two sisters who will soon sink their claws into the hearts of everyone in the Muthu family. Can Kesh withstand temptation? Will Wendy forgive him if he can’t? And if things take a sinister turn, will their marriage—and family—survive?

A dark and dazzling debut, Dear to Saturn liberates a romantic trope with a folklore-filled, surprisingly spiritual, tender and terrifying tale of love and lust that is both epic and deeply personal, propelled by a plot that becomes so enthralling the pages practically turn themselves.

If you are checking out this book, there is a good chance it is because of the viral Instagram videos of the “green flag guy” bawling his eyes out while reading it. After seeing multiple videos of the same guy completely melting over this book, something felt suspicious to me. I did some research and found out that the guy is actually the author himself.

That may be a marketing strategy, so do with that information whatever you want. However, after reading the book, I can say that I found it mediocre, and it definitely did not inspire the kind of emotional reaction that the author, or the other probably staged readers, were having.

Now, let me be more objective and talk about the actual writing.

The writing is very basic and, honestly, nothing special. One thing that stood out to me immediately was the excessive use of em dashes. There seemed to be one or two on practically every page. And do you know who loves using those? AI!

I do not have any concrete evidence that this book was written using AI, and there are certainly readers who are much better than me at identifying AI generated writing. But even if AI had nothing to do with it, the book desperately needed a better editor. A proper proofread would not have hurt either, especially when you have mistakes like this:

“You have want you wanted. Why—why torment us anymore?”
I assume “want” was not exactly what was wanted there.

The story itself did not feel particularly special either. I saw a lot of comments from people saying they had never read anything like it before, but as someone who has read 1400+ books, let me tell you that I have. And I have read much better versions of similar ideas. I have even seen comparable storylines in cartoons and anime I watched as a kid (Which is probably not the benchmark a supposedly revolutionary novel wants to be compared to.)

One of the viral videos claims that page 148 is so shocking that the reader literally threw the book away. I can tell you that I saw that twist coming about ten pages into the story. To be fair to the author, though, there was a later plot twist that I actually thought was good. In fact, that twist is pretty much the sole reason I am not giving this book one star.

The characters were also quite two dimensional and often felt like they were simply acting according to a script. Some of this can be excused because of the circumstances they were placed in, but some of it was simply the result of weak writing.

I was also expecting much more from the “Indian inspired” aspect of the story. Unfortunately, the Indian inspiration seemed to begin and end with some women wearing saris. There was very little that felt particularly Indian about the story. I was hoping for more cultural elements, atmosphere, or anything that would make the setting feel genuinely inspired by India. Unfortunately, I did not get much of that.

So, to summarize, this book was not nearly as good as the hype around it suggests. The author mentions at the beginning that he wrote it because he could not find anyone willing to read his screenplays. After reading the book, I could not help but think that maybe he should have stuck with the screenplays.

The story had potential, but to me it felt much more like a first draft than a finished novel. If you are like me and picked this up because those viral videos made you curious, maybe think twice before spending your time on it. There are plenty of better books out there.

It succeeds more often than it fails, especially in how honestly it deals with infertility and in how willing it is to let characters stay messy and unresolved. The supernatural twist genuinely changes how you see the first half of the book, and the Saturn interludes give you a really unique picture of divine patience and humor and this has to be my favorite thing about the book.

Or someone who takes care of everyone and has to sacrifice everything in order to appeal to her man and society. This book had a good premise to become something of a stand on this. Like, she can still be a generous and karma-positive woman by saving the family who mistreated her but also putting her own wellbeing as a priority and leave. Genuinely. She never wanted the jewel, she wanted to make her husband happy and for that she needed to make his family like her. There was no reason for her to want to stay. Literally everyone hated her.

This is so twisted and genuinely feels like a slap in the face.

Women are finally divorcing those men who cannot bother to even like them and good for them, honestly. And, yes, it is a flex if you are able to divorce and break the toxic cycles. So many women end up being unalived and harassed for a better amount of time afterwards. You should not stay in a situation that makes you sad and miserable. Sure, trying to mend a relationship is not wrong. Like, even the sister-in-law agreed that Wendy is always sad and that she suffers. Like, hello? So many women go through this abuse and there is no spiritual being helping them. They die feeling terrible, alone and less than a living being with emotions. The scene where she refused to go the hospital when she was dying? It should have been a genuine wake up call. Especially because her mother in law found her, dismissed her absolute horrid state and not even her husband (poisoned or not) bothered asking her how she was doing or if they should find a doctor. Even in this moment, she was hoping to be finally pregnant. This is insane and poor her. But this is what I mean. In that moment, any clear thinking person would have upped and left. This is not normal, nor is it okay and this falls beyond "fixing a marriage."

Don't get me started on the whole religious pressure and all that either. This is a horrible book and outcome. Imagine being a young girl and you read about a woman suffering to a point where she happily sacrifices herself only to end up being "fulfilled" only because she finally has a kid and did not divorce and forgave her trash husband. This is not real love. It's terrible.

But of course, the man gets everything he wants in the end. Kid, wife, family loves him. And everyone is better. This could have gone somewhere meaningful. Instead, we get the message "Yeah, well, suffer woman! Suffer! And in the end god will hear you and lift you up." All Kesh had to suffer were some comments and disdainful looks aimed at his wife, the "love of his life."

End Word and Writing

So, yeah. I hated this book and the message it comes with. But also the writing felt so juvenile and incomplete. It was quite messy at some points too. It was a general hard read even for only 200 pages.

The author's acknowledgements told me all that I needed to know. He almost names everyone, even his son, and gives them a thanks and a few lines. His wife gets a one liner and in the end he thanks Jesus. A whole paragraph. He says this book is like written for/by him and yeah. It told me all I needed to know as to why we are degrading and dismissing women in this book so much.

It was not my cup of tea and if it was yours, congrats.

It left me raging and disgusted. If you need a rage read, then maybe this is for you.

I really wanted to love Dear to Saturn more than I did, but unfortunately, it didn’t quite work for me.
The first half was genuinely a 5-star read. I was invested in the tension, the family drama, and all the uncomfortable dynamics. There was a constant sense that something was about to go terribly wrong, and I was completely on board. I was especially interested in the story when it felt like a thriller—full of secrets, suspicion, interpersonal tension, and drama.
Unfortunately, once the fantasy elements became more prominent, my enjoyment started to drop. The introduction of the demoness/fantasy aspect took the story in a direction I wasn't expecting or particularly interested in. By around 70%, I was down to about 2.5 stars.
I also have to mention the marketing, because I think it heavily influenced my expectations. This was presented to me as a romantasy, with the promise of something painful, emotional. I honestly didn't get that experience. There are romantic relationships in the story, but I wouldn't describe romance as a major part of the book, and I never felt particularly attached to the characters or emotionally devastated by what was happening. I kept waiting for that big emotional impact that the marketing seemed to promise, but it never really came.
The interlude was another difficult part for me. It felt much too long, overly philosophical, and, ultimately, boring. I understand what the author was trying to explore with it, but it pulled me out of the story rather than deepening my connection to it.
Ironically, I think I would have enjoyed this book much more if it had stayed a thriller. Give me all the family tension, secrets, manipulation, suspense, and drama from the first half without taking the story so heavily into the fantasy/philosophical side, and I probably would have been much more invested.

I started at 5 stars, dropped to 4 when the fantasy took over, and ended somewhere around 2.5–3 stars. Ultimately, it just wasn't the book I thought I was getting.
