The Curse of the Fates #1

The Washwoman at the River's End

Megan Shade

4.39

Rate this book
A stunningly haunting gothic romantasy inspired by the Scottish legend of the Washerwoman, following an immortal woman cursed to wash the grave clothes of the dying for eternity and the man blood-sworn to kill her. Perfect for readers who love The Knight and the Moth, One Dark Window, and the Scottish atmosphere of Outlander.


They call her a monster.
A murderess.
But legends rarely tell the whole truth...

For over a century, Bea has haunted the River’s End. Forced to eternally wash the grave clothes of the dying, she spends her days searching the bloodstained garments she cleanses for the pieces of a spell that could finally break her curse and allow her to go to her rest. But Bea’s search is interrupted when the river brings her a warning:

He is coming.

Brennen knows the Washwoman’s tale all too well—the monster who murdered the woman he loved and left him with nothing but an unbreakable vow of vengeance. So when he’s assigned as her guard, he believes the Fates have finally blessed him. But the woman he finds at the River's End is nothing like the monster from her legend.

When women across the kingdom begin to vanish and mysterious deaths follow—deaths only Bea can foresee—Brennen is forced into an uneasy alliance with the very woman he’s spent years hunting. But as they search for answers, hatred twists into reluctant trust, and trust into something far more desire.

With fate closing in and time running out, Brennen must decide if he will damn his soul to save the woman he swore to kill, while Bea must choose between the rest she once longed for and the love that could destroy them both.

A gothic fantasy with Scottish folklore, a curse, revenge and a slow-burn romance. This was such an interesting story, and Megan Shade’s beautiful writing really brought it all to life.

Bea is the Washwoman, cursed to spend eternity washing the clothes of the dead so they can move on to the afterlife. She’s been trapped there for 100 years, and the way she’s treated by others is awful. She’s abused, called names and hurt, yet she keeps going. She’s immortal, but that doesn’t mean she hasn’t suffered. I really felt for Bea because she’s judged so harshly when there’s so much more to her than people see.

Then there’s Brennen, who turns up determined to kill her because he believes she murdered the woman he loved. He’s looking for revenge and expects to find a monster, but spending time with Bea changes things in ways he never expected.

And this is where things get complicated! Brennen is carrying so much grief and guilt over the woman he lost, and now he’s developing feelings for Bea, the very woman he believes is responsible for her death. He’s torn between what he came to do and what he’s actually feeling. You can see how much he’s struggling with it, and I found that part of their relationship really interesting.

The romance is a slow burn, with plenty of emotional conflict along the way. I enjoyed watching their relationship change as Brennen started seeing Bea for who she really was rather than the monster he’d imagined. His feelings are the last thing he expected, and that makes their situation even more complicated.

The writing is beautiful, and I loved how the Scottish folklore was woven into the story. The pacing is a little slow in places, but the characters and storyline kept me interested, and I wanted to see how everything would turn out for Bea and Brennen.

There’s a lot of hurt, grief, guilt and revenge in this story, but also the question of how well we really know someone when we’ve already decided who they are.

Overall, I enjoyed this one. Beautiful writing, great characters and a fascinating storyline, with a gothic feel and a romance that definitely doesn’t make things easy for itself!

“…it takes integrity to separate justice from cruelty.”

This story pulled me in immediately with the premise, the gothic vibes, and the beautiful cover. There shall be no questions on the importance of aesthetics in my book choices. Due to the way the plot progresses, adding anything to my pre-read (below) in my opinion would spoil too much, so I’ll just dive into my thoughts.

Bea, the Washwoman: she is somewhat an enigma in the romantasy world. While she is not filled with feminine rage or anger, neither is she a damsel in distress or a woman whose convictions are easily swayed. There are times when it’s easy to think that she is a believer in her own punishment, but as the story progresses it is clear that her actions stem from a sense of empathy it’s difficult to imagine a murderer possessing.

Brennen the soldier: a man who subscribes to the theory that justice is black and white. His belief in Bea’s monstrous reputation and the murder of his former betrothed has him signing up for guard duty with a side of assassination faster than you can say “tha mi ’gad mhallachadh” (Scottish for “I curse you”). Thank goodness there’s a glossary in the back for all the pronunciations. But Bea challenges every preconceived notion he’s ever harbored.

And that’s one of the aspects I really enjoyed about this story. It takes a look at prejudice from different sides. One side shows the repugnant cruelty that some will justify based on nothing more than hearsay or reputation. The other asks what it actually means to be a bad person, and what do people who do bad things actually deserve? Is redemption possible and can the present ever atone for the past?

This story may be slow for some, since there isn’t a lot of action until the end. But I was completely hooked. Watching Brennan slowly start to question his own beliefs as he got to know Bea, trying to figure out the mystery behind her murderous history, as well as the mystery behind women who are disappearing all over the kingdom had me clutching my kindle like I was on trial. The pacing between the suspense and the character development kept the momentum at peak levels for me.

And once these two started to trust each other? Let’s just say a gruff Scottish man in a kilt and the forbidden touch trope is an approved recipe.

I’ll also say that while the final revelations in the end are not easily guessed, there are plenty of clues sprinkled through the story. There are quite a few bombshells that pushed this story into more traditional romantasy territory at end, but I’m not mad about it. I’m reserving my final thoughts until I see where the next book takes us. Which I’ll be ordering as soon as it’s announced.

I think my biggest disappointment was the setting. We are supposed to be in Scotland, but the entire book takes place in a river bed, or a couple scenes in a tavern. The only thing Scottish about this story is the MMC's accent, and the mentioning of kilts on 2 pages, and some historical folklore names. There is so much more to Scotland than an accent and a kilt, the setting of Scotland itself is otherworldly and mysterious but all we get its a rocky riverbed.

Whimsical, cozy and a little heartbreaking. This is the story of Bea, a woman cursed to wash the clothes of the deceased at the river’s end, and Brennen, a man who swore to kill her. The main themes are loss and forgiveness.

The pacing is slow and not very action-packed, and I also began reading at a snail’s pace, which did not help. Luckily the chapters are short, so I eventually picked up speed. After all, what’s another 5 minutes? 8 minutes? (I read on Kindle)

Bea is cursed to the river and cannot (or should I say “cannae”?) leave, so most of the story takes place in one location. That makes the world feel small, but not in a bad way. I really liked the world that was built here. It’s beautiful, cozy and magical.

I started out disliking Brennen (yes, there is a brooding man in this book), but he slowly grew on me, and in the end I really liked him. I liked Bea immediately. She doesn’t kick ass, but she tries to do the right thing and stays kind even though she has been through so much. A nice break from your typical FMC.

The story is shrouded in mystery, and I found myself making notes and hunting for clues like it was a murder mystery. It was hard to guess where things were headed, which I appreciated. I did get a couple of things right, but the big plot twists near the end I did not see coming at all.

All in all, I enjoyed this book but wasn’t blown away by it. It’s a bit overhyped, but still a very good slow-paced read and a nice palate cleanser from your typical Romantasy.

Pre-read Going in blind. All I know is that it’s a gothic tale set in Scotland and everyone is raving about it. I’m expecting at least one brooding man.
