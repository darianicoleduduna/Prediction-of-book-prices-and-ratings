— 0.5/5 stars

Okay, I genuinely don’t even know where to begin with this absolute trainwreck because this book put me into the nastiest reading slump imaginable. Like not just a slump, a shit-shit slump. The kind where you stare at your bookshelf wondering if you even enjoy reading anymore. That’s how painful this experience was for me.

First of all, WHY did this 350-page book feel like it was 3000 pages long? I swear this book just kept dragging and dragging and dragging to the point where I thought I was trapped in some kind of literary purgatory. Every chapter felt never-ending. Every scene overstayed its welcome. I’m not even exaggerating when I say this book consumed my soul, and not in the sexy “this book ruined me emotionally” way. No. This consumed me in the “oh my God PLEASE let this end” kind of way.

It literally took me ages to get through 20% of the book. TWENTY. And once I crossed halfway, I still felt like absolutely nothing had happened. It was exhausting.

And Ana Huang… babe. Please. I am begging you to switch the formula up because WHY are we reading the exact same fucking story for the sixth time in a row?

It’s always the same copy-paste couple with different names slapped onto them. The man is some emotionally constipated alpha billionaire who acts like smiling would physically kill him. The girl is tiny, fragile, “sassy,” bad b who thinks world revolve around her and then we get the same recycled cycle:

• They’ve secretly loved each other forever.

• They’re both in denial.

• The guy suddenly realizes “life is short🥀” and decides to do ONE decent thing.

• The girl instantly folds.

• Then comes the dumbest third-act breakup known to mankind over a misunderstanding that could’ve been solved in literally five fucking minutes if these grown adults knew how to communicate.

And OH MY GOD the unnecessary drama. The drama wasn’t even entertaining, it was just annoying. Every conflict felt forced, exaggerated, and thrown in there purely so the book could stretch itself another hundred pages longer. Like please, enough. I am tired.

And the worst part? I wanted to enjoy this so badly. I really did. But instead I felt like I was reading the same damn book over and over again with different character names and different hair colors.

For the love of fucking God, can we PLEASE get a new storyline now?


➳ABOUT THE BOOK

So i genuinely want to why did Ana literally throw every single trope that trends on BookTok and Instagram Reels into this book and call it a day? Like genuinely, it felt less like reading a romance novel and more like scrolling through a “top romance tropes you NEED to read ” compilation.

Emotionally constipated billionaire man?
Check.

Tiny, fragile girl who acts all fierce but melts the second the man breathes near her?
Check.

Enemies to lovers? Apparently check, even though these people barely acted like enemies for five fucking minutes.

Obsessed male lead?
Check.

Did he actually show the obsession in a convincing way? Absolutely the fuck not.

And PLEASE don’t even get me started on the “yearning.” WHERE was the yearning we were promised? Where?? Was it invisible? Did it evaporate? Was I supposed to hallucinate it myself? Because I sat there waiting for the tension, the aching, the desperation, the “I want her but can’t have her” energy and got absolutely nothing. These people weren’t yearning. They were just existing and occasionally staring at each other dramatically.

And then suddenly the man wakes up one random Tuesday and goes, “Oh no… life is short… people die… maybe I should confess my love.” Sir be fucking serious.

At this point Ana Huang has a formula and she is STICKING to it for dear life.

And the smut… oh my God. THE SMUT. I genuinely had to skim through half of it because it was physically painful to read. I’m not even exaggerating when I say I was sitting there cringing so hard I had to put my phone down and stare at the wall. Some of those lines had me fighting for my life. Like Ana PLEASE, I am begging you, free us from whatever the fuck that was.

Also can we PLEASE stop bringing up Christian Harper and Alex Volkov every two business seconds? Girl, we GET IT. They exist. They were iconic once upon a time. But every single book does not need to remind us that they breathe oxygen. It’s starting to feel like Marvel cameos at this point.

And now we have another book lined up about Killian and honestly? I haven’t even read it yet and somehow I already know exactly what’s going to happen. Same emotionally unavailable billionaire. Same recycled tension. Same “touch her and die” energy. Same dramatic breakup. Same happy ending wrapped in expensive suits and trauma.

Like I’m sorry but at some point the tropes stop being fun and start feeling like copy-paste fanfiction with better marketing.

➳ABOUT THE CHARACTERS

Okay, but can we PLEASE talk about how every single character in this book felt absolutely fucking soulless? Like genuinely, it was impossible to connect with any of them. I kept waiting for at least ONE person to make me feel something other than irritation, but nope. Nothing. Zero. Zilch. I hated them all equally. Actually no, maybe I hated Maya more than Sebastian. Or maybe Sebastian more than Maya. Honestly, I’m confused because the rage I felt toward both of them was genuinely astronomical.

And Ana… babe… PLEASE do proper research before writing about somebody else’s culture because what the actual fuck was that?

That dosa line pissed me off so bad I had to physically put the book down. NOBODY and I mean absolutely nobody, I have ever known eats dosa like that. Why was dosa being treated like some crepe?? It’s DOSA. You eat it with sambar and chutney, not strawberries and whatever cursed combination that was. I was genuinely sitting there wondering if this woman has ever even SEEN a dosa in real life because what the fuck do you mean by that scene?

And don’t even get me started on the Indian representation because OH. MY. GOD.

It felt so surface-level and stereotypical that it genuinely annoyed me. Maya Singh is supposedly Indian, right? Okay cool. Then where was the actual culture? Where was the depth? Where was literally ANYTHING that felt authentic? If she was meant to have a Punjabi upbringing as she was a SINGH also i could be assuming that but then SHOW IT. Because all we got was vague “strict family,” marriage pressure, and random stereotypical references thrown around like confetti.

And why were these women acting like marriage is the only fucking purpose of life? The whole “you’ll end up like Meera auntie” thing pissed me off SO BAD. As if not getting married automatically makes a woman some tragic cautionary tale? That woman literally did nothing wrong except exist unmarried and somehow the book kept using her like a warning sign. That’s insane to me.

Yes, marriage is important in many Indian families, but our ENTIRE lives do not revolve around finding a husband and producing babies. Indian women are actual human beings with ambitions, dreams, personalities, careers, and lives outside shaadi conversations. The way this book portrayed Indian culture felt so westernized and watered down that it genuinely made me uncomfortable.

And listen, I’m not saying authors can’t write outside their culture. Of course they can. But at least PUT EFFORT into understanding it beyond clichés and Google-search-level references. Because this felt less like authentic representation and more like somebody watched two Bollywood movies, saw a Pinterest board titled “Indian aesthetic,” and called it research.

Honestly, the entire thing just felt so shallow and frustrating. The characters were unbearable, the representation felt off, and by the end of it I was more angry than entertained. Which is honestly impressive in the worst possible way.

➳FINAL THOUGHTS

Okay, if it’s still not painfully obvious by now, I absolutely did not like this book. Like genuinely, from the bottom of my exhausted little soul, this book pissed me off in more ways than one. And honestly, as an Indian, it annoyed me even more because what the fuck even was that representation?

First of all, the stereotypical references were driving me INSANE. Why did every Indian woman in this book act like their entire existence revolved around getting married? Like yes, marriage can be important in Indian culture, nobody is denying that, but this book acted like Indian women wake up, breathe, eat, and sleep thinking about shaadi 24/7. We have ambitions, careers, personalities, dreams, actual lives outside of marriage proposals and family gossip, thank you very much.

And the way unmarried women were spoken about?? Oh my God. That pissed me off so bad. It was giving “if you don’t get married your life is automatically tragic” energy and I HATED that. It felt so outdated and shallow. Indian women are not just walking stereotypes whose life purpose is to find a husband before thirty.

And don’t even get me started on the characters because SWEAR TO GOD Ana Huang keeps recycling the same emotional baggage in every single male lead. Every man in this series has some deep dark guilt that apparently stops him from behaving like a normal fucking human being. Like sir, PLEASE. Seek therapy. Journal. Meditate. Do SOMETHING.

And the girls aren’t any better either. They all start off with some unrealistic worldview or emotional issue, and then suddenly ONE incident happens and boom, character development unlocked. No healing journey. No actual growth. No therapy. Nothing. They just magically wake up cured like some emotional software update downloaded overnight.

Everything about this book felt so artificial.

Nothing stood out. Literally nothing.

And honestly? The ONLY thing that made this book remotely tolerable were the narrators in the audiobook. They carried this book on their backs because if I had physically read this instead of listening to it, I genuinely think I would’ve lost my mind. The narration made it bearable enough to finish, because the actual story itself felt never-fucking-ending. I still cannot believe this book was only around 350 pages because WHY did it feel like a lifetime sentence?

At some point I was checking how many pages were left every ten minutes like a prisoner counting days to freedom.

⚘ "Overall, I genuinely would not recommend this book unless you’re already obsessed with this author’s formula and don’t mind reading the same recycled dynamics over and over again. Because me personally? I regret picking this up so bad." 💌
