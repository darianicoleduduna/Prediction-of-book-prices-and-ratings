this is a rant, not a proper review, at least not the kind i usually write. whatever this is, i’m writing it for myself, for my own peace of mind. i don’t have good things to say, so if you’re a die-hard bab fan, maybe don’t read this. and don’t come here telling me i “missed the entire point” of the story. if that’s the case, i’m fine with it. my opinions are my own, and i'm entitled to post it here.

i do believe stories are dynamic and grow with people. when i first started reading this, the premise completely hooked me, but unfortunately that effect gradually fizzled out, especially after the last volume. whatever the author is trying to say, i either don’t understand it or i do understand but simply don’t want to read something like it. and no, nobody is forcing me to read this book. i’m doing this to myself because i don’t value my time and can’t leave anything unfinished.

for so long, i tried in vain to see beyond what is written on these pages. i tried hard to read between the lines, and all i found was one plot hole after another. the entire story feels like a superficial hot mess where shock factors are added purely for the sake of it. it’s as if the author challenged herself and said, “i’ll write the most unhinged book possible.” i’m sorry to say it just didn’t work for me.

rant with heavy spoilers

this volume picks up right where the last one ended. xqc in he yu’s house, getting treatment and atticwifed. and that’s where the never-ending series of absurdities begin. during his stay, xqc steals data from he yu’s devices. now you’re telling me world-class hacker he yu left everything unprotected for his ex-doctor? normal behavior for them, i guess.

then anthony shows up and insults xqc. after seeing the cctv footage, he yu asks him about the encounter because there’s no audio. but later we find out mandela org. has technology that records audio too. couldn’t they lend some of that to he yu for his house surveillance? then chen man enters, and he yu basically goes, “arrest me when homosexuality becomes a crime.” kind of iconic, honestly, but at this point i’m exhausted by whatever is happening.

project dream breaker sends an entire battalion to the island, but that thousands-strong force gets immobilized within seconds. so very naturally, they decide to send xqc, a professor with no military training whatsoever because he has the “power of adaptability.” then superhuman xqc enters with his super hearing alongside zheng jingfeng, an old cop who’s about to retire. couldn't they send someone better? before they leave, the team leader gives xqc a police uniform and an id number made from his dead parents’ badge numbers. highly unethical, but i guess “special circumstances require special solutions.”

on the way to mandela, instead of strategizing, our veteran jingfeng starts telling xqc stories about his parents and their first mission together. is this really the time for that? they’ve known each other their whole lives, but suddenly, right before the most dangerous mission imaginable, grandpa pulls out his bag of tales. dude, conserve your energy.

now for the highlight of the story, combative robots carrying the consciousness of xqc’s dead parents, and they know how to salute properly like policemen. marvelous. our team of two fights for their lives and gathers data that a team of thousands couldn’t. brilliant. those battle scenes gave me chills from disbelief.

then he yu enters the scene riding our star, dear pegasus, which turns out to be a modified helicopter. yeah, i feel cheated. where’s my winged unicorn? at this point the “helicopter helicopter” meme started playing in my head and i could physically feel another headache coming.

i feel like i’m just writing down a summary of this book, so now i’ll skip a few things.

our little devil captures xqc and atticwifes him again, but now we learn that he yu is actually part of project dream breaker, working as an informant. bingo. what a brave 23-year-old silly little dummy. duan wen’s chips and serums apparently don’t work on he yu’s super special blood toxin, yes we have another super man, so he can send intel whenever he wants. extremely convenient for the plot.

to maintain their “enemy” facade, hexie are having sex everyday, while duan wen’s subordinate checks the bedding for 'semen', what a pervert!! also, there are cameras in the bathroom because apparently duan wen loves watching people shit. wonderful. marvelous. life-changing.

and we’re still not done. let’s talk about xqc’s pregnancy. our 36-year-old man injects himself with a special serum for superpowers and somehow ends up with pregnancy symptoms. now he’s throwing up and lactating. truly the pinnacle of fiction. the funniest part is that he yu genuinely believes that his uncle oops lover can actually get pregnant. sir. please. spare me.

oh, and i almost forgot the cameras in he yu’s mandela room can record both audio and video, but apparently the audio only works when people talk loudly. that’s a massive security flaw for a futuristic organization experimenting with all kinds of advanced technology. naturally, our super duper clever 'loid-level' spy hexie exploit this by whispering their plans nonstop.

mandela island itself is actually pretty interesting. we got zoya a maniac scientist raising pigs. what’s next? a mango garden? an apiary producing organic honey for the residents? to top it all, the island even has an ice cream parlor. again, how convenient!

and all this chaos started because the island’s leader, one hag who just refuses to die, keeps transplanting her brain into different bodies (bitch die!). we basically have a fake god chasing immortality. at this point i’ve entirely lost it. even my own thoughts sound absurd to me.

spoilers end here

another very important thing, now we finally know why that organization is so desperate to find the first emperor, and honestly i’m scared for xqc. if they find out, they’ll definitely do unspeakable things to him.

despite all my complaints, i can still acknowledge some good qualities. for example, the entire concept of mandela and this insane organization is trying to reshape the world order, which is pretty interesting. the premise is good, but the execution is not.

you can’t paint with a broad brush because inevitably you’ll miss the finer details. that’s exactly what happened here. the author tried to cram in so many shock factors that the story turned ridiculous. what even is this book? dystopian? sci-fi? thriller? romance? or just something made for unhinged people? it feels like a heterogeneous mixture. the repetition turned beyond redemption. i refuse to believe this was written by the same author who gave us masterpieces like yuwu and erha. i’m no one to tell an author what they should or shouldn’t write, but this story doesn’t even come close to those two epics.

this time i won’t even discuss the so called “romance,” but i will say it’s nice that they’ve finally started communicating with each other. it’s been long enough. please wrap things up already, i see no reason for this unnecessary angst just for the sake of it. eh, i think i’m still forgetting one or two points. when i remember them, i’ll come back again and whine. i'll edit this so-called review.

bottom line, i think meatbun’s grocery list would have more substance than this, and i’d happily read that. anyway, secretly looking forward to the next volume. final verdict, interesting, somewhat overindulging, but still bad.

p.s. he yu and chen man should fuck. i'm rooting for a threesome. ★: 1.75

I don’t even know where to start with this volume because my feelings were all over the place. At first, I honestly thought the whole thing was ridiculous. I kept reading like, what is happening right now and why am I still turning the pages? Then Mandela Island started to make more sense, and I actually got impressed. The idea behind it was strange, unsettling, and way more interesting than I expected. I went from side-eyeing the plot to being curious about what the island really meant for the story. Then that intimate scene happened and I was mindblown. It was so intense and personal, but I was also laughing. LAUGHING!!! I don’t even know how to explain that reaction, but wow, this book is bold.

This volume really does not hold back. It touches certain topics and ideas that made me pause for a second because I couldn’t believe the story actually went there. Some parts felt dramatic to the point of being almost too much, but I also couldn’t look away. I’m exaggerating, of course, but there were moments where it felt like 20% plot and 80% porn. The physical tension is very present here, and sometimes it felt like it swallowed the actual story a little. At the same time, I can’t pretend I wasn’t entertained. The emotional mess between Xie Qingcheng and He Yu still got to me. They frustrate me so much, but I’m invested in them. There is so much guilt, anger, attachment, hurt, and longing between them that every interaction feels heavy. I don’t always agree with their choices, and sometimes I wanted to shake them both, but I still love them.

My biggest issue is how thin this book is. The story feels short, so everything moves fast, and I wanted more room to breathe with the plot and the characters. Just when I felt like things were getting serious, the volume was already ending. And that ending? I am nervous. We still have two volumes left, and I have no idea how I’m supposed to feel calm after this. This was messy, bold, emotional, ridiculous at times, and honestly kind of exhausting, but I was never bored. I didn’t love every part of it, and some scenes were a lot, but I still had fun reading it. It left me stressed, entertained, confused, and curious, which pretty much sums up my whole experience with this series so far.
