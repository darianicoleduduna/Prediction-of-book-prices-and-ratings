I wanted this woman, and I was a man who always got what he wanted.

Nico 'Junior' Trocci knows Lauren Marchetti is off limits. She is sweetness and laughter, whereas Junior exists in a world of violence and depravity. Men like him don't get to have women like her. It's why he pushed her away back in high school and has kept his distance ever since.

But when Junior looks Lauren up online, he discovers the shy, bookish girl he remembers is gone. In her place is a strikingly beautiful woman whose social media is filled with scantily clad pictures of herself and a connection to a nearby play club. Junior's innocent curiosity quickly turns into a dark obsession. But watching Lauren from a distance isn't enough; he needs to make her his.

He's ruthless; a walking red flag. Good thing red is my favorite color.

Lauren is intrigued by the mystery man who keeps showing up at her club, who tempts her white-hot desires. But as their flirty online messages stir up a deeper form of connection, Lauren worries she's developing real feelings for this brooding stranger. As their combined desire ratchets up to a breaking point, who will seduce who? And will they survive the fall?

Caught Up is a fast-paced dark romance with a morally grey male lead. Some themes and scenes may be disturbing to readers. Please check the content warning at the beginning of the book.

The biggest impression I had upon closing this book was... that was a lot. Not of plot or character development. Just a lot of sex and I feel numb.

Look, I like a spicy scene here and there but when nearly the whole book is a montage of sex (and rather explicit, kinky and...unusual scenes), I noticed that my brain actually started disassociating from the book.

Objectively, it was an interesting phenomenon because I've read hundreds of audiobooks but I've never had to work so hard at keeping my attention focused on the book.

But there was such an overwhelming number of explicit scenes that it ceased being fun or spicy, but something I had to slog through to get to the next plot point (which felt few and far between).

The whole Junior/Lauren relationship felt bland to me despitethe frequency of their... scenes.

Basically, he was her top subscriber, she was a top-earner on the OnlyFans equivalent of this world. But there wasn't much depth to their relationship.

He's stalked her for years and she finds it charming. He's obsessed and willing to pay, she's a sex-positive online content creator who happily took all the money he offered.

Something I did find interesting, is that junior says over and over that he is fine with her job and while we do see Lauren "at her job" quite frequently, she's never really shown sexting or filming specific videos for other clients.

It's mentioned that she does and will continue to do that, but the author only focuses on her custom work for junior. So there's this weird dynamic where realistically, junior feels like he's getting this unique experience but he really isn't but the author chooses to only highlight relationship building moments from sexting and whatnot between the two MCs.

Anyway, what's wild is that the first book (Lights Out) had a somewhat similar premise (obsession and being charmed) and yet that one worked so well for me. I thought the banter between the characters was hilarious, the scenes were spicy but exciting, and I liked the push-pull of the relationship.

But Junior and Lauren felt like shadows compared to Josh and Ali.

Honestly, the most interesting thing was Lauren's best friend getting a request to cover herself in food and when the friend was like, "hey, is this weird?" And Lauren's like, "oh he's a cannibal, just ignore it. " and I'm like, "What? WHAT!?" but nothing ever comes of that plot point! I'm not saying one of the characters should've been eaten but why throw that tidbit in there if you aren't going to use it?

With book 1 being so good, and book 2 being rock bottom...I wonder where book 3 will land?

P.s. I don't believe for a second that Junior actually has money. At the rate he was throwing it at Lauren - couple of hundred to thousands a week? Yeah right. He couldn't save up to save his life.

I think the author had an "ohsht" moment where she realized she was making Junior poor cause he spent so much on custom photos and videos and retroactively wrote in that he was uber rich to compensate.

That's my headcanon and you won't ever convince me otherwise.

A Crimson Waltz of Power, Pain, and Passion!

Mafia romances used to thrill me. It’s been a while since I let a mafia dark romance crawl under my skin —less a love story, more a dissertation on inherited violence and the quiet, terrible cost of wanting to be good in a world that never taught you how.
So Caught Up is a dark, erotic, and deeply contemporary romance that interrogates the intersections of trauma, power, and healing. Navessa Allen uses the conventions of the mafia romance and the sex work romcom to subvert expectations, centering consent, agency, and the messy work of forgiveness. The novel is unflinching in its portrayal of violence—both physical and emotional—but refuses to glamorize it, instead showing the cost of generational harm and the courage required to break free. At its heart, the story is about reclaiming narrative: Lauren reclaims her sexuality and her story from shame, while Junior reclaims his future from the grip of his father. Their love is not a panacea but a partnership, built on honesty, mutual respect, and the willingness to do the hard work of healing. The found family at Velvet, the celebration of kink and sex-positivity, and the ultimate triumph over cycles of abuse make Caught Up a powerful meditation on what it means to choose love, again and again, in a world determined to deny it.

🩸 The Premise
What happens when the man you loved kills your father—and then becomes your most loyal subscriber?

Nico “Junior” Trocci was born with a bullet between his teeth and guilt in his bloodstream. The heir to a mafia empire, Junior has done terrible things, but none worse than what he did to Lauren Marchetti. Years later, he worships her anonymously through a camgirl screen, a ghost in her inbox, craving penance through pixels.

Lauren—saint, sinner, survivor—is not the same girl who fell for a boy with a gun. She owns her sexuality now. She sells it. But on her terms. With her girls. With her queerness. With her fire.

They’re not just two people with history. They are an open wound with no stitches.

And when they see each other again? It’s not a love story. It’s a slow-motion trainwreck dressed like a second chance.

🏷️Storyline recap:
Mafia heir Nico “Junior” Trocci gets a dead body for his birthday. Classic. It's just another night of blood, rain, and guilt—until he realizes the man he helped kill was the father of the girl he’s been obsessing over since high school: Lauren Marchetti.

Lauren? She’s not the same girl he left behind. She’s a camgirl now, loud about sex work rights, unashamed, and on fire. She’s got scars, rules, and a one-strike policy. And guess what? Her number-one sub online—the only one who really sees her? Yeah. It’s him.

What follows is a head-on collision of past betrayals, mafia mess, kinky club nights, and way too much tracking (both emotional and literal). Junior wants a second chance. Lauren wants answers. But between groveling, stalking, church confrontations, mafia dinners, and literal blood on the floor… nothing’s clean here.

This isn’t some soft redemption arc. It’s messy, feral, and laced with hurt. But maybe—just maybe—love can crawl out of all that darkness.

In a world of mob violence, sex work stigma, and chosen family, Caught Up Into the Darkness asks: Can love survive when it was born from lies and built in blood?

“She was my match, my queen, and I would spend the rest of my life worshipping her like one if she let me.”

A kink-heavy, obsessive second-chance romance with intense vibes and very little humor.

It didn’t give me kick-my-feet excitement, but I still enjoyed the ride. The duet narration by Teddy Hamilton and Kasi Hollowell helped a lot, and I’m definitely excited for Tyler’s story.

I don’t even know where to begin with this because WHAT. WAS. THIS. 😭 I went into this expecting a dark romance, and not even a mild one—I was fully ready for obsession, chaos, morally grey choices, all of it. I can handle toxic men. I can even love toxic men. But this? This wasn’t dark romance in the addictive, messy, “I can’t look away” kind of way. This was exhausting, uncomfortable, and somehow… painfully boring. And that is honestly the worst crime of all. 🫠

⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘

✧ The Premise (aka the biggest betrayal) ✧

On paper, this book had EVERYTHING going for it. A morally grey man. An off-limits obsession. A second chance dynamic. Dark, dangerous vibes. It should have been irresistible. It should have been intense. It should have had me hooked from page one.

Instead, it felt like nothing ever truly landed. There was no emotional weight, no buildup, no depth to make any of it matter. It just floated there—scene after scene—with no real impact. I kept waiting for something to click, for the story to finally become what it promised. But it never did. 😭

⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘

✧ Nico “Junior” — I CANNOT ✧

I have defended some truly unhinged fictional men in my time. I have read characters who are objectively terrible and still found something compelling in them. Junior, however, tested my patience like nothing else.

He wasn’t layered or complex—he was just frustrating. Arrogant in a way that wasn’t charismatic. Emotionally volatile without any real depth behind it. And the constant repetition of his trauma, especially the “my father” excuse, became so exhausting to read that it started to feel like a loop rather than character development.

There’s a difference between a broken man who grows on you and a character who just stays stagnant and irritating. Junior never crossed that line for me. He didn’t intrigue me, didn’t make me feel anything—except annoyance. 😭

⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘

✧ Lauren — girl… please ✧

I wanted to connect with Lauren. I really did. But she felt so underdeveloped that it was hard to latch onto anything about her. She didn’t feel like a fully formed character with her own emotional depth—she just existed within the story without leaving any real impact.

Because of that, the relationship also suffered. I couldn’t understand her pull toward Junior, couldn’t feel her emotions, couldn’t root for her decisions. Everything between them felt surface-level, and without a strong emotional anchor from her side, the story just fell flat. 🫠

⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘

✧ The Romance (if we can call it that) ✧

This is where the book completely lost me. Because I genuinely struggled to see the romance.

There was obsession, yes. There was attraction, yes. But there was no emotional intimacy, no meaningful connection, no foundation strong enough to support a second chance love story. Their past didn’t feel significant enough, and their present interactions didn’t build anything new.

It felt chaotic and immature rather than intense. Instead of tension, there was just noise. Instead of longing, there was confusion. And instead of chemistry, there was… nothing. 😭

⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘

✧ The Writing & Plot ✧

I kept waiting for the story to deepen—for more backstory, more emotional exploration, more insight into why these characters are the way they are. But everything stayed frustratingly shallow.

Some parts dragged endlessly, while others rushed through moments that could have actually mattered. And despite being labeled as a dark romance, the plot somehow managed to feel… dull. Which is honestly shocking, because this genre should be anything but boring.

But here, it was. Over and over again. 🫠

⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘

✧ The Only Saving Grace ✧

Josh and Aly. That’s it.😭😭😭😭😭

Every time they appeared, it felt like a breath of fresh air—like suddenly the story remembered how to have charm, chemistry, and actual emotional connection. Their presence reminded me of what this series could have been, and honestly, they carried whatever small enjoyment I got from this book. 😭

⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘⫘

✧ Final Thoughts ✧

This book had so much potential. The tropes, the premise, the setup—it could have been something intense and unforgettable. But instead, it ended up feeling hollow, repetitive, and emotionally disconnected.

And worst of all… it was boring. Not even chaotic enough to be entertaining—just draining to get through.

This wasn’t the kind of dark romance that makes you question your morals in a thrilling way. It was the kind that makes you question why you’re still reading. 🫠
