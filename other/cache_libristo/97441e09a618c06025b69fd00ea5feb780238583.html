Five Stars “Now and Always”!



So the bully romance with the rich privileged guy and the girl from the poor troubled background has been done before. But I love it anyway and this book one was a step above the rest. I actually wanted Cole’s story to be next in the series because he is intelligent and a bit mysterious. I thought Ronan’s story would be a bit boring since Ronan is such a nice guy. He didn’t seem to have much depth other than being a playboy.

I was so wrong! Ronan is hiding surprising intensity beneath that happy go lucky exterior and the heroine Teal had the same misconceptions as I did about Ronan. We met Teal in previous books when her and her twin brother Knox were found to be the foster children of Elsa’s father and they began attending the Royal Elite School. Teal was the quite and introspective girl who gives off the vibe that she doesn’t care what other people think about her and she does her own thing.

Teal was a tough nut to crack in this book which is why she is perfect for Ronan. She has a lot of secrets and some are much darker than others. However, she agrees to the arranged marriage to Ronan that her father and his father want to set up in order to cement their business partnership.

Ronan, as the son of an Earl can’t say no to an arranged marriage, as that is how it has always been done in his family. However, he threatens Teal and tries to get her to call it off, like he had done with a few girls his father tried to set him up with in the past. Though Teal isn’t as easily swayed because she has her own reasons for going through with the arrangement.

I don’t want to give too much away, but to say that one of the things that put this book above so many is the humor despite the troubling circumstances and backgrounds of our main characters. I laughed on and off through the entire book. Especially scenes between Ronan and his butler Lars. Lars is a traditional butler, Ronan even referred to the fact that he took cues from Downton Abbey and watched it regularly, taking it way too seriously.

“Lord Astor’s face remains blank as he talks to her, no expression, no smile, no nothing.”
“We agree that Dad is a robot, and by we, I mean Lars and me. Fine, Lars just listened with a judgmental expression while I informed him of that fact.”

Overall I just really liked the story and I read it through in one sitting. Both Ronan and Teal were lovable characters and made a great couple. The bully theme was not too strong and ended fairly quickly and they became a couple. I liked that there were some scenes where they provoked each other’s jealousy and there were some scorching hot intimate scenes between Ronan and Teal as well.



I am still looking forward to Cole’s book, though I am not too fond of Silver as his love interest. Though the fact that she is his step-sister does add another level to the story. I like that Cole reads a lot and apparently he has some crazy kinks, so it should be a fun one!

I voluntarily read & reviewed an advanced copy of this book. All thoughts & opinions are my own.

Me giving a Rina Kent book four stars? Well, that’s a miracle in itself.

I honestly didn’t expect to like this book or the characters. I just dived in headfirst without knowing a single thing about it, and I’m surprised by how much I actually loved and enjoyed it. Rina handled Ronan and Teal’s trauma in such a realistic and grounded way, and I loved how she delved deeper into their backgrounds, showing how their pasts shaped their personalities. The plot was intriguing, and the mystery surrounding what happened to both Teal and Ronan had me hooked.

Ronan is the embodiment of “It’s always the sarcastic and cheerful ones who fight the darkest battles.” I kind of disliked Ronan in the other books. I don’t know why, but his clingy personality and obsession with threesomes always irritated me. I never thought his story would be the one to break me the most in this series. He constantly surrounds himself with people because that’s the only time he doesn’t hate himself—when he doesn’t have to face his demons. Until Teal came into his life. He does so many surface-level things for attention, cracking jokes and acting like the happiest, most carefree person in the room. But in reality, he’s been suffering in silence. He’s the only normal MMC written by Rina so far—well, as normal as he can be. My heart broke for him when he finally opened up to Teal about his past, his coping mechanisms, and the loneliness he’s felt all these years. He always joked about having abandonment issues, and I laughed it off in the previous books—but looking back, he was giving us hints the whole time. I just ignored them because he was so clingy to everyone.

“They said you should find what you love and keep it close. The same can be said about what you hate.”


Teal is the IT girl—the one with a broken heart and a numb personality. She’s had physical relationships with men fifteen years older than her just to feel something that isn’t numbness. She’s the nerd girl with black cat energy—the one who built walls so high around her heart that nobody could climb them. Except Ronan, because he annoys her just as much as she annoys him. She was an anomaly. Until Ronan. I’ve been dying to know more about her ever since she appeared in Elsa’s book. I always had the feeling she was hiding so much pain behind her ‘I don’t give a fuck’ attitude. And I was right. Her story is just as heartbreaking as Ronan’s. She’s been through hell—the absolute worst—but this girl? She’s a warrior. She’s been numb for eleven years, but she knows how to fight her battles. And I loved that about her.

“I never allowed myself addictions before, because addictions screw you up and mess with your logic and your head. But as I hug Ronan, I know I have no choice in this addiction. It’s the type you just surrender to. You fall into it and let yourself float.”


The romance in this book shocked me because of how well done it was. From their very first meeting, I was rooting for Teal and Ronan. They didn’t just have physical chemistry—they had a deep emotional connection. They were the same in so many ways, but also different. Both of them hid their pain behind their personalities. Both of them only dealt with their demons when they were alone.

“You think I’m crazy?” My voice is barely above a whisper, and I hate it.
“I don’t think it. I know it.” This time he does run his finger over my lips as if he’s smearing my lipstick, just like that first time he touched me in the library. “And I want every last bit of your craziness.”
