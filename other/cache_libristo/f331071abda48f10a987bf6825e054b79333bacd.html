Scorch the realm. Bring the dawn.

Throne of Glass meets The Return of the King in the highly anticipated conclusion to the Best of #BookTok Shattered Crown trilogy.

A Fractured World

The fragile peace forged in the wake of the Crucible is unraveling. With the rise of an old foe and ancient magic stirring, the scars dividing Arcanis have begun to bleed anew, and the future of the realm hangs by a thread.

A Queen of Shadow

Elyria Lightbreaker has paid a devastating price to save her loved ones. Now, she faces a whole new darkness—and must decide how much more of herself she is willing to give to save a world that has already taken so much.

A Man on the Brink

Rocked by the secrets of his past and the truth behind his power, Cedric Thorne scours the continent in search of the woman he loves. But as enemies emerge from hiding and Elyria is pulled ever farther from reach, he must face the terrifying possibility that saving her may mean becoming something unforgivable. And once his fire is unleashed, there may be no putting it out.

A Final Reckoning

4⭐️ I’ll be honest, I wasn’t a huge fan of the first book in this series. Still, I decided to give the second book a chance and was pleasantly surprised by how much I enjoyed it. And somehow, the final book was just as good, if not better! If you stopped this series after the first book, I seriously recommend giving it another try!

Cedric has the greatest character arc I’ve ever seen in an MMC. He goes from being a weak, “damsel-in-distress” knight to a powerful warrior and magic-wielder who would literally burn the world down to save his woman. In the first book, I found him unremarkable, but by the time I finished this one, he had officially been elevated to top-tier MMC status!!

In contrast, we get to see a much more vulnerable side of our shadow-wielding FMC, Elyria. Though she’s still as sarcastic and sharp-tongued as ever, seeing her grapple with the grief and trauma of her captivity added so much depth to her character. She’s extremely dedicated to protecting those she cares about and is strong-willed without ever coming across as annoying.

The romance was a standout aspect of this book. Cedric and Elyria’s chemistry was lacking in the first book, so I was really worried about how it would develop over the series, but it progressed so naturally! Cedric is SO in love with Elyria, and it was adorable watching him constantly worry over her well-being. Their relationship has truly come such a long way from where it started!

The found family was another one of my favorite parts. While I’ll admit that some of the side characters’ POVs were boring to read at times, they each had such memorable and distinct personalities. I only wish that we had gotten more closure with their individual storylines, as a few of their endings felt unresolved.

This is definitely one of those series that gets better with each book. Though I wouldn’t say the series as a whole is one of my all-time favorites, I’m still glad I decided to stick with it until the end!

Thank you so much to Gretchen for the e-ARC of Shadow & Son! Returning to Arcanis was something I’d been so excited for ever since finishing Splintered Kingdom on a cliff hanger, and I was really looking forward to seeing how Elyria and Cedric’s story would conclude. Unfortunately though, this book didn't resonate with me personally.

This is not a spoiler-free review.

1) Pacing

One of the biggest things I struggled with in this final installment was the pacing, particularly surrounding Elyria’s captivity. Since rescuing her is such a major driving force of the story, I expected to feel far more emotionally invested in what she endured. While her time with Malakar was undeniably dark, a lot of it felt glossed over rather than deeply explored, which made it difficult for me to fully connect with the FMC-in-peril arc emotionally. Ironically, the relationship that affected me most throughout the book was actually Elyria’s relationship with her father. Their dynamic carried some of the strongest emotional weight for me, which is why I found myself disappointed that there wasn’t a true heart to heart reunion or sense of closure between them by the end.

2) The magic system

I find this a common problem with many romantasy books unfortunately - the magic system is underdeveloped. Cedric unlocking new Sunfyre abilities had the potential to be incredibly exciting, but many of these power developments felt sudden and insufficiently explained. Rather than seeing meaningful progression or a deeper exploration of the lore, it sometimes felt like abilities simply appeared when the plot required them. The life bond between Cedric and Elyria especially stood out to me as something that could have benefitted from much more depth and clarity, because beyond “they’re bonded and now stronger,” as a power-up to fight Malakar, there wasn't much more substance.

3) An oversimplified villain

Another unfortunate thing that deterred me from connecting with this story was the lack of any real motivations from Malakar who is your typical "i just want to take over the world" villain. He has no other motivations besides that and I have found that this works in other series when the villain is cunning and manipulative but Malakar lacks depth and complexity. His plans are predictable, his agenda is predictable and he always shows up exactly when you expect him to.

4) The multi-POV was messy

The multi-POV structure was another aspect that didn’t fully work for me. Once the main couple got together, I usually need more emotional tension between them or stronger development from the side characters. Unfortunately, many of the additional POVs felt disconnected from the main story. Several chapters focused on characters coordinating politics, relaying messages, or attempting to gather support, but these sections often disrupted the momentum for me rather than adding to it. I also struggled to feel attached the side characters because they didn’t feel distinct enough beyond one or two defining traits (e.g. Jocelyn having her wings chopped off). The sudden introduction of a dwarven clan of mercenaries near the final battle made it difficult to keep track of who was who and the character deaths unfortunately held little meaning to me when I couldn't really remember who they were.

That said, I still appreciated the ambition and scale of this finale, and there were moments between Elyria and Cedric that reminded me why I loved returning to Arcanis in the first place. The world itself still has such an immersive atmosphere, and I’ll always appreciate the emotional foundations that were built earlier in the series. While this conclusion ultimately didn’t fully land for me personally, I’m still grateful for the opportunity to revisit these characters and this world one last time.

Thanks to Scarlett Press for providing me with an eARC of this book!

Good conclusion to this series! Glad I finished and saw the conclusion of this series. This lost a little bit of tension for me but was overall a solid conclusion.

I liked book 2 quite a bit more than book 1 so I was excited to continue on in this sequel, We get a few more perspective series and some potential set up for spin off series but we remain mostly with Cedric and Elyria, which I think mostly worked. The difficulty with a trilogy when the main couple gets together in book two is how to keep readers interested and maintain tension. This book is a step up from book 2 in terms of sex scenes, but I found it did lose a lot of the romantic tension. I still enjoy Cedric and Elyria enough that this didn't both me too much, but I did find myself skimming sex scenes as they didn't always do anything to further the actual relationship or character development. As an example, Elyria is kidnapped by Malakar in the beginning of this book and his actions towards her are very traumatic. Cedric and her never really have an actual conversation about what happened to her, how it may effect their relationship, or how Cedric could help her manage the trauma. Having those sorts of intimate conversations would go a long way to show how they are continuing to deepen their relationship beyond just different titles and more sex.

There are definitely a lot of large action scenes. Action scenes are very hard to write well. They were pretty good here in terms of the actual writing. I think by book 3, you probably know if you enjoy her writing style. I did feel like there were several times where the solution of their problems came out of nowhere and without much effort or sacrifice from the characters. I never actually felt like there was much of a chance anything could really go wrong. Like I said, it seems like this author may want to write a separate book or series about the side characters but I think there was still a way to make the reader believe they could die and maintain some tension.

Having now completed the series, I do wish there was just a bit more world building. I never felt like I had a sense about who the cultists were, why they chose to become cultists, how they benefited from that choice, etc. The motivations of the villain are fairly clear - he wants to rule - but that's not a super compelling motivation and it doesn't explain why people follow him.

Overall I liked this series. I liked the relationship and the fantasy elements were interesting enough. I would definitely read more from this author, either books in this world or beyond it.

That being said, after finishing this last book, I did find myself feeling like there was something lacking. Another of the ARC readers already posted their review and I linked it below because I found myself agreeing with it exactly. It helped me put a finger on what I felt to be lacking in this final book.
https://www.goodreads.com/review/show...
I agree with the statements regarding the odd pacing, comments on the magic system and villain, as well as the confusing POVs.

This was hard for me to grapple with because I really felt like there was such a difference between the first two books and this 3rd book. I couldn’t give this book more than 3 stars, whereas the other two I rated higher.

I think part of my problem was the multiple POVs. Maybe if some of them were taken out and more time was instead spent on expanding on our two main characters (whom we love), there could’ve been a higher rating from me. I don’t think it needed to be broken into two books, 600 pages is a lot and makes for a good hefty romantasy, I think there just could’ve been some stuff cut out (maybe even put into a novella?) so that we could focus more on our main characters.

Elyria’s capture was brutal and traumatic, she needed more attention on her growth and battling through that. Almost being *aped is a BIG deal and I believe needed a lot more time to work through.

I also think that there could’ve been more growth between our two love interests in getting to know each other and navigating life as a couple. Book two essentially ended with their bonding mark and then they were almost immediately ripped apart. I would’ve liked to see them figure out how to do life together and maybe worked up more relationally towards the final “marriage” step at the end. Books one and two did SUCH a good job on this.

Also, the finale premise was good, but I think a lot of the deaths and almost-deaths were clustered together and not as impactful as they could’ve been.

But again, overall I loved this series. I loved the characters and following along with it all. I am so so sad this is over!
