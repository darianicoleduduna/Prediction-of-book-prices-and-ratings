Awake or dreaming?

For the past six months, Loren Calla's life has consisted of one simple objective: stay alive. With the help of her new friends and the love of a ruthless Darkslayer, she has defied the odds. But with the Veil rapidly falling, and swarms of bloodthirsty creatures invading Yveswich, survival has once again stepped beyond her reach. Pushed into a war between worlds, she must ask herself a question: what―or who―is she willing to sacrifice for victory? For love?

Darien Cassel fears that his luck is running out. Trapped deep underground, his Sight rendered ineffective by the darkness of another realm, it seems things can't get any worse. But when a new threat emerges, he is forced to push himself to the breaking point―and this time, if he falls, he may not get back up.

Roman Devlin's heart has been stolen by a beautiful thief. His father has demanded that he stay away from Shayla Cousens, but when their paths reconnect by force of circumstance, Roman finds himself falling for Shay all over again. With enemies closing in from all sides, Roman is faced with decisions that have the potential to either shape his future into what he always dreamed it could be...or destroy it.

In this breathtaking fourth installment in the House of Devils series, all must fight if they wish to stand a chance against the dark forces poised to devour their world. But when tragedy strikes again, they realize that nothing, not even love. may be enough to save them this time.

Kayla Edwards is the USA Today and International bestselling author of the new adult House of Devils series, including City of Gods and Monsters, City of Souls and Sinners, City of Lies and Legends, and City of Smoke and Brimstone. She also wrote the upper young adult romantasy novel Dreams of Ice and Iron. Her House of Devils series has been sold in seven languages to date. When she isn’t writing, she enjoys traveling, spending time in nature, and binge-watching her favorite television shows with her husband. Did I finish this book... or did this book finish me?

Here I am once again hurt (by choice) by another House of Devils book.

I did not have a moment of rest while reading City of Smoke and Brimstone. I was not joking about needing an inhaler while reading because I could not breathe. I think I need a hug? I'm not okay...

That is not a complaint, but a twisted compliment because... Holy. F**king. Sh*t.

The stakes keep on getting higher with every book, and so does my blood pressure.

Plans are wrecked, blood and death reeks everywhere, friends and foes part ways, and the odds keep turning against our beloved devils and everyone else.

They might've had luck on their side and the upper hand before, but now? Not so much and the reader will suffer alongside them.

We've always mostly followed Darien and Loren, who are my absolute darlings, and even though we got other POVs but I love that we get more of the other characters—and when they get their moment under the spotlight they really shine and I'm not just talking about Roman and Shay.

The dynamics slightly change in a way that hurt me but was kind of realistic and bound to happen under the circumstances they were in.

This book is definitely darker and more violent than it's predecessors. It's a nerve-shredder and I had to put the book down to take a deep breath and calm myself down several times.

But worry not, the humor is still impeccable and it gave me a break to laugh and dread what's coming at the same time.

I am left with a few questions surrounding the fates of some characters I hoped to see but I think those in particular will play a critical role in the next book, which is expected to be the final book in the series (book 6 is likely a prequel).

That final scene hurt and left a void in my chest...
I. Need. That. Book. NOW.

By now you should know that this series is a 5-star series by default. This is the perfect urban fantasy romance series for me due to the fact that it combines everything and I love and the author excels at gifting me with a new experience and journey in every book.

It's going to be so hard for me to part ways with these characters, but I'm grateful that I got to experience their stories and live through it with them. This will forever be one of my all-time favorite series.

Thank you Kayla for gifting me with an eARC and the opportunity to reunite with these characters and world sooner! I've been counting months, weeks, days to return to them!!

Somehow, despite the sheer length, the story must only span about two weeks in their universe and yet, nothing feels padded. Not a single page is wasted. I was fully locked in from start to finish.

One of the things this book did masterfully is split the characters across three different locations and events. This allows the story to explore everything that’s happening across their world, while giving each character space to grow and shine. With a character list this huge, it simply wouldn’t work if everyone stayed together — but when those rare, magical reunion moments do happen? Oh my god. Chills. Literal chills.

That’s why this installment is so disappointing — it takes everything that worked in the beginning and completely buries it under nonstop chaos and shallow drama.

From page one, it’s non-stop action. And not the kind that builds tension or pays off emotionally — no. This is chaos for the sake of chaos. Every chapter is a new disaster stacked on top of the last one, and just when you think things might settle for a second, nope. Something else goes wrong. And then another thing. And then another. It’s like the author decided the only way to move the plot forward was by continuously throwing the characters into hell with no break, no breath, no pacing.

The result? Total emotional numbness. I’m so exhausted by the endless, over-the-top “everything is going wrong” storytelling that I genuinely stopped caring about any of the characters. If they’d all died halfway through, I think I would’ve felt relief — at least the book would’ve ended.

It doesn’t feel tense or thrilling. It feels cheap. Manufactured misery. There’s no room to connect with the characters or feel the stakes because everything is so aggressively overblown and constant. I caught myself laughing — not because it’s funny, but because it’s just that ridiculous.

And then there’s the characters — specifically Darien and Loren. I’ve disliked Loren since the very first book. She’s selfish, emotionally immature, and contributes absolutely nothing. Every time Darien does something that isn’t 100% aligned with her expectations, she punishes him with coldness or drama. She doesn’t listen, doesn’t think, and spends more time sulking than doing anything meaningful. She’s a walking tantrum.

And sadly, Darien — who used to have potential — just ends up looking worse by association. It’s hard to root for a main character who chooses someone so unlikable, and keeps choosing her, over and over, even as she drags him down. In the beginning, he had some edge, some intrigue. Now? He’s basically her doormat. A shadow of the badass we started with. Just a tired, lovesick version of himself with no spine and nothing left to say that isn’t centered around Loren’s endless emotional mess.

By this fourth book, it’s a new low. Their relationship dynamic has devolved into something so shallow and melodramatic it’s bordering on parody. The miscommunication, the one-sided emotional labor, the constant bickering — at this point, it honestly feels like a Mexican telenovela, and not even a good one. I’m just waiting for someone to throw themselves down a staircase for the full effect.

What could’ve been a compelling continuation in the series ended up as a burnout slog of forced drama and bad pacing. I really wanted to like this. I’ve followed the series this far. But City of Smoke and Brimstone was just… too much, too fast, too often, and ultimately, too empty.

then we have darien who has 0 personality other than being obsessed with loren and you know what i have to say it: meeting this woman for a few months and then going so far as to prioritise her over your family and loved ones you’ve known for years is NOT a love story i can get behind. it’s just *insert eye roll* because i love a good it’s all or nothing romance but jesus how did it go from the found family in books 1-3 to whatever deluded ideals in this one? + atp reading the chapters with these two has become a stupid game of guess which person has a near or literal death experience at the end of the book

then we have roman and shay, the ship i adored most the previous book. here though … the amount of times roman practically humiliated shay publicly in this book rubs me the wrong way. i needed more groveling from him because it’s just absolutely cruel to lead someone on and then turn around to tell them they mean nothing to you. and i get it - he needs to protect her! but attempting to abandon the said love of your life (which seems to happen every 4 pages) is seriously the dumbest way to do so. still love them though & the main reason i’m giving this 3 stars

but there are some positives. i find that kayla has done an incredible job with the ensemble cast. there were so many povs but i truly enjoyed all the secondary characters. it just seems she can’t do the same for her main two leads which is rather ironic but still. it makes you really invested in the overall series and i’ll be anticipating the finale book!

My mind feels scrambled, because finally they get a moments peace, but at the same time they are all on the edge of a cliff, and about a breath away from falling over.

I was at the edge of my seat through the entire book, because these characters didn’t get even one moment to sit down and breathe.
I wouldn’t say this was the most intense book, but it was no less great.

I think my rating speaks for itself, it’s clearly a great book. There were however things that bothered me.

First of all. What the hell was Loren doing?
She knew they had less than a year, and yet she’s wasting time not communicating, and pushing Darien away???
She kept mentioning how she was still mad at him for the deal he made with the spider. But after everything that happened at the end of book three and beginning of this, she needs to sort out her priorities.
She was literally sobbing in relief when she found out he wasn’t dead, and then went straight to barely talking to him, or even looking at him for nearly 400 pages.
I know she has a plan to undo the deal and wants him to be able to move on. But respectfully, her plan is bullshit.
Even with what happened at the end of this book I don’t think anything could stop Darien from following her when she dies.

And at this point they have faced death so many times my head is spinning. Not necessarily in a good way..
There has been so much back and forth about when she will die, how he will die with her. How he can survive if she dies, and so on and so forth. And still her inevitable death loom over these books.
Sometimes a story can be great with an ending like that. This is not one of those cases. Not after the things they have been through, and not with the amount of times her heart has stopped and she has been brought back.
I’m going off of the assumption that she will somehow survive, maybe because of the well, and Helia or something will trade her life so Loren can live, and be immortal.
But that’s just speculation.

I love Travis, Max, Dallas and Malakai, but I haven’t enjoyed their pov this book.
At first when they got trapped in the city I was excited, and I felt the rush when they were trying to get out of the city along with everyone else before the barrier was brought back up. But even on page 600 something they were still just stuck there. We see them talk, try to get a room to rest in, go to the hospital to help Jewels, get Dallas’ wings removed and so on. But all we’re really reading about is them being stuck there and unable to get out.
They weren’t doing anything for the plot. And they had no way of contacting anyone. And it was only around page 600 that the others finally knew for certain that they hadn’t made it out of the city.
After that point things picked up the pace on that end, and I really loved how it came together. But I stand by my opinion on their pov.

And I was disappointed in Malakai almost the entire book.
I always loved what an absolute menace he is, and at the start of this book that opinion hadn’t changed.
But what was he doing this book?
He was incapable of being a team player in any and every situation, he blamed Darien and Travis for every hindrance they met. He kept being irrational about Jewels and Travis’s relationship, that isn’t even a relationship because there is no progress! And he put them all in mortal danger by lying about getting weapons, when he was actually trying to find Venom to get high. And everyone just forgave him, and he didn’t care the next morning.
I’m hoping this will lead to something. Like discovering how he is actually really scared and in pain to explain his self destruction.
But at this point I’m hoping Aspen will have enough of his bullshit. There are hints to there being more between them. But it seems like she has a thing for him way more than the other way around, and she deserves better.
Hopefully that will wake him up.

I love Roman and Shay. That hasn’t changed.
But I wasn’t falling all over myself like I was in the last book. Roman was pushing her away, reminding me of Loren, in the sense that they all almost died, and yet he’s still unable to see what actually matters.
I knew Shay wouldn’t have stood a chance if Roman abandoned her in that motel room like he was seconds away from doing. Literally in the car, bags packed, ready to just abandon her.
And he is a smart guy. But apparently he thought she would just be on her merry way.
Ten minutes after he decided to stay his fathers men showed up and they almost died.
Idiot.
The problem now is that I don’t trust him.
His and Dariens relationships don’t have to look anything alike, and I actually like how different they are. But there is never a doubt what Darien would do for Loren.
He would never abandon her and fight and kill anyone who hurt her.
I don’t trust Roman to stay - let alone love Shay openly - as far as I can throw him. And one time when I tried to throw something, it somehow ended up behind me!

Once again, in the end things changed, and I’m really looking forward to watching their relationship form, and their confessions to each other were really sweet. But he needs to step up his game and give her the validations she’s seeking.
But that’s one scene, where he introduced her to the guy who couldn’t stop staring at her, and was all «her name, is TAKEN»
Gah, I melted.

I’m REALLY interested in reading about Dean and Roman! At first I was really suspicious of Dean, because, hello. He has two other brothers who are both psychos, and we’re somehow supposed to believe his entire genepool isn’t screwed?
But then I learned he was actually Roman’s father! And now, my mind is kind of blown, and I desperately need to read more about them!
They haven’t even had the chance to talk since Donovan told Roman, and now Dean is stuck on fucking life support?!!! Unacceptable!
And honestly. Let’s listen to Uncle Dean. If you worry about something that hasn’t happened yet, you’ll only worry twice.

Watching Loren and Roark’s father daughter bond slowly develop was one of the highlights for me. I was desperate for more, and at the same time cherishing every moment because it was so special to see him care.
I know I’m supposed to hate him a little, considering the amount of times she said he had never been a father figure in any way.
But I am a sucker for a good father daughter story or whatever I’m supposed to call it.
And he really came through for both his daughters in this.
Now I need Teague to step up her game and be a mother.

Erasmus and Helia…what is there to say about them. They disappeared of course. No shocker there. Everyone else is dealing with the mess they basically created and they can’t even stick around to at least give them some information maybe? No?
They better show up in the next book offering the solution to all of this on a silver platter.

I feel like I could go on and on about the characters, and the story, an my speculations about what will happen next, but in short, the plot is unfolding through the whole story, never dropped in any way, and this book doesn’t feel like a filler or whatever.
The characters are all realistic even when it’s hard to agree with their choices. And sometimes the frustration just means you care about the story.
Whenever we see relationships, and personal growth it doesn’t happen in the blink of an eye, but it is a journey. Something I feel like a lot of authors miss in other books.
I love the whole series and can’t imagine why anyone akse wouldn’t!
