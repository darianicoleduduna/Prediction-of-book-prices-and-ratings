Rachel Gillig, the queen of gothic romantasy, returns with higher stakes, darker mysteries, and deeper longings in this gorgeous sequel to the #1 New York Times bestselling sensation The Knight and the Moth.

Aisling Cathedral is in ruins. Rodrick Myndacious, Bartholomew, and Maude Bauer are rumored to be dead—and the king has taken Sybil Delling as his bride. To show off their union and solidify his power over Traum, Benji proposes a series of tournaments throughout the hamlets. Captive and drugged, Sybil remains determined to vanquished him as she once did the Omens, even if she destroys the kingdom’s faith—and herself—in the process.

But then a mysterious knave rises to the top of the lists, a man with no name and no memory who may just be Sybil’s ticket to undoing Benji’s power. For in a land where stories repeat themselves, where a king can prove as cruel as a god, the only way to truly destroy the Stonewater Kingdom’s faith is to save it.

No one is more upset than I, Bartholomew, to see that three star rating at the top of this. When I finished this book and a friend immediately asked me what I thought, she got a voice note from me where I said, in a very small voice, "I don't...I don't think I liked it."

What I think could have been quite good about the book is the antagonist and ultimately what a weak antagonist he is. Purposely so! He's childish and impulsive, everything he does is fueled by a massive chip on his shoulder, he's a bit stupid, and a lot of the conflict in the book is downstream of having a person like that in a position of power, which I think is very interesting. Think of someone like Joffrey in Game of Thrones (I can reference only the TV show here); displays very little politcal acumen or strategic knowledge, basically a child with an ant farm he wants to shake, and that childishness becomes terrifying when it's emboldened by a system that gives him massive amounts of power and virtually no checks on it. Where I think this story is left wanting is in having that character and not having a well-rounded system to prop him up. The weakness of the antagonist becomes a genuine narrative weakness when you're left reading it like, 'well, okay, someone should just stab him and be done with it because we have no reason to think there would be any lasting consequences of that.'

In short, an interesting antagonist that exists in a flimsy system is left feeling flimsy altogether.

That's only one of many issues I had with this book, but ultimately I think is a good exemplar. This was just not a strong showing, which I am severely bummed about because of how much I ADORE The Knight and the Moth! A rare five star book for me and one which I loved re-reading and remembering that for all the problems I have with a lot of the current romantasy trend, it CAN be done very well!

This ended up being a very weak sequel. Much of the plot feels conveniently placed for convenience's sake and the whole narrative feels claustrophobic, which was a feeling I had to sit with for a while to figure out where it was coming from because structurally this book is an exact mirror image of the first, so why did this one feel more closed off? What I've arrived at, I think, is that the change in circumstances from the first book to this second one I think necessitated the world and our perception of it expanding, but they ultimately don't. What felt new and expansive in the first book suddenly feels confined in this one when we're retracing the same steps.

And in things that are out of the author's hands, I think this book also suffers a bit from Orbit's continuing problems with editorial. WHAT is going on over there? This is the third Orbit book I've read this year and the third that has had at least some small amount of glaring line editing issues. I'm really surprised to see this plauging even one of their biggest authors (if not THE biggest author they're publishing right now), though it's much less of a problem here than I saw in others. There were at least a couple scenes where a misused word or a seemingly forgotten piece of armor necessitated reading a scene three or four times and consulting with a friend about what was going on in that moment.

I do not wish to be all doom and gloom though! I really do like the prose here, I (mostly) like where this story ends, and our core characters I think do shine. Rachel Gillig is a VERY good character writer (though, if I'm being honest, I think book 1 is doing a lot of heavy lifting there) and it's not like I was struggling not to DNF or anything at any point. While this will go into the category of books I won't be revisiting, it does not diminsh how much I loved - and still love - the first book and these characters, and it won't keep me from continuing to be excited to see what she brings next.

2.5 ⭐️ i’m sawri 😭😭. i thought i would love this, truly, but i’m whelmed. and not the over kind. i was contemplating giving this a 3 but it took me staring at the wall for half an hour upon finishing to really digest that i felt nothing for any single character except bartholomew, and the magic of book 1 didn’t persist for me. i’m so sad, because i really banked on this being a hit ☹️.

this rating probably makes most sense if i frame it within the context of how i felt about the knight and the moth. i’ll break down my review of that book into three key takeaways:

#1
what i loved most about it — the main thing that made me give it 4 stars at the time — was how unique the story and the worldbuilding was. it felt like a completely new story to me, especially in a sea of very similar-feeling (popular) romantasies. this fresh feel and the lush writing is what made me enjoy that book as much as i did.

#2
i didn’t, however, really find myself connected to the mcs all that much. i liked sybil more than i did rory, but both were characters i wouldn’t say i felt a big draw to. i thought rory was a boring hero, especially. i also found myself apathetic towards the romance in there, and mentioned a hope around book 2 converting me on that piece.

#3
bartholomew was in fact pookie — my favourite character by a landslide. he was the other main contributor to my 4 star rating on book 1.

now, reflecting on these things from my first go-around with this story, i’ll link the thoughts that i have about the sequel to my initial feelings on its predecessor. this is therefore not really a review — it’s just a reflection. the “critiques” that i have aren’t even that. it’s really all about how i felt, so it’s more of a personal wrap-up.

🪦 the uniqueness and fresh storytelling

i lost some of that novelty i felt around the world, its constructs and the storytelling when this sequel devolved into the same tropes and plot devices we see in many a romantasy — trials (disguised as tournaments), large amounts of time spent on hauling ass / travelling, mcs seperated by amnesia, etc. if you like these things, you will enjoy them here as well, but because i wanted to keep feeling that newness and wonder i initially felt when i picked up the first book, these done-so-many-times-before aspects didn’t hit home for me. book 1 also had something of a quest, but it was fun because it felt like a scavenger hunt for the omens and the lore was lore-ing — which again, gave the story something of a fresh feel. we were going somewhere that mattered. the plot progression in here though? i really didn’t care about them damn tournaments 🥲, and since a lot of the lore wasn’t new anymore, this read felt incredibly stale for me. the whole thing felt pointless compared to the quest in the first book.

i also found the pacing of this one uneven. despite the fact that the book is only 350 pages, it felt really slow for a long time there. this could be my own fault though, since i read it piecemeal — i read a couple chapters at a time, and broke off to go read something else on several occasions — and i think i came back to this with marginally less enthusiasm each time that i returned. that could very well be why i felt it dragging me along and my enjoyment with it waning (because i was having a pretty decent time in the beginning). then, i was walloped with a very chaotic and crammed ending. the resolution felt quite rushed.

the writing was just ok for me? it felt better in the first one. maybe it’s that i’ve read some fantasy favourites recently that served some stand-out writing — but the prose in here kinda paled in comparison. it wasn’t bad by any means, but i wasn’t as deeply stirred by the delivery as i was expecting to feel.

🪦 the characters and the romance

i STILL didn’t feel all that connected with the mcs in here or care too much for the love story. the amnesia trope ruined things for me a bit but that’s a personal preference since i don’t get on with that device much, rare exceptions aside. the resolution of that problem also felt unintuitive to me.

and what was the vampirism about? their chemistry wasn’t really sizzling for me either.

honestly, rory just doesn’t DO anything for me. i tried to feel SOMETHING, to the point i sprung a headache during my tussle with the second half (which i did read continuously). i don’t dislike him at all but he’s just so bland and uninteresting to me 😭. i wanted to love him like everyone else does, but i just didn’t. adding his pov to this book was more of a hindrance for me than a good thing, because i was wholly disinvested in his character. if i recall, book 1 was single pov (sybil’s) — we could have kept it that way and i wouldn’t have minded. something about their voices didn’t feel easily distinguishable from one another either (and i’m talking about a print version since i read on my kindle). it wouldn’t always be quick for me to tell who’s pov i was in right from jump because their povs were written homogeneously. again, keeping to single would have been preferred for me.

also somewhat linked to character work, i lost the found family vibe that i got from the first book in this one. the diviners, and then even the gang hunting down the omens together in book 1, had their own brands of camaraderie that appealed to me. this book didn’t have that for far too long. and no, i don’t count benji’s bandits lmao. idk, things just felt a bit isolated. it tried to get it together in the second half, but i think it was too late for me to start caring all that much.

🪦 bartholomew

he was still the best thing to happen to me with this story. he was so funny and endearing, and the moments i felt the needle moving most with this sequel were ones he was involved in. this, at least, remained consistent from my experience with the knight and the moth. it’s just not enough for me to go any higher on the rating for this whole read.

🪦 another thing

one of my biggest issues with this book is how i felt as though it was built around an incompetent and lazy villian. i could nawt take that twerp seriously, with his drunk ass and zero charisma. his character and motivations didn’t read to me as though they had a ton of dimension or complexity, respectively. he just didn’t feel formidable enough and i was wondering how he made it so long and continued to thrive in the book. at some point, i almost started to question the prowess and intelligence of the good guys and how they hadn’t managed to dispose of that dweeb sooner. a very weak antagonist, if you ask me.

🪦 in conclusion

i almost wish that the first book was a longer standalone and we wrapped up there. this may have kept the story feeling crisp and novel for me, if so. don’t listen to meeeeee though because so many people are enjoying themselves with this, and you could very well too 🫶🏾.

✦ let's talk about these two books because i have THOUGHTS

I didn't really review the first book, so you know what? Let's just review both of them together because I haven't written an actual review in ages and apparently I have been hoarding thoughts like they're going to expire. So let's fucking go. Let's dig into this.

First of all, I picked this book up because EVERYONE was talking about it. Like everyone. The hype was everywhere and I was experiencing the worst FOMO known to mankind because people kept mentioning one name over and over again.

Bartholomew.

And I was like... WHO THE FUCK IS BARTHOLOMEW???

Seriously. Who is this man? Why is everyone obsessed with him? Why does everyone talk about him like he's some celebrity who personally pays their rent? I was so confused that at some point I genuinely felt like I had to read the book just to understand the Bartholomew agenda.

And honestly?

I should have.

Because that gargoyle is an ICON.

But let's start from the beginning.

✦ book one

The first book was honestly not what I expected it to be. And do I mean that in a bad way? Absolutely fucking not. I mean it in the nicest way possible because this book genuinely surprised me. I'll be honest, the beginning was slow. The world-building was slow. Like, really slow. The first 50–60 pages were a bit of a struggle for me because I was trying to understand the world, the characters, the rules, the whole fucking situation, and everything was being introduced at a very steady pace. But I wouldn't call it boring. It was just slow. And I think that's an important distinction because once I actually got into the world, I was OBSESSED. Like genuinely obsessed.

The concept was so unique to me. I've read quite a lot of fantasy, and this didn't feel like I was reading the same recycled fantasy plot with different character names slapped on top. There was something about the whole idea of the diviners, the omens, the kingdom and the way people perceived these abilities that made it feel different from the fantasy books I've read before.

And THAT was a huge plus for me.

Rachel's writing also deserves a massive fucking applause because once I got into it, her writing was so addictive. She has this way of pulling you deeper into the story without you even realizing it. And the way she made me feel about these characters??? The way she made me actually CARE??? Ma'am. I was in a fucking reading slump and this woman basically dragged me out of it by the hair. Cheers to her.

✦ so what is actually happening here?

The story revolves around Stonewater Kingdom, where there are six diviners. These diviners can dream and divine for people, interpreting omens and giving signs about what may happen whether those signs are good or bad depending on what they see in their dreams. And because this is apparently a kingdom where everyone takes omens VERY seriously, these diviners are basically treated as these almost god-like figures. Except they're not gods. They're humans with certain powers.

And then things start getting weird because, one by one, the diviners start disappearing. And eventually, the last remaining diviner, Sybil, seeks help from a knight and not just any knight.

Rodrick Myndacious. The most atrocious, nasty, insufferable knight in the entire fucking kingdom. And honestly? I stayed for him.

✦ Rodrick Myndacious

Let's talk about Rodrick because I need everyone to understand that this man had the sluttiest mouth ever. Like, sir???? I was NOT prepared. The dirty talk aside because honestly, that's a whole separate conversation, what I genuinely loved about him was the way he loved Sybil. It wasn't just the typical "she's mine, she's pretty, blah blah blah" kind of thing. He genuinely appreciated her. He loved her in such a soft, affectionate and almost adorable way, and I really loved seeing that side of him because underneath all the nastiness and the attitude, there was so much love there. He was absolutely my favourite character in the first book.

And I loved him in the second book too, don't get me wrong.

But...

The second book belongs to Sybil.

That woman fucking ATE.

✦ Sybil Delling

If the first book made me love Rodrick, the second book made me look at Sybil and go: oh. this woman is THAT bitch.

I loved her with my whole soul. The way she stood her ground. The way she fought for what she believed in. The way she questioned things when they didn't make sense. The way she refused to just sit there and accept whatever was happening around her. The way she did everything in her power to get back what she wanted. I LOVED THAT. She wasn't written as some tiny, dainty little character who needed a man to come in and save the day every five minutes. She didn't just depend on the male character to get what she wanted.

NO.

She fought for herself. She made her own decisions. She questioned things. She pushed back. She stood up for what she believed was right. And she was willing to fucking fight for it. And if you know me, you already know I will eat that kind of female character UP. Give me a woman who refuses to be passive and I'm seated.

✦ MAUDE BAUER. MY WOMAN. MY IT GIRL.

And speaking of strong women...

MAUDE BAUER.

How can I possibly write this review without talking about her? That woman was the IT GIRL. I genuinely cannot forgive Rachel for what she did to her. I simply cannot. I am holding a personal grudge and I will be taking it to my grave. Because Maude was one of those characters who just stays with you. I've read countless books and countless characters, but Maude is genuinely one of those characters I know I'm going to remember for a very, very long time. She had this incredibly warm, motherly charm to her, but underneath that she had SO much fire. She knew when something was right. She knew when something was wrong. And she knew how to stand up for it. There was something so comforting about her while simultaneously being so powerful, and I absolutely adored that combination.

Maude, you deserved better. That's all I'm saying.

✦ AND NOW... THE MAN. THE MYTH. THE LEGEND. BARTHOLOMEW.

THE REASON I PICKED UP THIS FUCKING BOOK. The icon. The celebrity. The GOAT. The entire reason I was sitting there wondering who the hell everyone was talking about. And honestly?

I GET IT NOW.

Bartholomew genuinely made the entire book better. I am not even kidding. He was such a lovable character, and there is just something about him that makes it almost impossible not to love him. He was funny, charming, memorable and just had that IT factor. Like, you know when a character enters the story and you're immediately like oh, you are going to be everyone's favourite, aren't you? Yeah. Bartholomew. I fully understand the hype now. If anything, I should have picked up the book sooner just for him.

✦ the world-building & why i actually loved this so much

One of the biggest things I loved about these books was how immersive they were. This is genuinely why I love fantasy so much. There is something so special about opening a book and suddenly feeling like you've stepped into an entirely different world. A world that doesn't belong to you, doesn't exist around you, and yet somehow, while you're reading, you feel like you belong there. And that's exactly what happened here. Rachel's writing made the world feel real. I didn't just feel like I was reading about Stonewater Kingdom. I felt like I was there. And that's one of the biggest compliments I can give a fantasy book. The writing was so immersive that I genuinely felt connected to the story from beginning to end. And honestly, if I had actually had the time, I probably would've finished both books within a few days because once I was in, I WAS IN. Unfortunately, life decided to be life and I was busy as fuck for basically the entire month, so it took me a whole month to get through everything.

But even with that huge gap between reading sessions, I never lost that feeling of being immersed in the story.

✦ and THE CHARACTERS???

Another thing I really appreciated was how much depth each character had. Even the side characters didn't feel like they were just thrown into the story to fill space. They had their own stories. Their own personalities. Their own motivations. Their own little pieces of history. And that made them feel like actual people rather than characters who existed purely to move the plot forward. I love when authors do that because suddenly you don't feel like you're just reading about a cast of characters. You feel like you know them. And that was absolutely the case here. I cared about these people. I was angry for them. I was rooting for them. I was emotionally attached to them.

And apparently that is exactly what Rachel wanted because look at me now, writing an entire fucking essay about them.

✦ final thoughts

Overall, I genuinely loved these books. The beginning was slow for me, especially those first 50–60 pages, because the world-building took its time and there was a lot to understand. But once I got past that initial hurdle, I was HOOKED. The concept was unique. The world-building was immersive. Rachel's writing was addictive. The characters had depth. The relationships actually made me care. And most importantly, it got me out of a reading slump.

That's honestly one of the biggest compliments I can give a book because when I'm in a slump, getting me genuinely excited about reading again is not an easy fucking task. But this did it. I fell in love with the world, the characters. I got emotionally attached to people I absolutely did not expect to get attached to. I understood the Bartholomew hype. I developed an entirely unnecessary personal vendetta over Maude. I watched Sybil absolutely EAT in the second book. And I stayed for Rodrick and his ridiculously slutty mouth.

⚘ "Overall, if you're thinking about picking this up, please do. Just give the beginning a little time because it takes a while to settle into the world. But once it clicks? You're fucking gone. And if nothing else, read it for Bartholomew." 💌
