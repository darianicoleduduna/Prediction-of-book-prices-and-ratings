UNDER SIEGE

Having at last consolidated the six prefectures of Zhongbo under his rule, Shen Zechuan stands between two looming threats: the Biansha Horsemen to the east, and the imperial court of Qudu to the west. Although Qudu remains paralyzed by internal strife, Shen Zechuan knows it’s only a matter of time before the fledgling emperor and her supporter, Xue Xiuzhuo, turn their eyes to him.

To the north, while Hassen continues to hammer Libei’s generals with relentless attacks, Xiao Chiye suspects his real target lies along the vulnerable border of Zhongbo. If he’s right, Shen Zechuan will be cut off and surrounded before reinforcements arrive.

Yet when Hassen’s attack comes, it is with a brutality no one anticipated. Forced to make a desperate stand, Shen Zechuan must hold the walls until Xiao Chiye arrives—or the city falls.

“Today’s pair of matched jades will be tomorrow’s tigers fighting over the mountain.”



I genuinely thought this was going to be the final major arc before a quieter ending volume full of extras, but clearly I underestimated just how much story is still left to tell. And honestly? I’m glad I was wrong because this volume feels huge. The war is so detailed and stressful that I had to keep putting the book down just to breathe for a second. Every chapter carries this constant pressure that something terrible could happen at any moment, and with this series, death is never just a possibility. It’s expected. That’s part of what makes it such a brutal read emotionally. This series is absolutely not for the faint of heart. I also love how war centered and political this volume feels. Normally I hate politics in books because I struggle to follow them half the time, but somehow danmei historical politics always completely hooks me anyway. QJJ continues to be one of the biggest exception to my usual reading tastes because I knew this series would become one of my all time favorites after only the first volume. Out of my top five danmei series, this is the only ongoing one, and somehow it still just keeps getting better.

Shen Zechuan feels fully sharpened here, not because he suddenly controls everything, but because every decision now carries the survival of Zhongbo on his shoulders. He’s no longer operating quietly from the shadows. He has to hold together land, soldiers, alliances, trust, and political authority while enemies close in from every direction. It makes him such a compelling main character because even with all his intelligence, he still feels human under the pressure. And seriously, how is this man with his weak, constantly ailing body still standing toe to toe with Hassen? I genuinely thought Shen Zechuan would get one-hit KO’d the second things became physical. Xiao Chiye remains incredible too because his role beside Shen Zechuan never feels performative. He stands with him as an equal, as a military partner, and as someone willing to carry the same burdens. Their relationship works so well because love doesn’t reduce the tension around them. It makes every decision feel heavier and more urgent. Xue Xiuzhou is also such a strong antagonist because he doesn’t feel evil for the sake of being evil. One of my favorite things about this series is how some enemies genuinely want what they believe is best for the nation. How do you fight someone who has no selfish ambition? Honestly, the thought of what Xue Xiuzhou could’ve become under teachers like Hai Liangyi or Qi Huilian is terrifying.

I also need to scream about Gu Jin because omg, I love him so much. And Qiao Tianya too. The quieter emotional moments in this volume hit especially hard because characters like Qiao Tianya and Yao Wenyu bring tenderness and heartbreak into a story otherwise filled with warfare, siege pressure, and political maneuvering. This series constantly reminds you that today’s ally could become tomorrow’s enemy, and that instability makes every relationship feel fragile. Li Jianting and the Empress Dowager continue to prove how dangerous legitimacy and political influence can be even from far away. Nothing in this story feels simple anymore. Every side has reasons, every victory comes with a cost, and every moment of hope feels temporary. The volume is dense and definitely asks for your full attention, but the result is incredible because Zhongbo feels real, vulnerable, and absolutely worth fighting for. Few series manage to balance war strategy, leadership, romance, and emotional devastation this well. I finished this volume stressed, exhausted, emotionally damaged, and already desperate for the final one.

Balance is the key to successful relationships – romantic and otherwise. In this seventh volume of “Ballad of Sword and Wine” by Qiang Jin Jiu the web of political intrigue, border disputes and various personal relationships is beginning to tighten. The various strands of the plot are beginning to come together in a close-knit weave. The power struggle in the Qudu court is affected by Shen Zechuan´s increasing control of Zhongbo which in turn is interwoven with Libei because of his relationship with Xiao Chiye.

At the heart of the dispute lie grain politics; the corrupt nobles seize it to appease their greed while others want it to control the political game. However, Shen Zechuan shows charity by subsidizing grain prices in order to feed soldiers and the general population in neighbouring prefectures. Whereas Yan Heru is motivated by money, Shen Zechuan sees both political opportunity and the need to prevent people from starvation. He is ruthless when need be but never on a whim. It therefore comes as quite a surprise when he finally makes move on Yan Heru, who had thought that he had worked out from where to anticipate danger. “The truth was, Yan Heru, didn´t understand Shen Zechuan or Xiao Chiye at all. He hadn´t the slightest clue which of the pair was the sheath and which the blade” (chapter 228).

Shen Zechuan makes his move because of Master Yideng, who is a master healer. Xiao Chiye has been searching far and wide for someone to cure Shen Zechuan, whose health has been failing since he was poisoned in his youth. Yan Heru´s schemes end up hurting this miracle doctor thereby dashing their hopes of a cure for both Shen Zechuan and also Yao Wenyu. Poisoning is a major aspect of the plot because it threatens to take major players off the board: Li Jianting, the heir apparent, is poisoned in Qudu; Shen Zechuan was poisoned in his youth and Yao Wenyu when he wanted to defect to Shen Zechuan. It is an insidious type of violence because it kills slowly and thus can be administered clandestinely.

Yao Wenyu´s weakened health and inability to walk are, however, what enable Qiao Tianya to show his tender side. It is only because Yao Wenyu is sleeping that he sits at his bedside, braiding a red bracelet and places it on his wrist. Their relationship is clear but remains non-verbal for most of the book. But Yao Wenyu wears the red bracelet and keeps the silken ball that Qiao Tianya had gifted him after winning a horse race in the previous volume. At one point, Qiao Tianya even states that he would like to be reborn as Hunu, Yao Wenyu´s small cat, in his next life, because it can sit on his lap but it takes a life-and-death situation during the siege of Duanzhou for him to make a move. "Qiao Tianya cupped his cheeks and crowded him into a gloomy corner for a kiss. This kiss was not at all gentle; it blazed with hot desire beneath the tang of blood” (chapter 250).

In contrast, the relationship between Shen Zechuan and Xiao Chiye is fleshed out in much greater detail. It is definitely physical but its strength comes from mutual respect of the other and a willingness to sacrifice for it, making it a balance of happiness and impending danger. “When Shen Zechuan had made love to Xiao Chiye that first time, his battered, scarred body had finally experienced pleasure – the joy of being alive.” He had said to Xiao Chiye, “I am yours, even in death. And you are mine … If anyone tries to take you from me, I´ll kill them” (chapter 235). Both are political creatures, but Xiao Chiye´s greatest fear is Shen Zechuan´s failing health. This visceral fear is universal, because the courage to love is intertwined with the fear of loss of love.

The major event of this volume is the siege of Duanzhou when Hassen launches a surprise attack on the city while Xiao Chiye is at the border fighting. These battle chapters are full of exhilarating scenes where the defenders use everything they have to fight off the Biansha horsemen. The siege is waged with siege engines, boiling oil and sending flaming birds to burn down the wooden gates. The scenes are detailed while always maintaining a strong connection to the characters fighting so that the plot is tethered to the emotions and motivations of the defenders. Despite Shen Zechuan´s poor health, he rises to the occasion putting his father´s treason to shame by fighting with the common people. Unlike Xiao Chiye he is no ferocious warrior but mounts his white horse, Frostfall, and seizes Avalanche, his sword, and leads the charge. “Shen Zechuan was not strong, but he would not fall. In the dim light of the coming dawn, he was like a single speck of sand, but also like the glittering steel blade nailed to the ground before the city of Duanzhou” (chapter 247). This shows that courage is found in the willingness to fight despite the odds.

When Xiao Chiye eventually arrives on Snowcrest to save Shen Zechuan, he is both a saviour of his lover but also a skilled warrior who can take down a formidable enemy. He fully lives up to the image of the prince on the white horse but in a much more real sense, because everyone is traipsing through mud and rain. It is only after the battle that adrenaline drains away and reveals that Shen Zechuan is seriously wounded and feverish. Xiao Chiye´s previous vague worry about Shen Zechuan´s health now turns into a literal fear for his life and it is not only Xiao Chiye who is worried sick. Fortunately, Master Yideng had a young disciple called Jiran and he has learned enough to prevent the worst from happening.

The volume ends by teeing up the situation for the final battle. Xiao Chiye had stayed at Shen Zechuan´s bedside while he was sick, but Shen Zechuan knows that he is itching to cross the Chasi River and smash Amur and the Biansha riders in order to avenge his father. As he so sweetly says, “My wife understands me well” (chapter 254). Even though Shen Zechuan may want Xiao Chiye to stay safe, he also knows that he must give him the freedom to pursue his father´s killer. Happiness is not togetherness at any price; it is fighting for a life worth living…
