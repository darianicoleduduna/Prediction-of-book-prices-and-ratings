The Instant #1 New York Times Bestseller

The must-have, true enemies-to-lovers standalone romantasy about a woman whose lips are deadly poison . . . until she meets the one man who can survive her kiss.

This edition features a full-color illustrated case, designed endpapers, and an exclusive bonus chapter from Henry’s POV.

In the absence of a conscience, men need something to fear. I’ve made myself their monster.

Every person Harlow Carrenwell kisses dies immediately, and that’s the way she likes it. The poison-lipped youngest daughter of Lunameade’s magical founding family has used her power to annihilate their opposition.

Her first husband is in the ground. Her mysterious new betrothed is next.

But Harlow has a secret. When she’s not acting as an assassin at her parents’ whims, she moonlights as a vigilante hunting abusive men in their walled-off city.

Meet a man. Lure him in. Kill him with a kiss. Until one night Harlow kisses a mark and he doesn’t die.

Worse, the man immune to her kiss is her new betrothed, Henry Havenwood, and now he knows about her double life. The only way to protect her secret and her city is get close enough to Henry to figure out why the Havenwoods have reappeared after years of silence and what they have planned.

Cursed with a husband she can’t kill and trapped in a fortress surrounded by vampire-infested woods, Harlow’s survival requires her to do the Make the man who knows she’s a killer fall in love with her anyway.

About the author

Sheila Masterson

9 books3,129 followers
Sheila Masterson is the #1 New York Times Bestselling author of The Poison Daughter, a dark enemies to lovers romantasy standalone about a vigilante who uses her poison kiss to take revenge on abusive men in her community, until she's unwittingly thrust into an arranged marriage with the only man immune to her magic.

Her other works include the gothic fairy tale fantasy romance, Song of the Dark Wood, the angsty epic romantasy quartet, The Lost God Series, as well as its next-generation standalone spinoff, A Legacy of Stars.

When Sheila's not busy stabbing beloved side characters and dreaming up new ways to make her readers cry, you can find her browsing the shelves at local indie bookstores, drinking fancy cocktails with friends, or curled up reading books and tarot. She lives outside Philadelphia with a small army of underwatered houseplants that survive out of spite.

ㅤ ‍‍‍‍‍‍ ‍‍‍‍‍‍ ⋅ ☾ I loved the concept of this world and the way it was constructed. The contrast between the polished, stuck-up perfection of Lunameade and the untamed beauty of Mountain Haven was genuinely fascinating. I also loved the difference in atmosphere between the two: one serious, elegant, and refined; the other supposedly wild, instinctual, and unapologetically primal (yet far more gentle).
ㅤ ‍‍‍‍‍‍ ‍‍‍‍‍‍ What I found most interesting, though, was how beautifully the book played with those appearances. Because beneath all that elegance and class, Lunameade often hides things far more barbaric than the people it looks down upon. In contrast, those considered "primal" frequently show more honesty, loyalty, class and humanity than the supposedly civilised world ever does.

ㅤ ‍‍‍‍‍‍ ‍‍‍‍‍‍ ⋅ ☾ Sadly, the whole idea of women being forced into these roles and being abused by the very people who are supposed to protect and care for them hit far too hard. It was such a beautiful yet deeply unsettling detail, especially because it mirrors issues that still exist today.
ㅤ ‍‍‍‍‍‍ ‍‍‍‍‍‍ What made it even more striking was seeing these things happen within a society that prides itself on being elegant, educated, and civilized, while the supposedly "primal" society built around instinct is the one that actually shows women more respect. It really gives you something to think about.
ㅤ ‍‍‍‍‍‍ ‍‍‍‍‍‍ And yet, both societies reflect a problem that feels increasingly familiar: women are expected to play their role, stay agreeable, remain "proper" and accept a man's protection for the sake of their own safety. Enter our FMC, who has absolutely NONE of that nonsense. I am fairly certain her husband's entire world is about to be turned upside down because of her, and honestly? I would have paid good money for an epilogue just to witness the aftermath.

ㅤ ‍‍‍‍‍‍ ‍‍‍‍‍‍ ⋅ ☾ The book deals with some very harsh realities and it might definitely make you uncomfortable at times. But, if you ask me, I think this is exactly the scope of these elements being present here. You ARE MEANT to feel uncomfortable reading them just because they are meant to shock you so that you can actually understand how flawed and broken these characters actually are. Because these are some VERY FUCKED UP CHARACTERS.

ㅤ ‍‍‍‍‍‍ ‍‍‍‍‍‍ ⋅ ☾ This definitely won't be everyone's cup of tea. It tackles some pretty heavy concepts, and unless you find yourself relating to the characters or understanding where they're coming from, I can easily see parts of this story feeling uncomfortable, frustrating, or even downright ridiculous. A lot of what makes this book work depends on whether you connect emotionally with its themes and characters. If you do, it hits hard. If you don't, you might spend half the book wondering what everyone else is seeing that you're missing. So, in conclusion: CHECK THE TRIGGER WARNINGS BEFORE PICKING THIS UP. I am begging you.

It let me have my little moment of intellectual superiority when I correctly guessed one of the revelations, only to immediately humble me by throwing something even more shocking my way. The audacity. I genuinely did not see some of those twists coming, and I absolutely loved that. This book kept me constantly guessing, constantly suspicious, and yet somehow completely unaware of what was actually waiting around the corner.

ㅤ ‍‍‍‍‍‍ ‍‍‍‍‍‍ ⋅ ☾ Now take a seat for the romance section of this review, because this might get a bit long; I am after all a romantic at heart: give me a messed up love story that I can analyse, and you have my entire attention.
ㅤ ‍‍‍‍‍‍ ‍‍‍‍‍‍ This romance is very multifaceted in a way that is both frustrating and beautiful. I definitely had moments where I questioned what on earth was happening between these two, but at the same time, their internal monologues, their pasts, and the way they see the world constantly clarified everything.
ㅤ ‍‍‍‍‍‍ ‍‍‍‍‍‍ No, this is not a soft, fairy-tale love story built on trust, patience, and gentle emotional growth. These people are freaks. Very damaged freaks, to be clear. They are so flawed and broken that it is almost impressive they managed to find each other at all. Everything about them is unconventional, yet it works in its own strange way. It begins from something much darker, rooted in hate, but still grows into something strong enough to change them both. This love is not soft or slow. It is violent, wild, primal, and vicious, and somehow exactly the only kind of love these two could survive in.
ㅤ ‍‍‍‍‍‍ ‍‍‍‍‍‍ The ending does bring a softer edge, where both of them finally realise that their ruined selves somehow created something real between them. Something quiet and fragile, born from the need to be seen, to be safe, and to be themselves without masks. Watching them understand that what started as an act turned into something deeply authentic was genuinely beautiful.
ㅤ ‍‍‍‍‍‍ ‍‍‍‍‍‍ I will also repeat: this story deals with strong themes and very primal, intense dynamics. It is not for everyone, and whether it works for you will honestly come down to taste.

The Poison Daughter completely pulled me into its dark and dangerous world and ofcourse there is Henry, need I say more...

I love fantasy stories with morally gray characters, and Harlow Carrenwell was the kind of heroine I couldn't stop rooting for. Look at times she was a bitch, but I'm not one to talk, so I am am not going to judge. She's fierce, flawed, and determined to protect vulnerable women, abd deeply driven by her desire for revenge and justice, even when her methods are brutal. What I enjoyed most was that she wasn't written as a perfect character. Her struggles, choices, and emotional battles made her feel real and unforgettable. The more you get into the story the more you learn about the why's. Why she is the way she is and why she does what she does. The twists kept twisting in this one let me tell you.


The relationship between Harlow and Henry was my favourite part of the book. The tension, banter, and slow development of trust kept me turning pages. Their enemies-to-lovers journey felt intense and believable, but let's be honest they were more like enemies and lovers because I don't see this man ever having a day's worth of peace with this woman in his life. And believe me he is exactly where he wants to be.

Henry is so HOOOOOT. That man would have me on my knees in half a second flat no lies. At first, he seems mysterious and impossible to trust, which makes sense considering he comes from a rival family and suddenly becomes the one person immune to Harlow's deadly kiss. Instead of being intimidated by her reputation or trying to control her, he challenges her in ways that force her to confront her fears, beliefs, and vulnerabilities.

What makes Henry stand out is that he's far more than a typical fantasy love interest. Beneath his confidence and wit is someone carrying his own burdens and secrets. As the story unfolds, his actions reveal a deep loyalty and compassion that contrast beautifully with Harlow's guarded nature. I loved watching their relationship evolve because Henry doesn't try to "fix" Harlow or change who she is. Instead, he sees all the darkness she carries and chooses to stand beside her anyway.

Their chemistry is incredible, but what made me truly invest in them as a couple was the emotional connection they built. The banter is sharp, the tension is irresistible, the spice is SPICY damnnnnn I needed to fan myself, and the moments when Henry lets his guard down are some of the most powerful in the book. He becomes Harlow's equal in every sense, matching her strength while also providing the understanding and acceptance she desperately needs.


I also loved the atmosphere of the story. The dark fantasy setting, dangerous forests, family secrets, and supernatural elements created a world that felt both magical and threatening. There were plenty of twists that surprised me for real... I really didn't see some of them coming. Maybe I was just to into Henry to notice.

What didn't make this a full 5 star was that some things were left open ended and questions remained unanswered some of the plot fell a bit flat or made me say huh???? BUT OVERALL, The Poison Daughter was more than just a romance. It was a story about survival,dealing with grief, justice, trust, and learning to let someone see the parts of yourself you try to hide from everyone.
