Return to a world sparkling with dark magic and desire with this highly anticipated sequel to the enemies-to-lovers romantasy sensation Rose in Chains…

Briony Rosewood escaped the worst of it – or so she tells herself. Though forced to serve the woman who gutted her kingdom, she has the protection of the powerful Hearst family. But even they have their limits. Now Briony must do the unthinkable: share her magic – and her heart – with Toven Hearst. Once her enemy…now her tentative ally.

Forced to travel to distant kingdoms to play the part of Toven’s adoring heartspring, their survival depends on Briony pretending to love the man whose family helped destroy her own. But the cruellest part is that her treacherous heart has always been his.

While she smiles for the enemy, behind closed doors Briony feeds the rebellion with whispered information and dangerous new alliances. And the Hearsts harbour secrets of their own – but none is darker than Toven’s shadowy, devastating gift.

When the lost daughter of Evermore finally chooses where her heart belongs, everyone will come to know the full force of their combined power – and her wrath.

EDIT: Adding more quotes in the review after pub day!

First of all, thank you SO MUCH to the publisher and NetGalley for the ARC in exchange for an honest review!

When I got the email for this ARC, I immediately dropped what I was doing and started reading... and finished the entire thing in LESS than 24 hours. Who needs sleep, anyway?

However much you think you’re going to love this book, double it.

If you love agonising slow burns, YEARS of yearning, delicious banter, a hopelessly devoted MMC, two people who are ridiculously horny for each other, and a romance full of heartbreak and SO MUCH PINING... this book is EVERYTHING.

Throw in political intrigue, dark magic, dangerous secrets, and revelations that will leave you absolutely REELING, and you’ve got a book that’s impossible to put down.

And while I’m about to spend an unreasonable amount of this review screaming about Toven Hearst and the romance (HOW COULD I NOT?!), I also want to emphasise just how dark and heartbreaking this book can be. Much like Rose in Chains, Vine of Hearts doesn’t shy away from the gruesome realities of war, human trafficking, and unimaginable cruelty.

Julie Soto balances the darkness with moments of tenderness, humour, and romance SO beautifully. And somehow, all that suffering makes the moments of love and hope feel even more meaningful.

I was completely and utterly hooked from beginning to end.

♦️ Picking up where we left off ♦️
⊱ “I give myself to you freely. I grant you my heart.” ⊰

Vine of Hearts is the second installment in the Evermore Trilogy. It picks up right where Rose in Chains left off, with Briony and Toven navigating the consequences of their newly formed heartspring bond, which now binds not only their magic but their heartbeats.

Forced to travel to foreign kingdoms, Briony must pretend to be Toven’s devoted heartspring while secretly gathering information, forging dangerous alliances, and aiding the rebellion.

But pretending to be in love becomes considerably more complicated when neither knows what the other truly feels. Meanwhile, political tensions escalate, dangerous secrets emerge, and unsettling revelations about Toven’s mysterious magic threaten to change everything Briony thought she understood.

I was hooked, hooked, HOOKED.

♦️ The flashbacks are so, so perfect ♦️
⊱ “She was pretty, yes, particularly when she scowled. Or perhaps he had only seen her scowl.” ⊰

One of my favourite things about Rose in Chains was how cleverly the flashbacks were incorporated into the narrative, and that’s done so well here, too. This time, the flashbacks are told from Toven’s perspective, and I cannot emphasise enough how much I loved that decision.

We get to see so much more of his childhood, his loneliness (I WANT TO HUG 9-YEAR-OLD TOVEN SO BADLY), his friendships, and the experiences that shaped him into the man we know in the present. I especially loved getting to know him better through his interactions with his wonderful parents and seeing more of what his life was like growing up. We also learn more about his magic (!!!!), which becomes even more interesting when you begin to understand how those revelations connect to everything happening now...

The placement of these chapters was absolutely perfect, adding another layer of meaning to what we’re seeing unfold in the present. It’s such an effective way of telling the story because we get to see the experiences that made him who he is. And some of those experiences were genuinely heartbreaking…

Also, getting answers to some of the questions raised by Briony’s flashbacks in the first book was SO satisfying (JUST WAIT FOR THOSE MOMENTS, GAHHH). Seeing certain moments from Toven's perspective, finally understanding what was happening behind the scenes, and realising what some of it actually meant?! I WAS SCREAMING INTO MY PILLOW.

There were moments in these chapters that made me want to reach through the pages and give that boy a hug. And don’t even get me started on some of the revelations involving Briony and her family in the present day chapters, because I was close to tears!

I was equally invested in both the flashbacks and the present-day chapters, and I think that says a lot about how brilliantly they complement each other.

♦️ Toven Hearst, the man that you are ♦️
WHAT A MAN. Toven Hearst is the ultimate book boyfriend, and I will not be accepting arguments at this time. Oh, how I love him.

The yearning. THE YEARNING!!! Getting his perspective in the flashbacks takes everything to another level from the first book.

Seeing how his feelings for Briony developed over the years, how he noticed her, how their interactions became something he treasured, and how deeply he came to love her was just... ugh.

It was wonderful seeing the contrast between the boy he once was (I REPEAT: I WANT TO HUG BABY TOVEN SO BADLY) and the man he has become, especially when you realise just how much of himself he’s kept hidden… and why.

The thing about Toven is that he would do absolutely anything for Briony. ANYTHING. And he does. He does, he does, he does.

I don’t know how to explain the extent of this man’s devotion without spoilers, but the lengths he’s willing to go to for her? The things he does, the choices he makes, the way he continually puts her needs and her safety before his own? I was losing my mind.

What makes it all the more wonderful is that his devotion is in the smaller things, in how closely he pays attention to her, how WELL HE KNOWS HER, and how much he cares about what she wants and needs.

He doesn’t simply want to have her. He wants her to be happy, to be safe, to have the freedom to make her own choices, even when those choices might not include him. He’s so desperately in love with her, and yet so convinced that she could never genuinely feel the same way. And knowing what we know from those flashbacks? HEARTBREAKING.

I loved him before, but this book made me love him even more... which says A LOT.

♦️ Miscommunication done RIGHT ♦️
I never thought I’d be praising the miscommunication trope quite this enthusiastically, but here we are. Because THIS is exactly how you make misunderstandings leads feel believable rather than frustrating.

Toven has genuine reasons, rooted in their shared past, to believe that Briony doesn’t return his feelings. And because of their new heartspring bond, he has another reason to question whether any attraction she expresses towards him is actually her own. Meanwhile, all Briony sees is a man who can convincingly play the part in public, only to avoid affection when they’re alone… And of course she interprets that as rejection.

The wonderful thing is that we, as readers, understand both sides. We know why Toven is holding himself back, and we understand why Briony reads his restraint the way she does. The flashbacks make this dynamic even stronger because they give us the emotional context behind his assumptions. Some of the moments that shaped his understanding of Briony’s feelings were absolutely devastating (oh god, a certain scene made me want to scream and CRY), and knowing about them makes his behaviour in the present so much more meaningful.

It’s not miscommunication for the sake of prolonging the romance, but one that grows naturally out of their circumstances, their history, and who they are as people. I absolutely loved it.

♦️ The romance, the banter, and the delicious tension♦️
Briony and Toven’s banter is one of my favourite things in these books. I love the way they challenge each other, the way they respond to each other’s remarks, and how much personality comes through in even their smallest interactions (they know each other SO well!).

There’s something so natural about their dynamic that makes their developing relationship feel completely organic. And then, of course, we have the delicious situation of them having to pretend to be a couple in love…

The forced affection. The public displays of intimacy. The kisses that are supposedly just for show. The tension of knowing that both of them want so much more than they’re willing to admit.

I ATE IT UP.

I loved watching their relationship develop throughout the story, and I particularly appreciated that the emotional progression felt earned. For all the yearning and sexual tension, the romance never loses sight of the fact that these are two people who genuinely enjoy and understand each other, even when they don’t quite understand what the other is feeling.

♦️ Briony Rosewood was DOWN BAD ♦️
Briony, bless her heart... she was THIRSTY. And who can blame her? Honestly, the sheer amount of time this woman spends thinking about Toven Hearst and all the things she’d like to do to him (or have him do to her) was adorable.

The yearning might be Toven’s speciality, but Briony certainly gives him a run for his money when it comes to being completely and utterly consumed by the other person. And I loved that we got to see this side of her! After everything she’s been through, it was wonderful seeing her openly acknowledge her own desires, even if she’s not ready to admit the full extent of her feelings.

The contrast between Toven desperately trying to maintain some semblance of restraint and Briony becoming increasingly determined to make him lose it? DELICIOUS.

♦️ The slow burn finally catches fire ♦️
⊱ “Toven Hearst liked to take his time. That’s what she could gather from this.” ⊰

Considering everything Briony went through in Rose in Chains, it made one hundred percent sense to keep the physical intimacy restrained in the first book. I loved that Julie Soto never rushed that aspect of their relationship simply for the sake of delivering a few spicy scenes.

But Vine of Hearts? Oh, this book MAKES UP FOR IT.

The intimacy fits their dynamic so well, even if they were pretending (or PRETENDING to be pretending, lol). Because of course Toven Hearst, a man who has spent so long wanting this woman, would be absolutely determined to take his time with her. OF COURSE HE WOULD.

After everything these two have been through, I think we all deserved that payoff. THEY did, certainly.

♦️ Briony Rosewood and the importance of having a choice ♦️
One thing I appreciated so much in Rose in Chains, and was delighted to see continued here, is how much agency Briony has within the story. Because despite everything that’s happened to her, despite the circumstances she’s been forced into and the people who would happily make decisions on her behalf, Briony never stops being someone with her own convictions, desires, and determination.

For all of Toven’s power and his willingness to do absolutely anything for her, he never expects her to simply fall in line with what he wants. He respects her independence, her determination, and her willingness to challenge him, even when it makes things considerably more difficult for him.

And I think that’ such an important part of why their romance works so beautifully. Briony isn’t simply someone to be protected or rescued, nor does falling in love mean surrendering her own ambitions or convictions. She still wants to fight for what she believes in, and she still insists on having a say in what happens to her. After everything she’s endured, it’s particularly meaningful seeing her reclaim that sense of control, especially in a relationship where the imbalance of power could so easily have undermined it.

The best part is that Toven loves that about her. He doesn’t love her despite her stubbornness or her refusal to be controlled. Those are some of the very things he adores about her. I mean, who could forget his line from the first book? “I suppose I’ve never sought anyone who obeys easily.” EXACTLY.

♦️ The world-building and all those delicious mysteries ♦️
One of the things I love about this series is that there’s always this sense that we’re only beginning to understand how everything works, and Vine of Hearts takes that intrigue to an entirely new level.

The things we learn about Toven’s magic alone had me absolutely fascinated, though I won’t spoil any of that… And then there’s the history surrounding the first heartspring and that particular story… I MEANNNN. There is SO much to unpack there, and I am absolutely dying to know where Julie is taking it.

It’s the kind of world-building that makes me want to go back through both books and start connecting every little detail, because I have a feeling there are things that will become even more significant once we know the full story… And I cannot WAIT to see how it all comes together in the final book.

♦️ Middle-book syndrome? Never heard of her ♦️
To me, there’s a difference between a story being action-packed and a story being so relentlessly eventful that nothing has time to matter. Vine of Hearts gets that balance exactly right.

There’s intrigue, danger, political manoeuvring, revelations (!!!!), romance (!!!!), and plenty of action, but the pacing never sacrifices the emotional development or the quieter moments between characters.

I never felt bored, but I also never felt as though the story was rushing through important developments just to get to the next exciting thing. Everything kept me invested, and the mysteries introduced throughout the book only made me more desperate to keep reading.

For a book I half expected to function primarily as a bridge between the first and final installments, this felt very satisfying in its own right. Every moment between these characters felt like it was building towards something, whether through the growing magic of their Heartspring bond or the hardships that brought them closer.

Nothing felt like filler when even the smallest interactions contributed to how far their relationship had come. And while this book certainly lays the groundwork for the final installment (ESPECIALLY WITH THAT ENDING?!?!), it never felt like it existed solely to set up what comes next.

And yet it also left me desperate for more... So, SO desperate...!

♦️ Daddy Hearst, the supporting cast, and THAT ending ♦️
I MISSED THIS MAN. He’s so funny, so clever, and such a fantastic presence whenever he appears. I loved every single scene involving him, especially his interactions with Briony. Also, someone PLEASE give this man one minute of peace. He deserves it. And Serena! I absolutely adore her, too, and I particularly love the two of them together, both in present day and in the flashbacks.

Then there’s Veronika Mallow... I have SO MANY THOUGHTS I cannot say, but I’m incredibly intrigued by her, her motivations, and some of the mysteries surrounding her... actions. I have theories. And I am absolutely dying to find out whether any of them are correct. Especially after that epilogue. Because WHAT WAS THAT ENDING?

♦️ An utterly addictive masterpiece of a sequel ♦️
I absolutely adored this book. The romance, the characters, the flashbacks, the pacing, the mysteries, the emotional moments, the banter, the yearning...

Every chapter left me desperate for the next, every revelation made it impossible to stop reading, and I was so completely consumed by this story that the rest of the world might as well have ceased to exist.

I laughed, I nearly cried, I swooned, I spent an unreasonable amount of time internally screaming and squealing about Toven Hearst, and I devoured the entire thing in less than a day. Like, actually, literally less than 24 hours.

An absolutely addictive masterpiece of a sequel!

And now, if someone could kindly explain how I’m supposed to wait for book three when book two hasn’t even been released yet, that would be great.

Let me start with what I loved! Julie Soto is incredible at writing tension. I really, really loved his flashback chapters (except for 1 thing I'll get to...) and thought those chapters in particular had some of the best writing I've seen in this series yet. I also appreciated how the world was expanded upon. We didn't just stay in one place, and so we got a better understanding of the world and the politics beyond Eversun and Bomard.

Again, I appreciated how the stakes were raised and the worldbuilding was fleshed out. However, I think that also led to some plot holes that I couldn't stop thinking about. Warning that these venture into very mild spoiler territory.
1) Toven's power is revealed and it makes him (and therefore Briony) EXTREMELY overpowered. Since they end up doing a mutual bond, they get to use and share each other's "special" power—Toven's is insanely powerful for both healing and harming. Briony's has the Rosewood golden shield which renders them fully invincible when used. There are no clear limitations to either of these powers either. This makes it very unclear as to why they don't use these powers more in dire situations, especially as it involves Mallow (whose sudden rise to power is still confusing to me as nobody seems to actually like her).
2) Toven and Briony. There was a lot more spice in this book, which isn't necessarily my preference. That said, Julie Soto is wonderful at writing these scenes in a way that keeps me engaged rather than wanting to skim. I often find myself getting bored with spicy scenes after the first one, but that wasn't the case in VoH. But their romance really circled around it this book to the point where it started to feel like they were simply consumed by their lust for each other more than anything else. Secondly, through Toven’s flashbacks, we learn that he was deeply in love with her back in school. I could understand a crush, but framing his feelings as love made their entire relationship feel a little too much like insta-love/lust for my taste. I just didn’t feel like they knew each other well enough for his feelings to have developed into something already so profound. Again, much of this comes down to personal preference, as I’m generally very anti-insta-love.

Ultimately, I think a lot of my notes come down to personal preference, and I still really enjoyed reading this book. Julie Soto has such a talent for writing tension, and even when I found myself questioning certain plot points, I was still invested in the story. I'm very much looking forward to seeing how she brings everything together in the final book!
