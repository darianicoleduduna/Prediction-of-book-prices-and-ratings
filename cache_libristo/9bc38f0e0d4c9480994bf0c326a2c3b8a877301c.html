The cutthroat school drama light novels that inspired a manga adaptation and a spin-off series starring fan-favorite character Horikita Suzune (both also from Seven Seas)–and don’t miss the anime!

As Ayanokouji and his classmates begin their second year at the Advanced Nurturing High School, they’re greeted by a fresh gauntlet of exams and a fresh batch of rather unique first-year students. They’ll have to get to know each other quickly, because the first special exam pairs the first-years with the second-years on a written test—with only the second-years facing expulsion if their team performs poorly! Worse yet? It seems one of the new first-years is also from the White Room. Can Ayanokouji avoid expulsion while sussing out the identity of this hidden foe?

This volume was both interesting and boring.

The main event where I was interested to see what is going to happen next is the after effect of the Unanimous Special Exam. I also mentioned this already in the last volume that I was excited to see how everyone, especially Horikita will deal with it. Unfortunately, many people lost faith in Horikita as she broke a promise and used her power for self-serving reasons, but I believe that she can still turn the table around. Yes, Kushida and Haruka are both hard to crack, but Horikita was also hard to make her change her way of thinking and yet here we are with a more mature Horikita who is willing to take risks not just for herself but for her classmates. It was also once more shown how much each one in Horikita's class matured. I was once more happy to see how Sudou fought against his inner demons to not start a fight with someone again and this time Onodera helped him, which was unusual but very much needed in the end.

Lots of things were also uncertain in this volume. It gave me mixed feelings. Some of them were really bothering me, especially the one with Sakayanagi where she was put in a spot I never imagined her to be through a twisted way of wording things. (Yes, I talk about chapter 7). But there was also a positive aspect to it as I knew that some of them will be brought back up in the future volumes, for instance the ending of this volume which foreshadows the next big issue for Horikita's class or how Sakura Airi did something last minute before she got expelled which makes one wonder what it was and why Ayanokouji accepted to pay for it in her stead. Actually, since something like this also happened in a different volume already my mind immediately went back to that knife, but I'm certain that whatever it was that Sakura wanted to buy must be important for later otherwise it wouldn't have been a big secret.

Ayanokouji also has a new approach to supporting his class, which also foreshadows his now revealed plan for the future of Horikita's class. Somehow I want to see his plan blossom, but at the same time I don't want this to actually happen as it would mean that his relationship with a certain someone would change and thereof also be never the same again, which is a shame as I really enjoyed when they were seen together.

Wang-san might not be a major character, but I was happy to see that another illustration of her was included for this volume. She definitely needs more characters development, but until then I will curiously watch her performs.

Also another character which isn't mentioned that often (but probably more important in the plot than Wang-san so far) is Ibuki. I'm actually quite neutral to her as I don't really dislike her but I also don't really get excited to see her or something. But I wanted to mention her this time as she has a way of thinking which matches my way of thinking, which was a bit surprising as I didn't expect that a character so different from my own personality would share the same thought pattern. I'm not really cautious when meeting new people, but I also have some suspicion on people who are too good natured, it just feels too perfect than being true. Not just in fictional stories like Classroom of the Elite but also in real life, there are more than enough people who pretend to be innocent and all kind but deep down they aren't that nice. Wouldn't say everyone who looks kind from the outside is evil or something, but some are and I think unless one knows that person completely one can't really be sure if it's just a performance or their true self. (By the way, same goes for people who act though but are vulnerable deep down and so on).

I also wanted to talk about Amasawa Ichika. Even though she is a diva and I really liked her when her character was introduced, I see myself being a bit dissatisfied how often her underwear is a big role by all her appearances. I know that she has a flirty side, which is probably her way of manipulating others, but she knows that it wouldn't work on Ayanokouji but proceeds to do it anyway. + she did the same thing a couple of times now that it starts to just lose meaning. (She also exaggerated with the flirting this time, which made me feel a bit frustrated about her actions). The same goes for her reason for appearing in Ayanokouji's room, it also just repeats itself which is a pity as I thought that Amasawa could be an interesting character and potentially also an enemy. Since she isn't so long part of the story and there is also year 3 left to read, she might not repeat everything over and over again and actually does something else which might be important to progress the plot.

Now I will explain what parts I deemed boring for this volume. First of all, the special exam this time around which was the sport festival wasn't as interesting as I initially thought. Perhaps it's 'cause in the first year there was also a sport festival and even though it changed a bit since visitors were now present and the new first year students weren't there before, but I saw myself being bored by it. A huge factor for this was that Ayanokouji did not take part in the sport festival and that the reactions from other students to it weren't like I expected. The only part I enjoyed at least a little bit was that one match with Sudou and Onodera as Sudou also grew more mature in that moment.

Another scene I didn't like was probably the whole seventh chapter as Sakayanagi was treated in a way I never thought I would see in the official works. It felt like some fanart stuff, especially how purposely the words were twisted in a way to give room for misunderstandings. That felt unfair for Sakayanagi as she was portrayed previously as a strong independent leader who always keeps her composure and now she felt like nothing a completely different person. I do understand what the purpose of all this was, but I think it could have also been done differently with the same outcome in the end without having to do it in such a wrong way.

Este volumen empieza con la clase lidiando con las consecuencias del examen especial de unanimidad, en el que sorprendentemente solo la clase de Horikita elimino a un estudiante, ni Ryuuen se atrevio a tanto.

Nagumo sigue con su tactica de hostilidad a Ayanouji, lo que no sabe es que Kiyotaka no tiene sentimientos que el pueda pisotear.

Este volumen gira en torno al Festival deportivo de la escuela, pero también se empiezan los preparativos para el Festival Cultural, en donde la clase de Horikita tendra un puesto de Maid Coffe.

Horikita gracias a la ayuda de Ibuki, logra que Kushida decida coperar con la clase.

La relación de Ayanokouji y Kei se vuelve más estrecha, aunque no me queda claro que tanto, tengo indicios, pero necesito pruebas.

El enfrentamiento entre el antiguo grupo Ayanokouji y Horikita fue un buen momento, porque me encanto que Ayanokouji no se haya limitado y haya hablado libremente sobre lo que paso con la expulsión de Airi.

Otra sorpresa fue enterarme de Ayanokouji y Ryuuen tienen la misma fecha de nacimiento, me habria encantado ver ese cumpleaños doble.

Algo que no me esperaba fue el acuerdo de coperación entre la clase de Horikita con la clase de Ryuuen, y esa frase de Ryuuen "Ayanokouji es la arma más letal de Suzune, por así decirlo. Si presumes con una pistola ante un hombre desarmado, no es de extrañar que diga tantas palabrerías", fue un momento soberbio.

Me parecio tierno que Kei se preocupara y tratara de hacer algo por la perdida que tubo Kiyotaka de su grupo de amigos.

Me destrosa ver la depresión de Nagumo, no es santo de mi devoción, pero entiendo el vacio que debe sentir al no poder enfrentar a Ayanokouji.

El Festival deportivo se llevo acabo con la ausencia de Ayanokouji, debido a que acepto la propuesta del director Sakayanagi de ausentarse, para evitar que entre los invitados esten hombres de su padre que lo obliguen a abandonar la escuela a la fuerza.

Me gusto la escena de Ayanokouji con Sakayanagi en el cuarto, avaces siento que a Sakayanagi le gusta romanticamente Ayanokouji, pero aun no puedo estar segura, porque llegar al extremo de ausentarse sabiendo que eso determinaria que su clase pierda, no se hace por cualquiera. Asimismo la idea que le expuso Ayanokouji a Sakayanagi sobre su meta de demostrar que la educación en la Habitación Blanca esta lejos de ser perfecta, me sorprendio, ya que pensaba que su unica motivación era pasar un tiempo normal como estudiante, aunque conociendolo no sabria que es exactamente lo que esta detras de sus palabras.

El Festival Deportivo concluyo con el 1er lugar para la clase de Horikita y el 2do lugar para la clase de Ryuuen.

La declaración final de Ayanokouji me dejo perpleja, ¿AYANOKOUJI PLANEA DEJAR LA CLASE DE HORIKITA? es como si los conduciera al crecimeinto, para ponerse él como el jefe final que hay que derrotar, lo cual va de la mano con la idea que le expuso a Sakayanagi, pero con todo eso, no creo que ni en mil años logren vencer a Ayanokouji, por lo que me intriga cual es su objetivo final, aunque él dice que se encuentra en una contradición entre saber que no podran vencerlo y querer que lo hagan.

Ayanokoji is getting more and more attention as days go on. The best part of the book was definitely the meeting between Horikita and Ryueen. It was also kinda funny. Kushida and Horikita's part was just childish and kinda cringe. Ibuki's the GOAT. Best girl ever! ( maybe after Kei ig). Talking about Kei, well Ayanokoji and Kei are certainly progressing well. Also liked how her character is getting much needed development ( Should I call it that though is the question?)

The main sports festival was whatever, didn't get much excitement reading that. Honestly, it felt like a chore than pleasure. The volleyball event and tennis event were the two most prominent ones, but those felt okay at best. Didn't enjoy much.

The Arisu and Ayanokoji part was kinda creepy. Ayanokoji always does that to get the advantage. Sure, they didn't do 'that' but still, it's a bit concerning. Should've done something else. It just feels off.

"There was a hurdle blocking her path ahead, one that was too tall for her to jump over with her own level of ability."

Honestly, I enjoyed reading about how the class is currently and reading people's reactions. It was so much better than the whole event. Which was lacklustre if I'm being honest with myself. That being said, this volume leaves the reader with a lot of unanswered questions. So, I'm excited to read the next volumes.
