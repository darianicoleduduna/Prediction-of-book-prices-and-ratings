He's a boy who's lost. She's girl who might just be the home he's never had.

The second born son in a broken marriage, Joey Lynch has spent a lifetime picking up the pieces of a family unravelling. When his older brother Darren skips town, twelve-year-old Joey finds himself thrown into the role of protector to his younger siblings and mother. Plagued by self-loathing, and furious with the world, he grapples with teenage life, his unwavering sense of duty to his family, while balancing precariously close to a life of addiction that threatens to swallow him whole. The only light in his sea of darkness is his boss's firecracker of a daughter who refuses to back down. A girl who just so happens to be his classmate.

Aoife Molloy has never been backwards in coming forward. Her bubbly and self-assured nature has never led her astray. Until her first day of secondary school when she collides with a hot-headed boy who sparks a burning curiosity inside of her that only grows stronger by the day.

Falling into a complicated friendship with her father's apprentice, Aoife works to unravels the secrets around Joey Lynch's life - while he desperately fights to keep her out.

About the author

Chloe Walsh is the NYT and USA Today bestselling author of The Boys of Tommen series, which exploded in popularity on TikTok, Goodreads, and Amazon. She has been writing and publishing New Adult and Adult contemporary romance for mature audiences for over a decade. Her Tommen series has been translated into multiple languages around the world, finding bestselling success in several countries. Chloe focuses heavily on the mental health dynamics of her characters, shining a light on important, real-life topics that are close to her heart. Heavily influenced by her own real life experiences, she pens tearjerking, and often harrowing narratives, giving another perspective to, often, unspoken everyday issues. Chloe hails from West Cork, Ireland, where she continues to reside with her family.

It’s a tale where pain dances with hope, and love isn’t just a feeling but a lifeline, pulling them from the depths of their brokenness. This is a story that reminds us healing isn’t linear, and sometimes, the most profound transformations come from letting someone in to see the parts of us we hide the most.

🤍Characters:

♥ Joey Lynch – The boy who carried the weight of the world on his shoulders, who wore his bruises like armor and loved with a fierceness that defied everything. Joey’s story broke me. His struggles with addiction, family dysfunction, and self-worth were written with such raw honesty that I felt every pang of his pain. He wasn’t just a character; he was real, flawed, and unforgettable.
“I’m not a good person, Molloy. But for you, I’ll try to be better.”

♥ Aoife Molloy – Aoife, the queen. Hats off to her patience, her strength, and her unwavering love for Joey. “The woman you are, Aoife, deserves every ounce of happiness this world can give you.” Her unwavering faith in Joey, her quiet strength, and her ability to see the light in him when no one else could—my heart swells just thinking about her. Hats off to you, Aoife, for loving fiercely, for staying when it was easier to leave, and for fighting for the boy who needed you the most. 😭 While Joey’s world was chaos, Aoife was his constant.
“You’re my calm in the storm, Molloy. My safe place.”

🤍Their Love– The love story between Joey and Aoife was raw, messy, and achingly beautiful. “You’re not just my saving grace, Molloy. You’re my saving everything.” This isn’t a fairytale romance. It’s a battlefield of emotions, where scars and imperfections only deepen their connection. Watching them navigate the storms together—whether it was Aoife grounding Joey in his darkest moments or Joey proving his love in ways words could never express—was soul-stirring.
“I’ll never stop choosing you, Joey Lynch. Even when it hurts, I’ll choose you.”


The bad-boy-meets-good-girl trope is elevated here with so much angst and depth. The chemistry was electric, the banter was golden, and the vulnerable moments between them were enough to leave me sobbing.
“You don’t fix me, Aoife. You make me want to fix myself.”




🤍*Family Dynamics:

The Lynch family dynamics add another layer of complexity to the story. Joey’s fierce protectiveness toward his siblings is both heartwarming and devastating. He’s a boy turned into a man too soon, forced to shield his family from their shared struggles.

"He gave up his dreams to save theirs, but who would save him?"


Joey’s relationship with his younger brother and the moments of solace they find in each other provide glimmers of hope in a story steeped in despair.

“I’ll break myself before I let the world touch them.”



🤍Themes and Writing Style:

Chloe Walsh doesn’t shy away from difficult topics like addiction, mental health, and trauma. Her writing is unflinchingly honest, immersing you in Joey’s pain while still offering moments of hope. The pacing is deliberate, allowing readers to feel every high and low with visceral clarity.

"Every scar told a story, every wound a battle he survived."


The angst in Saving 6 is palpable, but it’s the resilience of the characters that leaves a lasting impression. This isn’t a story about perfect endings; it’s about fighting to keep going even when the odds are stacked against you.

-Matching Soulmate Scars: Joey scarring himself to carry Aoife with him forever? I’m crying all over again. “This scar means I’m yours, Aoife. Always.”

-Joey’s Vulnerability: “I don’t deserve you, but I’ll spend my life trying to be the man you think I can be.”

-Sibling Love: The quiet moments between Joey and his siblings—especially the sacrifices he made—broke me.

-Aoife’s Faith in Joey: “You’re not your mistakes, Joey. You’re more than your past.”

-Joey’s confession: “I don’t deserve you, Molloy, but I’ll spend my life trying to be the man you think I can be.”

-The Banter and Best-Friendship: The banter between Joey and Aoife was chef’s kiss ✍🏻. It added so much levity to the story, and their solid friendship made their romance even more special.
“You think I don’t notice you, Molloy? You’re the only thing I see.”











🤍What Fell Short:

♥ Joey’s pushing Aoife away: That said, what frustrated me was Joey’s unnecessary rudeness toward Aoife at times. His actions—like hurting her by sleeping with someone else—felt cruel. I do get that they weren’t committed at the time, but it still left a sour taste knowing what she feels towards him. Yet, despite these flaws, he was a deeply compelling character, and his journey was worth rooting for.

♥ Author’s Style: While the story had so much potential, Chloe Walsh’s writing style didn’t fully work for me. She tends to drag things out with long conversations and repetitive scenes, which made the book feel unnecessarily long. This was an issue in her previous two books as well (I’ll be reviewing those soon), so it seems to be a recurring pattern.

♥ Pacing: The pacing was uneven, with some sections dragging while others felt rushed.


🤍Why It Resonates:

This book is not just about love; it’s about survival. It’s about finding the light in the darkest corners of life, about loving someone not despite their flaws, but because of them. Joey and Aoife’s story reminds us that love isn’t perfect, but it’s powerful.
“I’m not afraid of the dark, Aoife. I’m afraid of what I’ll do when the lights go out.”





🤍Final Thoughts:

~Rating -3.5/5 stars

Saving 6 is a masterful blend of heartbreak and hope, wrapped in a story that feels achingly real. Joey’s journey will leave you shattered, but it will also remind you of the strength it takes to survive. If you’re a fan of emotionally charged stories with deeply flawed but beautifully human characters, this book will resonate with you.

"Some people weren’t born to be saved. Some were born to save everyone else>"
