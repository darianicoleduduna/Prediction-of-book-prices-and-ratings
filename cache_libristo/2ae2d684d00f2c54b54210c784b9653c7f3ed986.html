The stars called us.
The Veil closed at our backs.
And now death holds us in its clutches.

We were torn from life before our time, a Savage King, his queen, a betrayed son, a wronged brother, a scorned wife and so many more. We watch those we left behind as they struggle against the grip of the stars, the answers to so many of their questions within our grasp and yet out of their reach.
But what if we could find a way to answer their pleas? What if we could find a way to help them from this side of the divide? And what price would such a thing come at?

*This is a bridging book which takes place beyond The Veil (in the land of the dead) during the events of book 8 in the Zodiac Academy series. It should be read after book 8 to avoid spoilers for that book and holds the answer to what takes place for the characters who are held within the clutches of death by this point in the story, as well as vital clues to the conclusion of this epic series.*

I read this book only because it was on KU, and I was ready to skim and be disappointed, so it’s not a surprise that I am only giving this book 2 stars.

This book happens simultaneously with Sorrow and Starlight, so we get this extra 400 pages of stuff happening in parallel, beyond the Veil. As if the 15 POVs in Sorrow and Starlight were not enough, we have an extra 5 (or maybe more?) POVs in this one (I honestly don’t know how many POVs there were in the previous book, but it was too much). I liked seeing Azriel, Hail, and Marissa together, but I would have been happy with a 150-page novella, instead of a 400-page full book.

We see the same events and read the same conversations, but from a dead character’s POV, so it adds nothing to the plot or the characters. It was recycled content, but the authors tried to give it a new life by having us see it through another POV. I don’t need to “hear” Tory and Caleb’s conversations through Darius’ ears to know how he would feel about it, nor do I need to see Orion’s torture through his father’s eyes to know how Azriel must feel.

We get to meet new characters, but besides Azriel, Hail, and Marissa, I don't care about them at all. Worse, we got an awful lot of their backstories through flashbacks, and I struggle a lot with flashbacks in general, so it was tedious. We got so many details about characters that we didn’t know and that I didn’t care about. It was so long; I admit that I skimmed a lot of the flashbacks because they were never-ending at one point.

Darius’ plotline didn’t have a lot of impact, since we already knew what would happen with Tory and him, so I was mostly bored and just wanted him to get back to her and the Heirs. I was hoping to see more of his reunion with the Heirs since this part was almost absent from the 8th book, but it wasn’t in this one either, sadly.

Overall, this book was exactly what I expected: repetitive and pointless, but at least it was free with my KU subscription!
