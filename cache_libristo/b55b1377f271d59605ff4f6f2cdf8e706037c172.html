About the author

John Grisham

475 books94.7k followers
John Grisham is the author of more than fifty consecutive #1 bestsellers, which have been translated into nearly fifty languages. His recent books include Framed, Camino Ghosts and The Exchange: After the Firm.

Grisham is a two-time winner of the Harper Lee Prize for Legal Fiction and was honored with the Library of Congress Creative Achievement Award for Fiction.

When he's not writing, Grisham serves on the board of directors of the Innocence Project and of Centurion Ministries, two national organizations dedicated to exonerating those who have been wrongfully convicted. Much of his fiction explores deep-seated problems in our criminal justice system.

John lives on a farm in central Virginia.

Which meant that I went into this one blind. Ready to let this one simply unfold.

As an espionage thriller, Grisham has readers curious from page one about what is happening, why it is happening, and who is behind it all. This is a slow-burn setup, in which readers are initially uncertain about what is going on, and how it is affecting everyone involved. The middle rambles before the action accelerates towards a rather rushed ending.

There is a tense moral dilemma that Grisham puts before readers: what would we do for the ones we love?

With uneven pacing, the story felt a bit mechanical – like cookbook plotting, with Grisham following a recipe without adding any unique flavor. While it held my attention and curiosity, it never quite captured my full interest. A decent thriller, but not one of Grisham’s more memorable efforts.

I can proudly say that for years and years I’ve been devouring John Grisham novels, and every single time I open one of his books, I know I’m about to experience storytelling from one of the true GOATs of modern literature. It’s the same feeling I get whenever I pick up a Stephen King novel—there’s an immediate sense of trust because these authors have consistently set the bar so high that every new release arrives with sky-high expectations attached to it.

Well, this one didn’t just meet those expectations—it grabbed them, threw them into a speeding vehicle, and drove them straight off a cliff.

This breathtaking, hyperventilating, pulse-racing thriller had my heart pounding so hard that I’m convinced it raised my cholesterol levels from stress alone. My anxiety was operating at Olympic levels while reading it, but honestly, who am I to complain? If you want the rush of a truly great thriller, you have to be willing to surrender yourself to the adrenaline.

The story launches at full speed from the very first pages. What begins as a picture-perfect wedding quickly transforms into every parent’s worst nightmare. The Clayburn and Tanner families gather to celebrate the marriage of Chelsea and Paul. The lavish wedding is largely funded by Chelsea’s family, thanks to her father, Dr. Lowell Clayburn, a successful orthopedic surgeon whose smart investments have provided considerable wealth and security.

After the wedding, the newlyweds leave for a dream honeymoon in Paris. Chelsea enthusiastically documents every moment of their romantic getaway on social media, sharing photos, updates, and even snapshots of the spectacular meals they enjoy. Then suddenly… nothing.

No messages.

No photos.

No updates.

Complete silence.

The abrupt disappearance sends alarm bells ringing, and soon the horrifying truth emerges: Chelsea and Paul have been kidnapped by an organized criminal network. The families are contacted separately and ordered to pay enormous ransoms—millions of dollars—in exchange for the safe return of their children.

At first, it appears to be a terrible but straightforward case of international extortion. Pay the money, get the victims back, and try to move on with your lives.

But nothing is ever that simple.

The Clayburns have the financial resources to act quickly, while the Tanners are prepared to sacrifice everything they own, including their home and future security, to save their son. Yet as the investigation unfolds, it becomes frighteningly clear that this is far bigger than a typical kidnapping scheme. Beneath the surface lurks something much darker, more organized, and infinitely more dangerous than anyone initially imagined.

From that moment on, the story becomes an absolutely wild ride.

Every chapter raises the stakes. More agencies become involved. More secrets emerge. More players enter the game. The tension escalates relentlessly, making it nearly impossible to put the book down. The suspense becomes almost unbearable at times, leaving you perched on the edge of your seat, mentally screaming at the pages as events spiral in increasingly shocking directions.

This is intense.

This is nerve-shredding.

This is the kind of thriller that has you checking how many pages remain while simultaneously dreading and needing to know what happens next.

The rescue operation itself is masterfully executed and genuinely jaw-dropping. Every development increases the uncertainty, making you question who can be trusted, who is pulling the strings, and whether the victims will ever truly escape the nightmare surrounding them. The constant sense of danger and unpredictability keeps the pages flying.

One of my absolute favorite sequences in the novel involves the church scene, where everyone waits anxiously as the clock continues ticking toward a crucial moment. The tension in that section was almost unbearable. I could practically feel my pulse speeding up with every passing minute. The various operations carried out by investigators and agents throughout the story were equally gripping and often left my heart lodged somewhere in my throat.

What impressed me most was how effectively the novel balanced its relentless action with genuine emotional weight. The characters aren't simply pieces moving across a thriller chessboard. Their fear, desperation, hope, and determination feel authentic, which makes every twist hit even harder.

Overall, this is a phenomenal thriller packed with escalating tension, nonstop suspense, and enough surprises to keep even seasoned thriller readers guessing. It feels somewhat different from Grisham’s more traditional legal-centered work, yet that willingness to explore new territory pays off beautifully. The cinematic nature of the premise practically begs for a streaming adaptation, and I would happily binge-watch every episode.

Fast-paced, addictive, emotionally engaging, and impossible to put down, this is exactly the kind of thriller that reminds readers why John Grisham remains one of the greatest storytellers working today.

A huge thank you to NetGalley and Doubleday Books for sharing this impeccable, action-packed thriller with me in exchange for my honest thoughts, which I was delighted to provide.

The French Illusion is an upcoming John Grisham page-turner. Somewhat straying from his courtroom legal dramas, this one escalates into a multi-agency terrorist investigation involving the FBI, CIA, and French Intelligence. It starts with what should be a lovely honeymoon in Paris for Paul and Chelsea Tanner. But when they are kidnapped and held for ransom money, it quickly spirals into a high-stakes espionage thriller.
At first, it seems as if the young couple will be returned. Chelsea's family pays up, and she is found safely. Yet even after Paul's family follows the same steps in wiring the money, he remains kidnapped and tortured by these unknown forces. Finally, the families resolve to call in the big guys, bringing in the FBI and CIA. Here, we see this is not an isolated incident; two other young couples had been taken in the same pattern within the past three years. All wives were released relatively unharmed, but the husbands remain missing.
Making the connection that each husband had worked for the government, we see that our criminal wants revenge for how the US had treated prisoners. Now that they know the pattern of who the unnamed group is targeting, the CIA recruits a lawyer that formerly worked with interrogations to act as bait. The plan is to fake a marriage and honeymoon to Paris to draw out the perpetrators. The story launches forth in short chapters, rotating setting and characters, culminating to a dramatic end.
This novel was short, though much of it was delegated to the build up. Our lawyer acting in disguise was not introduced into at least half-way through, making it not seem like the central goal of the novel as described in the synopsis. Still, it was gripping to see how all the agencies had to find ways to work together and navigate all the orders of command. More so, we get to see into the lens of our criminal, making the crimes all the more real.
I found that the dialogue in a Grisham book is so distinct. He writes well, using the character's discussions to propel the story. He writes in third person, but this allows him to put forth each character's take on the events. The tension is built as we watch various intelligent people in the US and France race to outsmart intelligent criminals. Though, our ending was a bit lackluster given how anticipatory the buildup was. I did want to see more of the fallout, but Grisham kept it brief. Overall, this was a solid read, worthy for the genre.

College football is in full gear, racing towards the 12-team playoff, and the winter holidays are not far off. For me, it’s time to open the new John Grisham novel, “The French Illusion”, and let it take over my weekend in an absolute submission that paid off greatly.

In “The French Illusion” Grisham is momentarily stepping away from his comfortable world of practicing law as his primary focus and driver in the storytelling (yes, there is a character who is a lawyer, but his professional life has very little to do with the story). This time, he tries to change things up with more of a mystery driven story that develops into quite a political thriller. The best way that I can describe this one is to break it into two parts – the first half feels very much like a Harlan Coben urban thriller and the second half turns into a serious David Baldacci / Tom Clancy political thriller. I mean that as the biggest compliment that I can say about all of those authors. Still, it is very much a Grisham told story that works very well in a lot of ways.

“The French Illusion” begins with the honeymoon of Chelsea and Paul Tanner, an American couple who just celebrated their wedding and have arrived in Paris, France for their long-awaited honeymoon. Chelsea is looking forward to visiting the museums and works of art while Paul can’t wait to see the World War II memorial sites. Both appreciate the opportunity for wine tasting.

However, things go bad very quickly for the newly married couple. While on a day trip to the French countryside, the unthinkable happens. Out of the blue, the two are kidnapped by unknown assailants who immediately separate and leave them alone in a dark location.

The questions begin immediately for the readers. Who would want to kidnap them, and why? Is this the first time the kidnappers had done something like this, or have they done this before? Will they send a ransom demand to their parents? And if so, what will they require their families to pay for their safe return? Or do the kidnappers have other intentions for their victims?

At this point, I really don’t want to share any more information that hint or give away any of the possible ways the storylines could go or specific details. This is a book best experienced without any clues or fore-knowledge. Go in blind and enjoy the suspenseful thrill ride fresh and untainted. If you’re like me, half the fun with this type of Grisham novel is trying to guess what comes next, and that is one of the most fun experiences that reading can provide. Enjoy it!

As much as I’ve been reading Grisham for many years – all the way back to his first one – this one was one of his best. Taking some risks and expanding his storylines beyond the various underdog lawyers taking on the evil corporations and focusing on more global political themes and plotting pays off extremely well in this intense, nerve-wracking, rollercoaster ride of a page-turner. I honestly believe that changing things up in such an unexpected way, brought a freshness and edge to my reading experience that reminded me a lot of how I felt reading his first novel, “The Firm” many years ago. I literally had moments where the goosebumps were breaking out.

This one had all of the Grisham strengths that readers have come to expect and rely on from him. His masterful storytelling – smooth and fluid in his prose, engaging in his ability to capture the reader’s attention and emotional commitment – captures you from the first page and he never let’s go. You are along for the whole ride all the way to the end. He almost never takes his foot off the gas as the storylines develop, surprises elevate the tension and conflicts, and you seriously cannot put the dang book down because you feel the danger the characters are facing on a deeply personal and empathetic level. Their pain is your pain, and their outcomes are your outcomes. This is one of those pulse-pounding I-must-find-out-how-things-turn-out-regardless-of-how-late-I must-stay-up-to-find-out. I swear, he could even write a cookbook, and I would most likely read it with enthusiasm that I didn’t know that I had.

Like I mentioned earlier, the first half of the book has a very Harlan Coben feel as the story unfolds from the perspective of the families of the kidnapped victims. It feels very personal and you feel for what they and the victims are going through. Who the kidnappers are and why they are committing this crime is not given away easily, amplifying the tension and leaving the reader to speculate in much the same ways that the family members are doing.

Then, the second half of the book shifts and turns into a cross between a David Baldacci secret government agency spy adventure and old school Tom Clancy politically charged thriller wrapped into one. This may feel like newer territory for Grisham, but he’s in command of this novel and in full control all the way through. I, for one, am glad for his creative choices and excellent delivery of them, especially a strong emotional payoff that was worthy of the 300-page plus build-up.

My only constructive complaint would be that the last climactic 20 pages or so read more like a screenplay – a bit choppy and too thin. Although it didn’t completely take away from the impact of the ending, I would have preferred a bit more content than what was on the pages. It felt a bit too edited down for my taste.

Overall, I absolutely enjoyed “The French Illusion” and the enlarging of Grisham’s storytelling palette. He took a calculated risk that paid off greatly in my opinion. I give it a 4.5 out of 5-stars (rounded up on Goodreads) and would include it in my top ten list of favorite Grisham novels.

Here’s hoping that you enjoy it too.

⭐ 💤 A Thriller Without a Single Thrill

There was a time when picking up a John Grisham novel was practically a guarantee of an enjoyable reading experience. His books were a significant part of my earlier reading life, and I suspect nostalgia has occasionally made me more forgiving of his shortcomings than I should have been. Last year, I gave The Widow four stars, although I eventually began questioning whether my enthusiasm reflected the quality of the book or simply my familiarity with its author. Reading Camino Island this year only reinforced those doubts. As my reading tastes have matured, I have become increasingly critical of implausible plotting, superficial characterization, and writing that fails to evolve. Unfortunately, The French Illusion exemplifies nearly everything I have grown to dislike about Grisham's style. His reliance on short, choppy sentences, simplistic vocabulary, repetitive patterns, and uninspired dialogue feels remarkably similar to the writing I encountered in his novels decades ago. While that approach may have appealed to me in the 1990s, I now find it increasingly tedious. Nostalgia should never give an author a free pass, regardless of how much I once admired their work.

For a novel presented as an espionage thriller, The French Illusion is astonishingly devoid of both espionage and thrills. The plot is so thoroughly implausible that virtually every development strained my credibility, from the terrorists and their elaborate kidnapping operation to the CIA's increasingly absurd involvement. Yet the most glaring weakness is the characterization. Grisham introduces an endless procession of names, occupations, family histories, and government officials, but almost none of these individuals possesses a memorable personality or sufficient depth to warrant any emotional investment. They exist almost like background characters in a video game, appearing when the plot requires them and disappearing without leaving an impression. I could not have cared less whether Paul Tanner, the other hostages, Stuart Nash, or Rae Claire Russo survived. Even the supposed villains were utterly forgettable. Without compelling characters, believable danger, or meaningful suspense, the narrative became an exhausting exercise in indifference. The accompanying prequel, The Law Student, proved equally unnecessary, offering background information about Stuart Nash that could easily have been condensed into a few paragraphs of this novel.

The conclusion only compounded my frustration. The entire affair feels contrived, emotionally hollow, and thoroughly undeserved. I would have welcomed a darker, grittier conclusion that acknowledged the seriousness of the events and introduced some genuine consequences. Instead, everything falls conveniently into place, as though Grisham were unwilling to deviate from a familiar formula. I finished The French Illusion feeling neither excitement nor satisfaction, merely relief that it was over. Despite this disappointment, I know I will probably pick up his next novel, partly out of habit and partly out of curiosity. However, my expectations have changed considerably. An author's reputation might persuade me to open a book, but it can no longer persuade me to overlook its failures. The French Illusion was a completely forgettable novel from an author whose work I once found unforgettable.
