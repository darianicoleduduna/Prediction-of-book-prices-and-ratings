I cannot express how disappointed I am. There is genuinely nothing worse than the anticipation — the electric excitement after an incredible first book — only to be met with a sequel that feels like one long stretch of filler. Brimstone is exactly that. Filler dressed up as plot, stretched within an inch of its life.

I’m going to say it plainly: don’t turn a duology into a trilogy unless you actually have the content to justify it. This book did not. At all. At least 60% of it was just “they went there, then they went there, then they went there,” without any real development or payoff. With the money being put behind this series now, this simply shouldn’t have happened. It feels rushed, padded, and frustratingly hollow.

And the side characters — far, far too many of them. Most of them serve no purpose. They appear, deliver a dramatic line, and vanish again. It’s overcrowded without adding value, and the clutter drags the story down even further.

Then there’s the narrative device that made me want to fling the book across the room:
“Then the character explained the big secret to me, and suddenly I understood everything.”
Except we don’t get the explanation. And sure, you can get away with that once. But doing it twice is pushing it. Doing it three times? Absolutely not. By that point, it feels less like mystery and more like the book is actively gatekeeping its own plot.

And honestly, can we talk about the main couple? Because good grief. They only seem to come together for the smut scenes — and while that’s fine in moderation, you need an actual relationship to go with it. Chemistry requires more than proximity and convenient darkness. Give me banter, give me emotional tension, give me something that feels like genuine connection. Brimstone gives flashes of heat and then immediately separates them again, leaving no time to build anything meaningful. It’s maddening.

And after all those pages — all the meandering, the travelling, the repetitive beats — the story has barely budged from where book one ended. The stakes haven’t escalated, the plot hasn’t advanced in any satisfying way, and the momentum that Quicksilver built so brilliantly has been completely squandered.

It pains me to say it, but this instalment was a train wreck. Not in a chaotic, enjoyable way — just a deeply disappointing derailment of what started as a strong and promising romantasy.

Two stars, and even that feels like an act of mercy

ㅤ ‍‍‍‍‍‍ ‍‍‍‍‍‍ ⋅ ☾ As for the romance….I hate to say it, but I wasn’t really feeling it. Sure, there were a few sweet scenes and some nice dialogue that I appreciated, but overall I felt strangely detached. ㅤ ‍‍‍‍‍‍ ‍‍‍‍‍‍ I was really hoping to grow more attached to the cast, especially the main characters, to feel something for them, to care. But apart from Tal, I didn’t feel any meaningful connection forming; if anything, I felt more distant from most of them by the end.

ㅤ ‍‍‍‍‍‍ ‍‍‍‍‍‍ ⋅ ☾ Ultimately, my biggest issue with this book is how severely it lacks momentum. There’s very little action, very little plot progression, and a lot of movement without purpose. ㅤ ‍‍‍‍‍‍ ‍‍‍‍‍‍ If I had to describe it in one sentence, it would be this: not necessarily bad, just painfully empty.

ㅤ ‍‍‍‍‍‍ ‍‍‍‍‍‍ ⊹ Saeris — So I just read my review of Quicksilver and apparently I didn't really like her there. This feeling didn't really change in this book either. I don't really hate her or anything, but she is just the kind of character that makes it hard for you to relate to her, because apparently everything just works out for her. She has this insane magic that always seems to bring her something new exactly at the most convenient moment, she has everyone simply liking her no matter what, and she doesn't really have a personality apart from being suddenly a very imposing person and having Kingfisher as her mate.

To me, it reads like a textbook case of a novel suffering from the dreaded second-book syndrome: too long, too hesitant, and far too comfortable lingering in place instead of moving forward.
ㅤ ‍‍‍‍‍‍ ‍‍‍‍‍‍ However, I will give credit where it is due: the ending was strong enough to hook me. It was intriguing and abrupt in the right way and finally felt like something was at stake.
