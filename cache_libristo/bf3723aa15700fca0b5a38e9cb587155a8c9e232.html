Adversary to the Villain returns us to Rennedawn for the fourth—but not final—installment in Hannah Nicole Maehrer’s Assistant to the Villain series. The assistant has now been revealed as the true holder of the Villain’s magic, but Evie has little idea how to control her newfound power. Meanwhile, a dangerous prophecy threatens the kingdom, and Evie and Trystan must navigate both its alarming implications and the lingering awkwardness surrounding the future of their relationship.

The rest of their wonderfully dysfunctional collection of family, friends, and coworkers have plenty of problems of their own. The characters split into different groups to pursue clues, confront personal challenges, and search for a way to save Evie from the wrath of the crown. Along the way, they maneuver hilariously around new romances, exchange plenty of comical banter, and place themselves and one another in increasingly ridiculous and dangerous situations.

Even with such a large cast, Maehrer gives each character enough attention to make them feel like a fully-realized person. The supporting characters are not included merely for comic relief or to play the mirror to someone else’s antics. They possess individual histories, fears, relationships, and motivations that make it easy to care about them. One particularly effective sequence places several characters inside a magical book that tests them using their own pasts. The scene advances the broader mystery while also revealing something meaningful about nearly everyone involved.

We even learn more about Alexander Kingsley, the frog whose history readers have been waiting three books to uncover. His story is only one of several long-standing mysteries explored here, although many of the answers generate entirely new questions.

That pattern defines much of Adversary to the Villain. This installment feels more like a stepping stone toward the next book than a story designed to stand firmly on its own. Readers hoping for substantial resolutions will find few of them because almost every discovery exposes another complication. Most troubling of all, the clues increasingly suggest that Evie and Trystan may bring about Rennedawn’s destruction with Evie cast as the Villain and Trystan as the hero destined to oppose her.

Em Eldridge once again narrates the audiobook, carrying listeners through its large ensemble, rapid banter, romantic tension, and fairy-tale absurdity. Although this installment leaves far more unresolved than it settles, the affection Maehrer inspires for her characters keeps the journey entertaining. Adversary to the Villain expands the cast’s histories and relationships while positioning everyone for what promises to be an even more consequential confrontation ahead.
