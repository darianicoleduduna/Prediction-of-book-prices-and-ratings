From #1 New York Times bestselling author Victoria Aveyard comes an epic adventure of love and betrayal, amid a brutal war for the high seas.

May the sea always sing for you

Cat Rose knows what it is to live in two worlds, in two times. Before—as Lady Catrine Rose of the Meridian Empire, chained to a loathsome lord with a dwindling fortune. And after—as the Widow Rose, queen of a pirate tavern in the Lyrian Sea, determined to be the master of her own fate.

But tides turn and winds change. The Meridian Empire looms on the horizon, the full weight of her navy falling on the Lyrian, bent on reclaiming the wealthy colonies and crushing the pirate republic once and for all. Ship after ship is captured, save for one, captained by a pirate who continually evades both noose and blade: the legendary Valerian Kane.

With his fearsome ship and uncanny connection to the song of the sea, Valerian is the only man who might rally the pirates who remain. The only man who threatens the Empire. And the only man Cat has ever loved, blazing in and out of her life like a star.

Desperate for victory, the Empire offers Cat a devastating choice: bring them Valerian or hang. In truth, no choice at all.

But Cat Rose is nothing if not a survivor.

A storm is rising on the Lyrian Sea—the question is, what will it leave in its wake?

before my review, i'd like to offer a disclaimer:
- i fucking love pirates and especially pirates of the caribbean
- red queen was my absolute favorite book ever as a kid. it was also the first book to ever make me cry and i can still remember the exact moment that i did
- i've always loved victoria aveyard. i've followed her and enjoyed her content for several years now

now that we've gotten my credentials out of the way.

what the fuck?



victoria, what was this? i'm not sure i've ever complained about a book more to my friends and family. it was a nightly tedtalk situation- i get home from work, i vent about how bad this book is, my mom and best friend commiserate with me over sentences that are almost as bad as the legendary line from alex aster:

"she ran like she was running from something."


i have many, many gripes with this novel, but the first one was something i picked up on within the first five chapters, victoria lacks trust in her readers. the main island was called The Continent, worldbuilding was incredibly limited or force fed down your throat, and there were times where victoria did things such as- and i'm not joking- write an entire sentence dedicated to the idea that the tally marks on the wall of a prison cell were symbolic of days counted.

really? i had no idea.

the writing style consistently got on my nerves. i couldn't make it a full chapter without setting down the book and rubbing my forehead because either a) there was a sentence or paragraph so obnoxious i couldn't quite handle it, or b) the entire tone of the chapter was genuinely just getting on my nerves (granted the chapters were quite long, which is also a complaint i had). some examples are as follows:

"His hands curled into fists- out of frustration or anger, Cat could not say."


baby those are basically the same thing

"His eyes were two bolts of silver flame..."


"She did not miss the red flash of his tongue as it ran over his golden tooth, polishing a fang."


i just threw up in my mouth

"...Long fingers uncoiling..."


"Cat knew the low neckline made her amber eyes seem warmer, her tan golden, and even her freckles more refined."


don't ask me what that means. i don't know.

"Have I been kind to you, Cat?" He asked in a low voice that made her toes curl."


jesus christ why are we talking about our toes in what i'm assuming is supposed to be a sexy conversation?



my next complaint is the characters. cat rose feels both inconsistent, boring, supposedly badass (??), stupid, and ridiculous all in one. she didn't feel at all different from the Before and After chapters, which is insane, given there's a ten year difference between the two. a lot of her traits are told to us by other characters and not actually demonstrated by cat's talents or skills.

valerian kane is exactly how he sounds. (and also he has tempest eyes and reminds cat of a shark.) brooding but apparently emotional. (with tempest eyes.) way too vulnerable, to the point that it felt less like a character flaw/trait and more like victoria just really needed him to recoil from cat or open up to her the literal same day that they met (she did the same thing, mind you. he kidnapped her and then the night after they were having a genuine, emotional discussion. i had whiplash). (did i mention he has tempest eyes? oh by the way he has tempest eyes. and he reminds cat of a shark.)

jasper fell... oh jasper fell, the character that you could have been in another book. #staringwistfullyoutthewindow. he would've been a much more interesting and less stereotypical love interest, i felt like he actually had chemistry with cat, contained a genuine personality, and, oh my god, he was actually on page for more than 30 pages of a 500 page book!! the bar is in hell, ladies and gentleman.

i won't give the side characters my time of day because all of them blended into each other and i really just could not care less about any of them.



plotwise, i have two main complaints-

1: in the beginning Before chapters, cat seems very frightened but intrigued by the pirates, but it's incredibly shallow (it's because she wants to fuck valerian kane). there's no depth to her interest in them or how much she thinks about them. if victoria had wanted to create a genuine sense of yearning in young cat, i would've been far more receptive if she had seen a female pirate who seemed very free, or thought a lot about how open and inviting the sea is, etc. not about "the boy with tempest eyes".

this book dragged on an inhumane amount. i felt like i was literally being tortured by the last chapter. the stakes were the lowest i've ever seen them in the Before chapters (because we see every character alive in the After chapters) and there are no stakes in the After chapters because nothing is fucking happening in the After chapters. i am not using hyperbole or being dramatic when i say this book has no plot. nothing happens. there is no character development. there are no exciting scenes. nothing happens.



i have a thousand more complaints but this book sucked the life out of me and i couldn't handle giving more of my attention to it.

Another day, another YA fantasy book masquerading as an adult fantasy romance.

From the beginning I had issues with the writing style in this. It was so profoundly juvenile and was both over and under edited. The repetitive words were without a doubt the worst, but her lack of skill in descriptive writing was almost just as bad.

I’m also 90% sure Aveyard has aphantasia—and I don’t mean that to be rude, but it does affect how descriptions are written. Lots of similes and metaphors are used for individual objects or landscapes, but you never actually get a full idea of where the characters are.

I noticed this in Alex Aster’s writing too (who has openly admitted to having aphantasia) and so I can only assume it’s the same case here. Scenes would be written almost as one snapshot rather than an action—like an old fashioned flip book.

I don’t want to harp on that point too much because obviously authors can’t help it if they have aphantasia, but it did lead to this book having a very clunky, disjointed style.

Victoria Aveyard also can not write 3rd person. It’s very clear she’s more comfortable in 1st, but maybe assumed an “adult” fantasy book would be taken more seriously in 3rd?

I’ve said this before, but I think a big reason people feel disconnected by 3rd person is when bad 3rd person authors write like this: “cat did this, cat said this, cat wanted this.”

It’s the mark of an unconfident, first-time 3rd person writer who feels like they need to over explain what is a character is feeling, saying, or doing in a scene because they don’t trust the reader to interpret on their own.

But arguably the worst offender was her overuse of phrases and the repetition. My god. It was atrocious. And aphantasia can’t be blamed for that.

-“Cat thought” or “she thought” was collectively used nearly 200 times. At that point, just write in 1st. You clearly want to because you’re wanting to interject 1st person inner monologue into a 3rd person pov.
-“Tempest” was used 30 times, which seems so few for how many times I felt like it came up. If I had to read “tempest eyes” one more time…
-“his namesake” was used like twice in a chapter to denote the character’s husband.

These are just three examples. There are so many more.

Descriptions for characters were slim, but repeated over and over and over again. We harp on eye color SO MUCH and get little-to-no actual depth beyond the unoriginal, YA-leaning tropey description used in every romantasy book written post-2018.

You know, “muscle feathered in his jaw,” “she felt it in her core,” “muscle made of iron that flexed beneath tanned skin.”

Same old same old.

Aveyard also does the annoying (very Lauren Roberts style I might add) of adding an italicized sentence underneath paragraphs where she wants to reader to know what the character was really thinking. This isn’t an actual quote, but it would be something like:

He looked at Cat and questioned her motives.
As he should, I’m a great liar, she thought.

Sorry, why do we need that in an adult book? Can an adult reader not pick up on subtext? Or what we (should have) established from our character’s actions previously?

Beyond the writing, story itself started good, although you can pinpoint scenes or characters taken from Pirates of the Caribbean, which did admittedly take me out of the book at times.

Another offender was the before and after chapter choice.

I loved the before chapters and wish the story was written in a linear way. I understand why she chose to do it before and after, but I was getting so annoyed at enjoying a before scene and then switching to after just to leave tension.

If you just write a good story, you don’t need to add in these cheap tension endings to a chapter in order to get the reader to stay and wait for the inevitable next “before” chapter.

All of this combined with the classic, ye old ending written in the mmc’s point of view just made me roll my eyes. This needs more editing—preferably by someone who will be honest and tell Aveyard what’s actually poorly done. They can start with these:

“Blinding rage filled Cat's body, despite her predicament.” (Despite her predicament? That means in contrast to, which doesn’t make sense here because she’s angry that someone is manhandling her, as she should be.)

“She loosed her teeth.” (The fuck does that mean?)

“‘Don't,’ she heard herself whisper.” (Just say “she whispered.” What are we doing)

“Swallowed” being used 46 times. Cut it out.


That was long-winded, sorry!

Anyways, thank you Edelweiss for a free ARC in exchange for an honest review.

If you want to pick up this book because it was sold to you as a Pirates of the Caribbean style adventure with a thrilling ten-year situation-ship between a fallen lady and a dashing pirate captain: DO NOT FUCKING BOTHER.

If you want a story about bland, unpleasant people playing cards and drinking while visiting copy-paste islands where they play more cards and drinking more alcohol than this might just be the book for you!

This book is genuinely one of the worst pieces of English literature that has ever been published.
Not only is it poorly written, badly edited, horribly paced, filled to the brim with forgettable characters, and lacking any sense of adventure, but this book is BORING.
It is DULL.
I would compare its intrigue to that of watching paint dry, but I genuinely feel that would be an insult to drying paint. Nearly 95% of this book is completely useless to the purpose of telling an engaging story. This duology ought to have been a single book as originally intended, and frankly, I’m not sure why it wasn’t. All the important information in this book could have EASILY been edited down to about three scenes worth of information, and I really do think I’ve been generous with my estimation.
This book should’ve been an email—worse actually, it should’ve been a fucking text.

This book is allegedly a romantic fantasy (or romantasy for those who find such inane genre definitions annoying), but I fear I must ask a rather pressing question, how many fantastical elements does a book require to qualify as fantasy? Does a book that has only a singular hint at having (entirely unexplained) magic in the last three percent of a book count? Does having a singular magical amulet in the final twenty pages of a 468 page book make it a fantasy? I’d say no.
But wait! Now, consider this: What if a book had practically no real romance or romantic tension and only one single kiss in the whole of it? What if all the romance happened in the ten year gap that we don’t get to see? What if the extent of the romance is our main character repeating the same description of the male main character ad nauseam in order to convince you that this ugly ass blonde man is actually hot?
Now I don’t know about you, but if I’m sold a book that is pretending to be a ROMANTIC FANTASY I would expect some goddamn ROMANCE and some fucking FANTASY, but let me know if I’m off base.

But I must give credit where credit is due. Victoria Aveyard is bold. I think it incredibly bold to sell people a $34 book that has not a single thing that the book is advertised as having.

But obviously, a book can be different than described and still be a great book. Unfortunately, Tempest is not that.

Tempest is, on a prose level alone: abysmal. The amount of descriptions that are not only uninspired to begin with, they are repeated, again and again and again. Valerian is HUGE and GOLDEN and has TEMPEST EYES, Jasper has silver eyes, the Lyrian Sea is BLUE and DEEP (and did I mention the sea is BLUE?), Cat has amber eyes that are BRIGHT. Each of these descriptions are repeated at a minimum of five times and many of them are repeated upwards of TWENTY.
And I don’t know if Victoria has some kind of synesthesia or something but she’s convinced that just naming colors is profound description. Everything is blue or green or purple or pink or orange or gray or golden or some combination of the above. What does anything look like? Who fucking knows, but it’s certainly colors!!
And the same things are described with the exact same combination of colors every single time. The sunrise is purple and pink and orange, the sunset is orange and blood red and ALWAYS ends with a flash of green (as if that isn’t a very rare phenomenon).
Someone needs to grab Aveyard by the shoulders and shake her vigorously, or at the very least tell her this is so much more to writing good description than fucking colors. If I should never see the word golden again, it will not be long enough.

But that’s not even where the prose woes end. Aveyard has developed a peculiar and distracting OBSESSION with sentence fragments. She will end sentences. And then carry them on as if she didn’t just place a period. It creates a thousand fragments that don’t function as complete thoughts. I cannot for the life of me figure out why she’s doing it. I have to guess she’s trying to force rhythm and tone, but the result is not a good tone and rhythm it’s a remarkably worse one.
She also has the strangest way with wording very simple actions. For example: there are several instances where Cat (the main character) will “turn into” the sun or the wind. Turn into? Like sure I understand that she means is turning to face or look at those things, but it sounds like she is shape shifting into the sun or the wind. It’s awkward phrasing like this that’s used multiple times per chapter. At one point, Valerian (the main love interest) steps over the back of a chair and then sits down on it? What the fuck am I supposed to be imagining? Is the chair two feet tall? I know he’s large but he has to be ridiculously composed of the longest legs of all time to make such an action work. Is he a cellar spider? I am frightened and horrified.

And god, oh god, the REPETITION. I think you’d be hard pressed to find a single sentence or notion that isn’t REPEATED multiple times. This book certainly believes you to be the stupidest amoeba upon the surface of the earth. You need not exert any brain power to read this book for if you forget a single thing (though how would you when everything is told to you seven times already?) the book will simply tell you seven times more!
This book repeats descriptions and information and straight up sentences so often I think I came away from the book stupider than I entered. I swear I could feel my brain atrophy as I read. I can only imagine what brain damage Aveyard sustained writing it.

Not to mention the writing is just childish. It’s writing style seems to have been created as though it was made to appeal to the stupidest third grader on the planet, and yet it’s got uncomfortable references to sexual topics that are the only justification for its billing as an adult book.

Now this may come as a surprise as I’ve tried very hard to impress upon you how very boring this book is, but this book actually features two timelines. One titled “After” and another titled “Before”, both are equally useless and not in the slightest are they engaging, but the real turd upon the dookie cake is that the book flops between each timeline every chapter.
And these chapters are LONG, and each progresses the either timeline forward one singular step (if even that, for many don’t actually progress the plot forward at all), and I found that by the time I finished one chapter and was thrust back into the other timeline I would be unable to even remember what was happening last time I read in this timeline. This wasn’t for lack of trying, but so forgettable is 95% of this book that trying to remember whatever inane occurrences Aveyard wrote about two chapters ago, that I really struggled. It is such a confusing and idiotic structure, I can’t even imagine what Aveyard thought she was accomplishing with her choice of it.

But enough of prose and structure, I think my brain shall melt out of my head if I ponder it any further. Let’s talk about characters, since the book has no plot, no magic, no world building, no romance, surely the characters must be entrancing, right?

Well, no. There are remarkably few of them, and only three (four, if I’m being INCREDIBLY kind) that are consistently on page and have any character traits to speak of.

Our main character is Catrine (nicknamed Latrine—I mean Cat) Rose. To put it bluntly: she’s an entitled little cunt. She’s self-centered, self-obsessed, cruel, classist, hypocritical, and one of the stupidest women I’ve ever had to read about. And listen, I love unlikable women, but usually they have some quality, some personality trait that makes them fun to root for, but Cat is a bitch and also stupid. Being stuck in her head for the entire book is comparable only to taking fifty-seven gunshots to the skull.

The main love interest, Valerian, is simply not on page for like 80% of the book. The other 20% of the book is spent describing how big and golden and muscled and how he never wears shirts. I think his descriptions sound ugly and every time he opens his mouth, he says stupid shit that made me want to shove my head in an oven. He doesn’t really have a discernible personality outside of being a stereotypical pirate and finding Cat hot.

Jasper is also here, and he’s the most interesting character in the book. He has much more chemistry with Cat than Valerian does, but he’s also clearly not the love interest and I don’t really understand why he likes Cat at all. She’s incredibly weird and classist towards him. He works for the Empire and he’s supposed to be evil, but he has more of a moral backbone than both Valerian and Cat combined.

The whole trio is immature and stupid. They have the combined brain power of a fried egg, and are constantly making juvenile jabs and attempting to convey the most basic observations as mastermind level deductions. It’s wholly quite baffling to read.

Every other character is either not fleshed out or an archetype or just evil in a ridiculous comic book way that doesn’t feel real in any sense of the word. Also there are like two other named women, but not a single one is given any time or development. This book doesn’t give a shit about any woman other than Cat.

Finally, I want to talk about the way the book attempts to interact with anti-colonialism. This book would like you to think that it’s anti-imperialist and against the colonization of the Caribbean—I mean the Lyrian. But not only is this book lazy in its exploration of such themes, it’s also propping up Cat (a white blonde person from the colonizing Meridian Empire) and Valerian (another white blonde person from the colonizing Meridian Empire) as the saviors of the non-white island peoples of the Lyrian.
And as if white savior-ism wasn’t enough, Cat also describes Valerian as “A predator. A conqueror.” and as “the Lion of the Meridian. Deadly, powerful, golden, filled with the calm knowledge of his superiority. King of the jungle.”
Like I’m sorry? Excuse me, what? And by the way, these are DIRECT quotes from the books. How are you going to pretend to be anti-imperialist and then use that exact same language to prop up your ugly ass love interest?
And not to mention, there like two named PoC in this book, neither of which have anything to contribute to the plot.

Altogether, I’m not sure why this book exists save to cash in on Aveyard’s flagging popularity after releasing Red Queen ten years ago. It’s an unpleasant experience. It’s poorly written, horribly paced. It’s not interesting. It’s not engaging. The characters are juvenile (they’re all like 30-35 in the “after” timeline btw).

The kindest thing I can say about this book is that it’s a masterclass in what not to do when writing a book.

I truly am not being hyperbolic when I say that this book is the worst I have ever read and finished, somehow dethroning even Yarros’ Fourth Wing series.

There’s a lot more I could say, but I wish to wash my hands of this book and never think about it again, so I shall end this over-long review here. I may return to add some other thoughts if I think of anything, but for now, if you made it through, thank you for reading my review!

I beg you to not inflict this slog of a book upon yourself and to instead just go watch Pirates of the Caribbean. It’s much better and is actually interesting. Or, if you really want a sea-faring adventure book with magic, pirates, dragons, and politics try Robin Hobb’s Liveship Traders! It’s one of my favorite series with much better writing and far more fleshed-out plot and characters (and a bit of romance too).

We love a pirate sea-faring adventure, and Victoria Aveyard delivered with this world! It felt so fully fleshed out (but I am begging for a map... there wasn't one in my ARC copy! 😂) and the spirit and song of the sea felt so alive - which I loved so much.

The story is really interesting - I love the political setup, and I was immediately pulled in with this past/present narrative where I really felt intrigued at filling the holes of what happened "before" vs "after". I also really loved Cat as the protagonist. She felt realistic in the sense that she was just trying to do her best to survive in this harsh world, and while she wasn't the strongest physically or the most technically educated - she did her best to observe, and adapt to her surroundings. She wasn't perfect, but gradually learned from her mistakes.

The dynamic between our main trio of characters was setup perfectly, there was so much tension that I wanted to get to the bottom of. I do have to say, by the end of the book - I felt a bit dissatisfied with the amount of insight we got into how our characters got to this point with each other. Hopefully more of that will come in the second book!

What I didn't fully love in this one was the pacing. We followed a very rigid structure of one chapter in the past, one chapter in the present - and switching back and forth consistently this way. And we had some wonderful parallel moments delivered with this. BUT there were many moments throughout the book where I would have preferred to stay in one timeline for a few more chapters before switching back to the other. I think the quick constant switching almost made the pacing of both timelines drag on, and the majority of the book felt slow because of this. There were also a few adjectives and phrases that were used and repeated too many times for my liking... they started to really jump out at me because of their frequency.

All in all, a fun read. If you're looking for a pirate fantasy with some tension & romance - pick this one up!

If Victoria Aveyard writes something, I’m already in line to read it. No questions asked. And when I heard she was diving headfirst into pirate romantasy with Tempest, my excitement level skyrocketed. Pirates, dangerous seas, complicated love, political chaos, betrayal, survival… yes please, give me all of it.

This story drops us into a world where the ocean is both refuge and battlefield. At the center of it all stands Cat Rose, a woman who has lived two completely different lives. Once she was Lady Catrine Rose of the Meridian Empire, trapped in a suffocating marriage and a life she never truly controlled. Now she is the Widow Rose, the sharp-minded owner of a pirate tavern in the Lyrian Sea, determined never to be powerless again.

I absolutely love heroines like Cat. She isn’t soft, saintly, or designed to be easily liked. She’s calculating, guarded, sometimes messy, and fiercely determined to survive in a brutal world. She’s the kind of character who makes questionable choices, keeps dangerous secrets, and refuses to apologize for the lengths she’ll go to protect herself. Honestly? I respect that energy. Women in morally gray territory will always win my heart.

And then there is Valerian Kane… the infamous pirate captain who slips through the Empire’s grasp again and again like a ghost carried by the tide. The Empire wants him captured at any cost, and unfortunately for Cat, they believe she is the key to finding him. Their ultimatum is brutal: deliver Valerian to them, or face the gallows herself.

But of course things are never that simple—especially when the man they want her to betray is the one who still burns through her memories like wildfire.

The dynamic between Cat and Valerian is complicated in the best possible way. Their connection feels tangled in history, longing, resentment, and unfinished emotions. It’s not a tidy romance; it’s messy, layered, and charged with tension. The kind of relationship where love and betrayal seem to exist in the same breath.

What really impressed me throughout the book was the sheer scale of the world. The islands, the ships, the politics between the Empire and the pirate republic, the constant feeling that war is brewing just beyond the horizon—it all creates this sweeping maritime atmosphere that makes you feel like you’re standing on the deck of a ship with salt in the air.

The story moves between Cat’s past and present, slowly revealing the events that shaped her transformation from an imperial lady into the hardened survivor she is now. I actually enjoyed piecing together those fragments of her life. Watching how the girl she used to be slowly hardened into the woman she became gave the story an emotional depth that kept me invested.

The pacing leans more toward a slow burn than a nonstop action ride, but that worked for me because it allowed the characters and the world to breathe. Instead of rushing through battles and twists, the book focuses on building tension—political tension, romantic tension, and the constant sense that something explosive is waiting just beneath the surface.

And when the story finally hits its stride near the end? That’s when everything clicks into place. The stakes rise, loyalties start shifting, and suddenly you realize the calm waters were only the beginning of a much larger storm.

By the time I reached the final pages, I was fully invested in Cat’s fate, deeply curious about where this story is sailing next, and already impatient for the next installment.

Overall, this is a rich, atmospheric start to what promises to be a sweeping pirate romantasy adventure. It’s full of sharp edges, emotional tension, and a heroine who refuses to sink no matter how violent the storm becomes.

I’m giving this one four adventurous, storm-tossed stars, and I cannot wait to see where the next tide carries these characters.
