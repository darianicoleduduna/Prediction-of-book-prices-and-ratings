----------------------------------------------------------

Stripped of power. Fuelled by vengeance.

She lost everything because of his lie. Now the only way to save her sister is to win a deadly competition that could cost her the only thing she has left: her life.

When Prince Kyor twisted the truth about his mother’s death, Rose paid the price. Her family was stripped of their magic, cast into the slums, and abandoned by the kingdom they once served. Now her parents are gone, and her younger sister’s future hangs in the balance.

Rose’s only hope is the Tournament of the Gifting, a brutal competition held once a century, where the victor earns a blessing from the Goddess of Life. The catch? Every other contender wields the very power she was robbed of.

And Prince Kyor is among them. Commander of the armies. Rider of a bonded dire wolf. Wielder of lightning. He doesn’t just want to win for vanity – he wants the gift for himself. But what could a man who has it all possibly want?

Thrown into a world of magic, monsters, and merciless trials, Rose must fight not only for survival but for the chance to reclaim her future. Along the way, she’ll find unlikely allies, ruthless rivals, and a dangerous connection with her greatest enemy – one that could ruin or remake her.

Web of Vows and Vengeance is a dark, slow-burn enemies-to-lovers romantasy, set in a Nordic-inspired world, featuring deadly trials, found family, gods and goddesses, political intrigue, and an underdog heroine who refuses to stay broken.

This is a story of heartbreak, vengeance, and the kind of power that can never be taken.

The premise is similar to so many trending romantasy books right now: a girl from the slums enters into a set of trials in order to try to give her sister a better life with an enemies to lovers trope.

For the plot being predictable, this was such a fun read. The trials felt high stakes, and there was so much action and drama in between trials. I honestly couldn’t put this book down and stayed up way too late reading.

Rose is such a great FMC. She’s smart, stubborn, and caring. She has so much grit and has such a drive to succeed, but also has so much compassion for those around her.

I am obsessed with Kyor. He’s broody, arrogant, charming, but also so honorable. He’s cutthroat but also has such a soft side for Rose. I do wish the book had his POV because I feel like we would understand him more, and the shift from being cutthroat to having a softer side wouldn’t be as abrupt.

There is so much tension, yearning, and passion between Kyor and Rose. It’s very similar to Xaden and Violet’s relationship in Fourth Wing which I personally loved.

I adored all the side characters and friendships built throughout the trials. Through the trauma of the trials, they really become so close and have each others back, no matter what.

The ending was just so good, and I can’t wait to read the next one!

This book had me completely hooked for the first half.

From the very beginning there was always something happening, the pacing was strong, and I was genuinely invested in the story. I loved Rose as a main character and the position she found herself in. The stakes felt high, the world was interesting, and I kept finding excuses to pick the book back up because I needed to know what happened next.

What really kept me turning the pages was the mystery. There were so many little hints and pieces of foreshadowing sprinkled throughout the story that had me convinced something big was coming. Jonas especially gave me that feeling from the start, there was just something slightly off about him that kept me suspicious. Benny and his group also felt like they were hiding secrets, and the story did a great job of making me believe all these threads were building toward something significant.

Unfortunately, somewhere around the 60% mark, the story seemed to lose momentum.

The plot that had been moving so well suddenly felt like it stalled, and the tone shifted into something much more YA than I was expecting. The enemies-to-lovers romance started strong, but once the relationship developed, I felt like the prince lost a lot of what made his character compelling in the first place. The sharp edges that made him interesting seemed to disappear, and the romance ended up feeling a little immature compared to the tension and chemistry that came before it.

My biggest disappointment was that many of the mysteries and hints I’d been so invested in never really landed for me. The foreshadowing had me expecting bigger payoffs, bigger twists, or revelations that would completely change how I viewed the story. Instead, when the answers finally came, they felt underwhelming compared to the anticipation the book had built.

The ending was the biggest letdown. After such a strong start, I was hoping for something that would leave me reeling, but instead it felt rushed and surprisingly flat. I finished the book feeling more frustrated than satisfied, which is a shame because for the first half I genuinely thought this was heading toward a much higher rating.

Overall, I loved the premise, loved Rose, and absolutely flew through the first 50%, but the second half couldn’t quite maintain the same magic.

Web of Vows and Vengeance reminded me why I love romantasy. Rose is a witty and intelligent underdog fighting through a deadly tournament to save her sister. Prince Kyor is the brooding and lightning wielding royal she has every reason to hate. Then, Aria Ashbrook puts them together and lets the banter do its thing. Prince Kyor, the man that you are.

The Tournament of the Gifting gives Rose a chance to reclaim everything her family lost after Kyor’s childhood accusation destroyed their lives. Rose enters without magic and faces competitors who have every advantage over her. The trials bring monsters, gods, deadly rivals, and danger. The relationships remain the focus of the story. Trial based fantasies are rarely my favorite, but I loved this story because the characters are not buried beneath the intricies of the competition.

Rose and Kyor have the best chemistry. Their hostility slowly shifts into attraction as forced proximity complicates what Rose believes about him. Their banter is sharp, flirty, and ridiculously entertaining. Rose can hold her own against him too. Kyor is a sexy, brooding man and Rose is a witty woman who wants him despite every reason she should not. I was happy.

Llinos deserves her own appreciation because that woman had me laughing out loud. Her solution to Rose’s romantic triangle problems was to think about getting Kyor and Jonas naked so Rose could have both men. I respected the commitment Llinos had for improving Rose's life with questionable advice. Llinos brought so much humor and personality to Rose’s found family that the twist involving her was devastating. I needed ten business days to process what happened.

Ashbrook makes the characters easy to care about. The friendships matter. The attraction feels magnetic. Rose’s determination to save Kay gives the tournament reasoning beyond simply surviving the trials. Kyor’s secrets leave uncertainty surrounding his history with Rose which makes their growing connection complicated rather than convenient.

The final portion slows as the story shifts toward court politics and renewed distrust of Kyor. I wanted Rose and Kyor together more than I wanted another reason for Rose to question him. The cliffhanger certainly accomplished its mission. Book two is now required reading because I need Kyor to start clearing up whatever is happening immediately.

I cannot believe more romantasy readers are not talking about Web of Vows and Vengeance. The writing flows beautifully, the relationships take priority, the characters inspired actual emotional attachment, and the romance is exactly what I wanted. Kyor and Rose have my attention and Aria Ashbrook has me running directly toward the sequel.

Web of Vows and Vengeance by Aria Ashbrook is the book up for review, and I'm beginning to realize how much I love Romantacy. This will be my second 5 star book of 2026. I was both reading and listening to this book to maximize both experiences.

As for the reading part: this book is very well written. I like the flow of the writing. It's easy to get into and it keeps its strength throughout the book.

As for the listening part: it translates to audio very well, and I feel this is part of the strength at which it was written in the first place. This book was narrated by Amanda Leigh Cobb and Anthony Palmini, and I cannot stress how good of a job they both did. The way I wouldn't have been able to get through the spicy scenes if I had to read this book out loud. 😂😭 They both nailed it perfectly.

As for the story itself: it was quite a wild ride! I love books that keep progressing with each chapter. It was simple but informative when it needed to be, and it was dramatic and extra when it needed to be. I loved the characters. Even the side characters added so much drama. Our main female character, Rose, gets shafted by pretty much everyone else's mistakes and jealousy that stems from her parents, but she never lets it hinder her. Her weaknesses come from being too trusting. It was a good theme for her throughout the book. It created a unique dynamic between her and all the other characters, including her love interest.

The spice, though minimal (it's the first book, duh) was REALLY good. It was built up like a true enemies to lovers story should. It was placed in the parts where it actually made sense and also added to the progression of the story.

This book DOES HAVE CONTENT WARNINGS and I would recommend reading them before picking this one up. The death of the inner circle character(s) was a big one in my opinion.

Overall, this was an extremely strong start to a series. I am really looking forward to the next book coming out this summer. If you like dramatic stories with strong female main characters that overcome the odds, I would recommend this one to you.
