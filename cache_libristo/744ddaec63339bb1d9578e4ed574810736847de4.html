In the harrowing conclusion of The Eating Woods trilogy, an age-old prophecy will come to pass, plunging the immortal world into a blighted web of destruction and despair.



*****



Fate has never been entirely kind to Maevyth Bronwick. Though, after having survived a sinister plague, grotesque monsters, and gut-wrenching tragedy, she never imagined things could possibly get worse. But cursed gods, she was wrong.

It seems fate was only sharpening its claws.

Ensnared in the golden gauntlet of the most sadistic general in Aethyria, Maevyth faces the wrath of a jealous tyrant who longs to watch her suffer a slow and painful ruin.

A torment said to be worse than death.

Without the protection of her blood magic, Maevyth is no more than a powerless mortal and must fight the urge to succumb to the cruelty. Not an easy task, given the devastating loss she refuses to accept-and rightly so.

Because Zevander "The Scorpion" Rydainn isn't dead, after all.

He's very much alive and seething with vengeance. Brimming with rage. Merciless in his pursuit to reclaim his beloved mate and bring the ancient godsflame to anyone who stands in his way.

A necessary brutality when a slumbering god awakens and Aethyria's foretold destruction reignites the hunt of those who seek Maevyth's precious blood.

Prophecies will unravel. Walls will crumble. Kingdoms will fall. And through it all, a monolith of truth stands

Their love may conquer all, but death will tear them apart.

WOW!! What a phenomenal conclusion to a truly epic trilogy!!

Full of horror and heartache and the most stunning romance, Vasmora took me on an emotional rollercoaster that surged straight through to the most hard-won and satisfying conclusion I could have imagined for The Eating Woods. I’m never getting over this.

If you’ve read the previous books, you know Eldritch ends with a crescendo of chaos and anguish alongside an insane cliffhanger.. And let me tell you, Vasmora starts exactly where Eldritch left off and does not stop. It’s quite fast-paced, which works so well to continue that momentum and cover a lot of necessary ground. While Vasmora stays true to the gothic style, everything has escalated to terrifying new heights, culminating in an emotive and adrenaline-drenched conclusion.

It’s dark.. it’s stressful .. even devastating at times. I swear each chapter tested my emotional stability and my adrenal system. But omg was the payoff worth every moment!

I’d been shown a new dimension of love—the darker, more terrifying plane where pain was inevitable.


Even with some semblance of what these characters would have to face in this book, I still wasn’t ready. Maevyth’s journey crushed my already wounded heart and fanned the flames of my burning need for vengeance (iykyk). Chapter 18 wrecked me. But I was also so proud of her strength and resilience, and watching her continued character growth throughout this finale was really rewarding.

Speaking of vengeance… wow did Vasmora deliver. I couldn’t think of a better (read: worse) outcome for a certain someone and all I can say is THANK YOU KERI because now I can finally know peace.

“There would be no life after you, moon witch… Stars would lose their brilliance. My world would dull to a colourless hellscape.”


The character work in this series is unparalleled and the way it has shaped the romance is nothing short of incredible. The yearning and devotion, the mutual vulnerability and the instinct to protect each other at all costs... it is so profoundly moving. Maevyth & Zevander’s bond feels SACRED and their “happy ever after” is so indulgent, so well-deserved and satisfying… I could have read 800 more pages just to bask in their forevermore. SWOON.

“He’s crawled from death and shadows, risen up from that abysmal chasm. For me. My angel with dark wings. My merciless weapon of wrath.”


Keri Lake is truly the QUEEN of gothic romance and I will never tire of her insane ability to deliver a story that makes me feel so deeply. She is fluent in the macabre, the chilling and the dreadful… and she knows how to wield all of it to illuminate hope and love against all odds. Of course this translated so beautifully to an epic dark fantasy, but I am still left awestruck when I think about the magnitude of this world, the depth of the characters and the absolute masterpiece delivered.

I would recommend this trilogy to any romance readers that appreciate gothic romance, intricate fantasy worlds and magic systems, grimdark and horror elements, layered characters and slow burn, intense romance, vengeance-dealing, complex villains, and hard-won HEAs.

One last thing … If you’ve read Keri Lake’s other gothics you will know that she sometimes will include little easter eggs and OMG there is one here that had me screaming!! So clever and fun and a little bit mind-blowing.

We had more of a fight with them in the second book than we do in the final book, at least that's what I think. I really don't like it when a book series builds up the antagonist, making them seems like this terribly powerful opponent only for them to be defeated in a single chapter. Why?

I'm sad to be writing this because this was another one of those series that had me in a chokehold with the first two books, and I was so so excited for the finale. It wasn't a bad book, but it just didn't have the same hold on me like the others.

My main issue is that it felt like too much was crammed in without enough development. It's a huge book, but its length just didn't feel necessary. There were several characters and plot elements that were either introduced in this book or only hinted at in the previous ones, and I don't think they received the development they needed. I felt like these elements either deserved a lot more attention (perhaps making this a 4-book series instead of a trilogy) or shouldn't have been introduced at all. There was just too much happening without enough depth.

Secondly, I felt like after about 50%, the writing started to feel rushed, and the character interactions weren't as deep or meaningful anymore. This was particularly disappointing because the emotional depth and character development were some of the things I loved most about the previous books.

Overall, I love Keri Lake's stories and writing, and I think that this is absolutely worth it if you've made it this far, but I wish I had been able to rave about it like I did the other two in this trilogy. It was still worth it for me to read as a whole because Eldritch changed my life. I am still obsessed with Zevander and Maevyth because of that book.
