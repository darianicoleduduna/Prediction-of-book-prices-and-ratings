He can resist any woman in the world...except for the one he shouldn't want.

Famed goalkeeper Noah Wilson doesn’t do relationships or complications. The only thing the grumpy single dad wants is to do his job and spend more time with his daughter.

Unfortunately, his bachelor status has made it impossible to find the right nanny for her. With the World Cup looming and a string of fired nannies in his wake, he has no choice but to hire the last person he wants close to his orbit.

Carina is too stubborn and far too appealing for her own good, but she’s also a natural with his daughter.

And the way his heart skips a beat whenever she’s around? That’s nothing more than a coincidence. He’s sure of it.

***

Carina Vu has a problem. She’s worked multiple jobs and saved up for years to fulfill her childhood dream of visiting Antarctica, but just when she’s close, an unexpected emergency wipes her account clean.

Now, she’s back to square one. The clock is ticking, and she needs another job ASAP. Her best (and only) option is to nanny for Noah, the brooding athlete whom she’s convinced hates her.

But the more time she spends with him and his adorable daughter, the more cracks she sees in his gruff exterior. And the man underneath all that grumpiness? He’s enough to make her rethink everything—including what (and who) it is that she actually wants.

My journey with this series was a bit rocky - after The Striker and The Defender were a “meh” at best, you can see how I was reluctant about this book, but me being me, I wanted to finish this series 😅

Overall, this was much better than the first two books, and I really enjoyed the story.

We follow Noah - Blackcastle FC keeper - who, after a streak of bad luck, is searching for a new nanny for his 11-year-old daughter, Evie. At the same time, he is getting ready to compete in the upcoming World Cup, rethinking his career and generally trying to be a good dad for his daughter and navigating an unexpected appearance from the past.

Carina is on a quest to fulfil her lifelong dream, only for unexpected events to bring her back to square one, while also struggling to find her purpose in life. To restart her climb towards her dream, she agrees to be a nanny for Noah's daughter, only for their feelings to start bubbling to the surface.

Carina🐧- I think she might be my favourite FMC of this series. What I liked about her is how realistic her character felt, with all the challenges that may come her way. She was very likeable from the start, and all her relationships with different characters felt genuine. Her dream to see the penguins was absolutely adorable 🥹 While I loved seeing her relationship progress with Noah, I enjoyed how she was able to stay true to herself. ngl her approach in the little “break-up” was very refreshing - unlike other FMC’s I’ve seen, she made her feelings and expectations clear and managed to stand her ground.

Noah⚽- I would say he's also the best MMC of this series, and somehow I am a sucker for a grumpy single dad story 😅 He was such an amazing and caring dad for Evie (who is, I think, one of the cutest kids I’ve read in a while 🤍) and I also really enjoyed witnessing his progress as a friend to all the guys in the Blackcastle team. He struggles with communication and generally prefers to be closed off to himself, and I loved to see his gradual change to let more people into his life.

ngl, I was a bit sad we didn’t get to see him score that win at the World Cup, but this is also a reality: sometimes things don’t turn out the way they're expected, and that’s okay. His yearning for Carina was a chef's kiss- ugh, this man was so in love with her 🫠 The whole situation with the Andersons was a bit sad and honestly strange coming from someone claiming to be loving grandparents, but it was great to see how it all resolved.

Overall, this was a great culmination to this series, and I am happy that the third time was the charm 💚

After the last two books being so bland, I better not find the penguins to be the best part of this one (iykyk) 🐧

‎ ‎ ‎ ‎ ‎ ‎‎I’ve come to realize that Ana Huang books are a toss-up for me, and this book was one of the ones that fell flat. I didn’t even have that high of expectations and yet I was disappointed. As much as I enjoyed the previous book in this series, I could tell The Keeper wouldn’t be comparable. I was absolutely right. There were decent aspects, like the found family moments and wide range of events to keep the story engaging, but they were overshadowed by details that made the main characters and their dynamic dull. Not that they were extremely dull, but they weren’t outstanding. I honestly don’t think this book provided the closure this series deserved, it threw in one page to outline the other couples’ outcomes after a time skip in the epilogue and that was the end of it. Usually I’m left nostalgic when finishing a series but I was so focused on being done with this book that I felt little to nothing when reading the final page. I thought the premise was great and Ana Huang’s writing was as annoyingly addictive as ever, but The Keeper lacked a meaningful spark for me.

‎ ‎ ‎ ‎ ‎ ‎‎ Considering the fact Carina nannying Noah’s daughter was supposed to be the main storyline of The Keeper I’m shocked at how short it was. I feel like it could’ve been dragged out longer but instead it only lasted until around 50% in to the book? Carina’s nannying was one of the few enjoyable aspects and it actually seemed like Carina and Noah were growing closer through their shared interactions with Evie, Noah’s daughter. There were a decent amount of entertaining events during this storyline, like Carina’s trial run for nannying or the braiding lessons which were adorable. But at one point or another, the plot shifted away from these exciting moments towards duller, lust-filled exchanges.

‎ ‎ ‎ ‎ ‎ ‎‎ It’s a common theme in Ana Huang books for this switch to happen, but I was hoping that maybe this one would be an exception. It was not. I did love the attempt at implementing various settings into The Keeper and I think it allowed for further individual development, however it didn’t contribute much to the relationship of Noah and Carina. They spent a decent amount of time apart and whenever they were together it was often pages full of sexual thoughts. Also, the conflict with the Andersons, Evie’s grandparents, that was introduced early on felt sort of drawn out. I’m glad it didn’t completely overtake the storyline, but it did get tedious reading it at times.

‎ ‎ ‎ ‎ ‎ ‎‎ I was beginning to think Ana Huang was drifting away from basing her relationships on lust but unfortunately Noah and Carina’s dynamic was just that. Don’t get me wrong, they may have developed actual feelings at some point late in the story, but their potential chemistry ended up being a disappointment. I mean, they did have chemistry, but it wasn’t all-consuming like you’d want it to be for a couple expected to last forever. It was basically said they “liked” each other from the start of this book which was extremely annoying because where’s the build-up? Where’s the reasoning for why they liked each other aside from being sexy? Every part of their dynamic wasn’t the best, their banter became repetitive and what was supposed to be tension-filled moments felt forced and inauthentic at times.

‎ ‎ ‎ ‎ ‎ ‎‎ A decent amount of their interactions were Carina calling Noah a grump, leading to annoyance from the latter. It may have seemed amusing at first but after the 50th time it gets unexciting. It also felt like they got together, as in a “trial run” for dating, fairly early in the book which made their relationship even more lackluster. They did have their cute moments, I think if they had created a stronger emotional connection before getting together they would have been a really good couple. However they were also lacking in communication of emotions which was evident in the final chapters. Although Noah promised to do better at communicating near the end of the book, there was never another conflict where he had to prove he could keep this promise which sort of set his and Carina’s relationship up for doom. Unfortunately I just felt disconnected from their relationship as a whole, but I could see others finding them entertaining.

‎ ‎ ‎ ‎ ‎ ‎‎ Going in to The Keeper I already knew I wasn’t going to be the biggest fan of Noah, Ana Huang doesn’t have the best track record when it comes to writing grumpy MMC’s. They usually end up having no personality and come off more rude than they do simply stand-offish. While I didn’t love Noah, I do think he was different than what I was expecting. He actually had emotional depth and a personality, shocking, though said personality wasn’t the utmost entertaining. Carina is the main character that really saved this book for me, she was so sweet with unique interests and most of the time she had me entertained. I honestly think Gods of the Game is one of the few series of Ana Huang’s where the main characters were at least somewhat different. Each one had decent individuality and The Keeper continued this streak.

‎ ‎ ‎ ‎ ‎ ‎‎ As much as I hate the overly lustful focus Ana Huang brings to her books, I can’t seem to stop reading her work. She has a way of writing that keeps the chapters flowing together and although this book in particular had me more bored than usual, she kept that flowy style. The emotional depth was good, I’m trying to be less harsh when considering how well characters are delved into since everyone has different problems that need different amounts of attention. If that makes sense? Anyways, The Keeper unfortunately just wasn’t for me. It was quite bland and plot points either felt too short or too drawn out. The writing became repetitive quite early on and there were a few dialogue options that had me cringing. However, as much as I enjoy her writing (to an extent) I was expecting most of these aspects from an Ana Huang romance so I can’t say I’m all that surprised. I did love the side characters and their hang-out moments, so yay to that! Though I still would’ve traded The Keeper for an Adil book any day

౨ৎ Carina Vu.

‎ ‎ ‎ ‎ ‎ ‎‎This girl was so kind and mature, I adored her character! Her relationship with Noah’s daughter was super cute and I loved the realistic challenges she faced. Rather than having a one-of-a-kind traumatic past like many of Ana Huang’s other characters, she had fears that many people face every day which made her so much more relatable. Carina had great development as she discovered and took action on getting what she truly wanted in life. At times her depth could feel glossed over, but for the most part she was delved into well. Overall I had such a lovely time reading this book. It made me laugh, had lots of cute moments and I became really attached to the characters. **It wasn't a perfect book but it was the romance I wanted and I'm so glad I chose to read it.
