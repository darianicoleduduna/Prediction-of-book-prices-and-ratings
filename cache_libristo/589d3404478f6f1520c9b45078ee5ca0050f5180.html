5 Stars ⭐️

I loved Good Spirits and knew that I would also love this one. This was perfect; B.K. Borison gave us all the fall vibes with this one, and I was here for it!

Grim Tidings is the second book in the Ghosted series. This is an enemies-to-lovers paranormal romance that was cute and fun, had me giggling, and had all the fall vibes. There were also moments that had me sobbing, as it is a story about death, grief, a journey of healing, and the little things in life that have meaning.

Darcy Moore is looking for something different, as she's tired of death and is determined to save her next assignment. There's just one thing: she's a grim reaper. When she arrives in the town of her assignment, she ends up in the bar of Gabriel, a guardian angel who doesn't want her in his town. As they spend more time together, he realizes she's not as she seems.


Darcy Moore is stubborn in the best ways; she is strong, but she is also soft and wears her heart on her sleeve. I fell in love with her character the moment that she was introduced. She feels lost and lonely on many levels, and I just wanted to wrap her in the biggest hug. Gabriel Aetos is grumpy and I love it. He is broken in many ways and it shows at times. It's not a fault. In fact, it made me fall for him even harder. Deep down, he is kind of soft and sweet. I mean, the man yearns for and calls Darcy sweetheart. What's not to love?

The romance between Darcy and Gabriel was chef's kiss. They are opposites in most things, but the best was in the department of hauntings. This aspect was different and a fun time. I loved the push and pull between them. The slow falling for one another when it seemed everything was against them, and the soft moments in between the tension and banter. Let me just move them up to one of my favorite couples.

This may just be my favorite book by B.K. Borison, though I have a couple that I need to go read. I loved everything about it the cute moments, the ones that had me laughing, the moments of sobbing, and everything in between. A couple of side characters that I absolutely adored were Frank and Tater Tot. I'll be awaiting the next book, which I think will be Gideon's, and I can't wait.

╭➤ the premise ────────────────────────────╮
A guardian angel and a grim reaper who are drawn together despite being on opposite sides of death, duty, and everything they believe they understand about one another. What begins as a clash between two lonely, exhausted souls slowly becomes something far more dangerous: a forbidden love neither of them can ignore
────────────────────────────╯

Some books make you fall in love with the characters, and then there are books that reach into your chest, take hold of your heart, and leave you feeling like you’ve lost a little piece of yourself somewhere between their pages🥺

I was NOT prepared for how deeply this one would affect me. This author has such an extraordinary gift for creating characters who don’t feel fictional. They feel like entire, breathing worlds—full of grief, longing, fear, & tenderness🥹

and here’s the thing about BK Borison: she doesn’t write characters you can neatly divide into good and bad, right and wrong. She writes them with SO MUCH emotional depth that even when they hurt each other, even when they make mistakes, you can still understand the fear or grief behind their choices😭😭🥺

and i think that’s what makes Darcy’s love for Gabriel so incredibly special.🥺 because she doesn’t just love him for the good, easy, beautiful parts of himself. she sees him in all his complexity🥺 the pieces he has spent centuries trying to hide. the loneliness. the exhaustion. the guilt. the parts of himself he believes make him unworthy. And she chooses him anyway😭🥹

"Darcy wants me exactly as I am. She wants the rough and broken pieces as much as the shiny, perfect ones. In fact, I think she likes those pieces better."


EXCUSE ME WHILE I LIE ON THE FLOOR😭
Because isn’t that what we all want? To be known completely and still loved? To have someone see the parts of us we consider ugly, inconvenient, or impossible and not ask us to become easier before they stay?🥺🥺

There is something so profoundly moving about being loved without having to perform perfection. About being seen in your entirety and discovering that the parts you thought made you difficult to love are not the parts that make you unworthy of it🥺💜

one of my favorite things about this book was the way it explored death and the meaning we give to life. We spend so much of our time fearing endings, grieving what we lose, and wishing we could have just a little more time. And yet, there is something so beautiful about the reminder that death doesn’t erase the meaning of a life, just as grief doesn’t erase the beauty of having loved someone

Grief and beauty are not opposites🫶🏼

Sometimes grief is the evidence of how deeply we’ve loved. It’s what remains when someone or something has become so woven into our lives that its absence changes the shape of our world. We hurt because it mattered. We mourn because we were given something worth missing. And even when that loss leaves an ache we don’t know how to carry, it doesn’t make the love any less beautiful

there's something almost divine in the understanding that even in the presence of death, there can be tenderness, connection, and meaning. That we aren’t promised endless time, but we are given the opportunity to make the time we have matter. To love people while they’re here, to cherish the ordinary moments before they become memories, and to recognize that something doesn’t have to last forever to be precious

And that ending???
I bawled like an actual baby

FIVE STARS.
For the laughter, the heartbreak, the tenderness, and the way this book reminded me that even in a world full of endings, there is still so much beauty worth finding. For a love that doesn’t erase grief but makes room for it. And for the reminder that sometimes the most extraordinary thing in the world is simply being seen, understood, and chosen.

If there’s one thing B.K. Borison keeps proving to me, it’s the fact she could probably write a grocery list, tax report, or instruction manual for assembling a chair and I’d still kick my feet, giggle, emotionally spiral, and call it a five-star experience. And now after finishing Grim Tidings, I can officially say this series has become my newest guilty pleasure obsession. Honestly? Inject this paranormal chaos directly into my veins.

The first book Of Ghosted series already owned my heart, but this second one confidently marched in, spread its giant angel wings, wrapped me in grim reaper darkness, poured me a Sidecar, and whispered: “Oh sweetheart, we’re about to make you FEEL THINGS.”

And wow… the chemistry. THE CHEMISTRY!

That first bar scene alone? Someone call emergency services because the tension was practically setting furniture on fire. Darcy and Gabriel walked into the room carrying enough emotional baggage, unresolved resentment, suspicious side-eyeing, and accidental yearning to fuel an entire trilogy. A burned-out grim reaper who is exhausted from being viewed as the villain meets a grumpy guardian angel who trusts literally everyone except her? Oh B.K. Borison knew exactly what she was doing here. This enemies-to-lovers dynamic was outrageously good. Sharp banter, emotional vulnerability, simmering attraction, moments that made me laugh one second and stare at the ceiling emotionally devastated the next… perfection.

And can we discuss Darcy for a moment? Because I adored her with my entire soul. Underneath all her sarcasm, exhaustion, and emotional walls is someone desperately longing to experience life instead of constantly escorting people out of it. That hit hard. Really hard. She isn’t just “Death.” She’s lonely. She’s grieving pieces of herself she never got to keep. She wants joy, connection, warmth, messy human experiences. Watching her slowly open up felt beautiful and heartbreaking at the same time.

Meanwhile Gabriel completely snuck up on me. At first he’s suspicious, overprotective, emotionally constipated in the most delicious way possible, but beneath all that grumpiness is someone carrying the weight of everyone else’s safety on his shoulders. And the way he slowly begins seeing Darcy instead of fearing what she represents? EXCUSE ME WHILE I SCREAM INTO A PILLOW.

Also, the humor in this book worked so incredibly well. The corporate paranormal department setup remains one of my favorite things about this series because it’s absurd in the best possible way. Death but make it office politics? Guardian angels acting like overworked customer service managers? Grim reapers having existential burnout? I ate every single second of it up.

But what truly surprised me was how emotionally layered the story became underneath all the paranormal whimsy and romantic tension. This isn’t just a cute spooky romance. It’s a story about loneliness, grief, purpose, identity, and the terrifying vulnerability of allowing yourself to be loved when you already believe you’re unlovable. The emotional intensity sneaks up on you when you least expect it. One minute you’re grinning at the banter, the next your chest hurts.

And honestly, the cozy autumn atmosphere? Elite. This book feels like cold October nights, dim bar lights, oversized sweaters, haunted little streets, cinnamon smoke in the air, and emotional support cocktails.

B.K. Borison somehow created a paranormal romance that feels magical, funny, sexy, whimsical, and deeply human all at once. The second installment absolutely lives up to the first and maybe even emotionally destroyed me more. Which apparently is now my favorite reading experience.

Now excuse me while I aggressively wait for the next book like an emotionally unstable ghost haunting Avon’s inbox.

Darcy might have been dead for over 100 years, but she's no Grim Reaper. She's Death who wants to understand life. She's fascinated with life, love and ordinary things like seeing a sunset or jumping into a lake or baking a pie and getting a dog! She wants to experience a life full lived and can you really blame her for wanting something more than just one thats simply existential? So no, she's no scary Reaper, she doesn't take, take, take. She is someone who wants to give, give, give. So Darcy is in existential crisis more or less. She's lonely, she's tired of always taking souls away, she wants new things for herself that actually matter to her. She wants things that feel personal and bring pleasures and make her happy. And when she is assigned a new soul to reap, Darcy decides to do the opposite--she's going to save it. Except, there is a little problem of a Guardian Angel who stands in her way.

Gabriel.
A man who falls heels over head for Darcy but takes a little while to come to this conclusion. Gabriel, narrated by Stephen Dexter, probably had the hottest voice ever.
