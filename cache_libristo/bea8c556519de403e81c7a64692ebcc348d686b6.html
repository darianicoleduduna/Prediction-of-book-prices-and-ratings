She’s trapped in a magical prison. To break out, she’ll make a deal with the most dangerous prisoner of them all.

Sentenced for a crime she didn’t commit, Samara is bullied by guards and tormented by the screams of her fellow prisoners. She’s resigned herself to enduring her fifteen-year term… until she learns that the freedom she's been promised is the cruelest lie of all.

Now she's faced with a choice: resign herself to dying in the cold walls of Castle Greymere, or do the unthinkable and escape. But no one leaves Greymere alive. So to break out of the prison? She’ll have to use one of the undead.

Raphael is the worst kind of a vampire with a thirst for blood, the mortal enemy of witches, the same breed that killed her mother. And most importantly, he’s unspeakably powerful, his supernatural strength tamed only by the enchanted cuffs forced on him.

He’s her only chance at a life outside the prison walls.

The deal is Samara unlocks his shackles, and he'll help her escape. But although Raphael agreed to help her leave Greymere, he never promised to let her go…

⭐️ 3.5 ⭐️ Ever since I watched True Blood on HBO and read Charlaine Harris's The Sookie Stackhouse novels, I have been a vampire romance lover through and through. I also don't want to forget the classic Interview with the Vampire or the addictive pairing of a witch and a handsome vampire historian from Discovery of Witches...I mean these were all mature and amazing gothic vampire romances. So when I read the premise of A Bargain So Bloody, I set my expectations really high. I mean, a woman confined to a magical prison and makes a deal with the handsome devil who is a dangerous vampire in hopes of escaping that hellhole but in turns he never plans to let her go and has his own plans--ALL of this sounded so alluring. I saw witches, vampires, political intrigue, the word 'bargain' and my brain spiraled out of control, happily, thinking this will be my next 5 star hit. I truly do see how readers are so hyped up about this and I understand that hype...but only if they were to read or watch some of the shows/books I mentioned above...you can see the different between memorable and just mediocre.

Please don't read ahead as there will be spoilers

To briefly sum it up: We have Samara, a young woman who has been imprisoned in Greymere Castle since childhood for a crime she didn't commit. She thinks she is going to finish up her sentence and be free to leave but learns that no prisoner is actually ever released. She realizes that she needs to escape and here she meets Raphael, a mysterious, blood thirsty vampire who she is tasked to attend do. She hates vampires because of what they have done to her mother but knowing that he is her only option at an escape, she forms a reluctant alliance with him despite their mutual distrust. They strike a bargain and he gets her out. As they travel through the magical world, Raphael often has to risk himself to protect her and this causes Samara to slowly change her perceptions about vampires; she realizes that everything she was ever taught or believed in about vampire has been false. Together they travel to his kingdom, where she learns that he is the actual vampire king. Thinking that she will be released, Raphael had other plans for her. As Samara meets the people of his court, she is introduced to ancient prophecies and is tasked with looking for a forbidden grimoire. The grimoire is very important to the story and when Samara uncovers its truth, the novel ends on a very dramatic cliffhanger.

I'll start with the positives:

This book is marketed as dark romance and enemies to lovers. There is nothing dark about it and it's not a true enemies to lovers story. If you are looking for a romance and chemistry, you are not going to find it in this novel. This is kind of a book that will have you scratching your head thinking if you missed anything but in reality what ever you're looking for was simply not there. The book boyfriend material here feels like endgame but the spice meter? Not there. To be honest..I kind of appreciated that? There are so many books out there whose focus heavily leans into spice. But A Bargain So Bloody had none of that. I thought that one of the books strengths was that their slow-burn romance leaned in more on emotional healing and trust rather than lust and sexual need. Raphael is very protective of Samara despite his morally gray edge and 'vampire allure'. The most that they do in this book is passionately kiss. I really appreciated the minimal display of affection because I am someone who enjoys a really slow burn that is based on emotional connectivity and vulnerability vs. physical need. I also can't say they were enemies. They were distrustful of each other and Samara shows a lot of hesitancy/reluctance throughout the entire novel. I thought the novel tried to explore their mutual trust through actions rather than promises.

Where the novel lost me
I did not like Samaras character and I found her to be indecisive all through out. The novel predominantly focuses on Samara overcoming years of trauma, hence she struggles with trusting others and accepting her own growing feelings towards Raphael. She came off as impulsive and her internal conflict seemed to go on unresolved for a very long time and that became a very long, excruciatingly tedious reading process. Raphael has shown her again and again how he can be trusted; his actions spoke louder than his words but Samara's emotional unavailability and constant negative thoughts made her character insufferable. As Raphael became more likable, Samara become more unlikable. I really thought she had zero emotional growth. Her views of vampires were so jaded and prejudiced, I honestly couldn't understand why Raphael was so enamored with Sam. I also think his explained attraction towards her wasn't well panned out in the story. There is not enough background on Sam or much to her character besides her internal conflict.

I also think this book would do better with YA readers rather than adult. I just think this story had a lot of filler and was quite underdeveloped in all elements of the vampires, fantasy world-building and even political/court intrigue. It did end on a steep cliffhanger so there might be more payoff in the next book but overall this is an OK start to a trilogy. I'm just a hard critic and a picky fantasy reader.

I kid you not, I don’t remember what happened in the first 54% of this story. There were words. There was a little adventure. There was blood.

I don’t even know how to review this book. It’s not particularly romantic, but the romance exists, I think? It’s not plot heavy, but things definitely happen. There was some world building but I never met her because she came late to the party and I had already checked out. I feel like I’m muddling this, but bottomline is I liked and disliked this book at the same time.

Samara is a prisoner who has resigned herself to serving out her 15-year sentence at Greymere. The first time she meets Raphael, she’s sent to the cell where he’s shackled to clean and treat his wounds. As the prison’s most dangerous inmate, Raphael makes her an offer.

❝ Free me from my chains, and I’ll let you escape with me...❞

I liked how the story started out. Though the world building stayed simple for a while and I could more or less predict how the story would go, I was still excited about it. However, once Samara and Raphael arrive in Vampire Kingdom (why is the name so uninspiring?), things start to unravel differently. The world finally begins to take shape and gain layers and there's a bit more complexity to the plot. Funnily, it was also at this point I lost all visibility on what kind of story this was or where it was headed. I feel like the tone of the novel changed several times throughout the story, which added to my uncertainty about the direction of the plot. We start with imprisonment, then move on to a bit of adventure before stopping off at intrigue and court politics. I don’t mind not knowing where a story is going, sometimes you end up in the best places, but I got bored following the tonal shifts and was left unimpressed with where this one went.

The final blow was the romance. When I say crumbs, I mean KRAMBS 🤌🏼 It should be a crime to call starvation slow burn. I’m surprised we even got there this year. What am I even saying? We didn’t get anywhere and left to this story, before we do.




Every interaction felt like it was building toward something, only to become nothing. I guess their love isn’t supposed to be emphatic but mercy 😭 The chemistry was right there, simmering on the page, yet I was surviving on scraps and begging the author to feed me 😭



I don’t think this fits the dark romance or romantasy genre, it reads more like fantasy with a romantic subplot.

Both Samara and Raphael are fine as characters at first. However, in the second half of the book, I started wishing Raphael had more presence and personality. He says the right things, does the right things, yet I think there's little depth to his character. As for Samara, I found her a little irritating at times, especially with how repetitive her thoughts could be. She comes across as whiny, sanctimonious, contradictory, and worst of all, illogical. Now I know there is a better world out there with heroines I do not have to gaslight myself into liking, there's no going back for me.

I feel so terrible saying that because I know she spent so many years surviving in that awful prison, but given that experience & her time with Raphael since she escaped, I feel like her naivety & very childlike mannerisms should have at least abated some. I can understand why she is the way she is, theoretically, but it started to take away from my enjoyment. If you asked me to describe her character in one word —-mouse. She needed the mmc’s assistance or rescuing several times & it was just too much of the mouse in distress trope back to back.

Samara was also highly biased on more than one occasion. By 75% in we know vague details about what happened to her mom & that it involved vampires, but it was not explored enough for me to accept why she was still so back-&-forth with her acceptance of Raphael’s kindness & lack of ill intention towards her. With all his kind gestures & protection? With all the awful things she has seen & experienced more frequently at the hands of humans? I also wanted to learn more about Raphael… I only knew that he was handsome & a fair-ish vampire king.

The romantic buildup between the two mcs was going good & gradual until they made it to the vampire kingdom where it completely paused for a chunk of time. They were separated often & their banter disappeared. Because it was all in Samara’s pov, I got to witness why she’s growing feelings for Raphael, but I didn’t get to understand why or what it was about Samara that intrigued Raphael enough to make her his Chosen. Again, beyond being meek & self-aware of her ignorance, there wasn’t much to her character. At first I enjoyed getting to see what it was like for Samara to experience simple moments she was denied in life, but then those experiences stopped & her character growth stayed stagnant.

And I have to also admit that the first scene where sex between them is openly discussed, & all because Samara drank, gave me such bad second hand embarrassment. I know it was supposed to come off endearing & touching, but I hated every second of it.

For most books I can tell early on if I’ll like it, but this book caught me off guard because I was seriously loving the first half, only to find it obnoxious in the second half.

I’d been holding off starting this because I wanted to be closer to book two. My heart simply cannot take another unfinished series this year.

But… I folded. I went in excited, expectations high.

I love vampire stories. They’re my weakness and this started strong. A solid first 60% or so. The premise hooked me, the FMC had a great introduction, and a white haired, red eyed MMC? Mhmm. Hello 👀🔥

The magic system was great - voids, our magicless humans, witches, vampires, hints of other creatures and even fae realms were mentioned. And card magic? Loved that.

Trope wise, it was ticking all my boxes. One horse, one room at the inn, ballrooms and big dresses…

HOWEVER I wanted more from the writing.
Our FMC Samara, has spent most of her life imprisoned, yet when she finally experiences new places, towns, libraries, temples - it reads like a checklist.

“I went here. I asked this.”

Where are the details? The atmosphere? The sensory overload? She’s been locked away her whole life, I wanted to feel that freedom with her. The sights, the smells, the sheer overwhelm of it all. Instead, it felt flat. Give me MORE.

The romance had such a strong build up. The tension, the slow burn, the chemistry - and then it just faded for a large portion of the book, which was massively disappointing.

And Samara - Firstly, you survive life in what sounds like a horrific prison (virginity in tact? I mean great but come on, unlikely), where you must keep your whits about you to survive. Only to the second you escape, tell everyone your real name and where you're headed?! WHAT?!

I struggled with her towards the end even more. I’m not a fan of the miscommunication trope at the best of times, but here it felt especially frustrating. Just tell him. We as readers don’t even fully understand her past yet, so it’s hard to empathise with her decisions in the final 20–30%. Especially when the MMC is consistently supportive and patient.

I'll happily be your chosen one Raphael.

I’m honestly PROPERLY gutted, because this had so much potential and such a strong start.

Will I read book two?
…I genuinely don’t know. 💔
